# Evidence acquisition report  -  4 November 2026

Five original studies were audited. The most useful new lead is LifeLines: its published model reports a continuous BMI association conditional on fasting glucose, age, sex and four additional factors. This narrows the evidence gap but does **not** identify the requested Chinese target coefficient. Hoorn provides an important contrasting association under a different, richer adjustment set. Both findings are retained.

This stage generated no cohort, fitted no model, read no patient data and inspected no prior target results. The complete extraction and limitations are in [EVIDENCE_CANDIDATES.json](EVIDENCE_CANDIDATES.json); the actual searches, failures and exclusion exposure are in [SEARCH_LOG.json](SEARCH_LOG.json).

1. **LifeLines, Jansen et al., 2013.** Table 3, Model III gives BMI **0.05 (CI 0.02-0.08) mmol/mol per kg/m^2**. The final model contains age, gender, BMI, fasting plasma glucose, MCH, MCHC, current smoking and alcohol. The BMI estimate converts algebraically to **0.00457 (CI 0.00183-0.00732) HbA1c percentage points per kg/m^2**, using the paper's factor 10.93. Participants were Western European, nondiabetic and anemia-excluded; 89 alcohol responses were missing. Model III sample size is not printed; 2,832 is an expected complete-case inference, not a reported model count. Explicit sex coding and the confidence level of the table's "CI" were not found, so neither is invented. This is a source-context constraint; omitting its extra predictors requires unavailable joint information. [Publisher original](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joim.12010). Exact inspected methods and Tables 1-3 are saved in [web-rendered original](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joim.12010) (publisher original; saved rendering remains local). This source is **web-inspected, not gateway-admitted**.

2. **Hoorn, Wisgerhof et al., 2020.** Table 2 reports BMI **quartile contrasts**, not a linear slope. Q4 versus Q1 was -0.135 percentage points (95% CI -0.228 to -0.043) in the first cohort and -0.022 (-0.044 to 0.001) in the second. Models also condition on waist, hip, fasting and 2-hour glucose, age, sex, smoking and dietary factors; creatinine and fiber are available only in the first model. Authors explicitly caution that related body-size/glucose predictors make the BMI estimates unstable. This cannot be reconciled with LifeLines by selecting a preferred sign or pooling incompatible coefficients. Table 1 also supplies source means, SDs and Spearman correlations. [Original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7272077/), local XML table IDs `pone.0233769.t001` and `pone.0233769.t002`.

3. **Japan Diabetes Prevention Program, Sakane et al., 2017.** In 245 IGT-selected adults aged 30-60, Table 3 reports sex-specific BMI "Beta" values of 0.27 and 0.22 in models containing fasting glucose plus different additional predictors. The paper does not explicitly define the coefficient scale; these are **not** treated as verified standardized or unstandardized slopes. Table 2 Pearson correlations and Table 1 sex-specific medians/IQRs are usable source summaries. Age is absent from the final models. [Original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5330780/), local XML `T1-T3`.

4. **Pani et al., 2008.** The main results report cross-sectional age slopes of 0.010 HbA1c percentage points/year in nondiabetic NHANES 2001-2004, adjusted for BMI, fasting glucose and sex, and 0.012 in Framingham with additional 2-hour glucose adjustment. These constrain the age relationship in those source populations; they do not provide a BMI slope. CI/SE are absent from the inspected main text. The supplement's legacy DOC format is unsupported by the gateway and was not inspected. [Original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2551641/), RESULTS paragraph beginning "There was no difference in BMI...".

5. **Hainan, Li et al., 2021.** The 1,579-person Chinese study includes diabetes and medication users and supplies sex-specific age, glucose and HbA1c means/SDs plus HbA1c-glucose Spearman correlations of 0.44 in men and 0.50 in women. These can constrain descriptive/rank relationships. Reported regression direction, ordering and log-scale wording are ambiguous, so its slopes are withheld. The main text does not supply the requested conditional BMI coefficient. Its DOCX supplement was listed but unsupported; data reuse with the authors' 2020 publication is recorded. [Original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7960723/), Results and Methods.

The four PMC originals are gateway-approved version 1 XML/text artifacts, with hashes and original URLs in the ledger/provenance log. Approval means the gateway's retrieval screen accepted those bytes; it does not establish transportability or validate an extraction. The independent reviewer receives the same originals.

No source gives the full five-variable joint distribution for Chinese community adults aged 35-74 with unrestricted diabetes/treatment status. The target's serum glucose and unverified individual fasting remain particularly important: fasting plasma studies do not determine a nonfasting contamination rate or measurement-error law. Population, treatment, assay, calendar and adjustment differences remain explicit assumptions for any later synthesis.

The acquisition stopped at **5 studies, 10 combined queries (including 3 gateway autoload lookups), 4 gateway downloads and 5 acquisition web opens**. The independent reviewer reports 2 further opens, giving 7 shared opens within the 20-open allowance. One gateway metadata record was excluded. A separate CHNS search snippet was incidentally visible, immediately excluded and logged; its numerical evidence was not used. No alternative route was used to recover a rejected cohort source.

# Candidate A · Hb and ferritin

## Evidence and measurements

Zhu et al. (S1, 2010, DOI 10.1024/0300-9831/a000014) studied 1,671 nonpregnant, nonlactating, parous women using IUDs 2–4 years postpartum in Xianghe County, China, in 2004. Hb was venous HemoCue measured in g/L. The generator divides the study cutoffs by ten to give g/dL. S1 measured plasma ferritin by immunoturbidimetry in µg/L. I carry that concentration scale into the target raw ferritin column. The packet supplies no cross-assay conversion or individual inflammation measurements. The intended US population includes nonpregnant women aged 20–44 irrespective of parity, postpartum status, lactation, or contraceptive method.

S1 Table I gives a ferritin geometric mean of 17.4 µg/L. The band-specific geometric means also imply approximately this value on aggregation. The reported location for Hb is 129.9 g/L, or 12.99 g/dL. The Table I heading and prose/Table II leave the type of Hb mean ambiguous. I used that figure only as broad context for Hb scale. I derived no standard deviation from an interval. Table summaries are rounded finite-sample estimates, treated here as fixed centers.

Pita-Rodríguez et al. (S2, PMC10049065) found an OR of 3.023 (95% CI 1.816–5.033) for low Hb with low ferritin in Cuban mothers aged 18–40 after survey-specific inflammation correction. Population, biomarker treatment and estimand differ. S2 motivates considering heterogeneous associations. Its OR and interval set no numerical coefficient in this generator.

## Source construction

1. Draw an Hb band with its S1 sample probability and a uniform within-band rank. Linearly interpolate Hb at rank knots [0, .05, .25, .50, .75, .95, 1]. The assumed Hb values at these knots are [5.5, 7.5, 8.6, 9.2, 9.65, 9.94, 10], [10, 10.2, 10.85, 11.38, 11.72, 11.95, 12], and [12, 12.2, 12.7, 13.25, 13.9, 14.85, 17] g/dL. The cuts are reported. The interior knots and outer limits are assumptions. Each draw is continuous within its band.
2. Assume depleted ferritin becomes less likely as Hb rises. The probability is linear in rank within each band and continuous across Hb cuts. Start the lowest Hb rank at probability .993, an assumption. With each band's left endpoint fixed, the right endpoint equals twice its reported low-ferritin fraction minus the left endpoint. This preserves the observed fractions in expectation.
3. For ferritin ≥15, draw X from a gamma distribution with shape 1.6 and mean .85. Set log(ferritin/15) to .85 × sqrt(X)/E[sqrt(X)]. The square-root transformation gives the raw concentration finite positive integer moments. Its implied conditional geometric mean of about 35.1 µg/L and the shape are assumptions. For ferritin <15, model log(15/ferritin) on (0, log(15/.1)) using a scaled beta distribution with concentration 5. The lower concentration limit .1 µg/L and within-component dispersion are assumptions, not assay specifications.
4. Let the mean log deficit for depleted women decrease linearly in Hb rank within each band, starting at 2.8 log units at the lowest Hb rank. Join the end of each band's curve to the start of the next. For band low probability p and geometric mean G, the required mean deficit among the depleted is [log(15/G) + (1−p) × .85]/p. Integrating the linear deficit curve against the rank-specific depletion probability determines the right endpoint of each segment. This matches each band's mean log ferritin in expectation despite very different distributions below and above 15.

## Target transport

I represent the broader US population with two fixed departures from S1. First, I weight source records with depleted ferritin by 2/3 relative to other records. This allows for the source's selection of parous postpartum IUD users, under an explicit assumption of greater iron depletion in that source group; IUD type and individual menstrual loss are unknown. Weighting shifts both ferritin and Hb margins through their source association. The code samples the corresponding normalized distribution directly. Second, in 20% of target draws I sample Hb independently from the reweighted target Hb distribution. This represents variation in the causes of Hb differences across women and weakens the joint association while preserving the two reweighted marginal distributions. The fractions 2/3 and 20% are round structural assumptions, without quantitative US or S2 calibration. Other than these changes I transport the component ferritin distributions and Hb rank curves. None of the target statistics were used to set these choices.

## Interpretation and limits

Both columns on a single draw are used for every analysis with the stated strict thresholds. Source OR and joint prevalence are anchored directly; target OR and joint prevalence depend on the two transport assumptions. The continuous rank correlation additionally depends on the unspecified Hb tails, ferritin dispersion and within-band dependence. Subgroup, individual-prediction, etiologic and inflammation-adjusted analyses lack a basis in the packet. The target draw does not implement survey strata, sampling weights or design-based uncertainty.

Randomness within a fixed model represents person-to-person variation and Monte Carlo error. Seeds do not propagate uncertainty about source sampling, assays, the transport weights or the missing conditional distribution. The packet cannot identify those uncertainties or a target-population performance interval. The initial generator's isolated source replay and the one subsequent structural revision are documented in decision.md. Finite model moments do not establish that population ferritin dispersion has been estimated.

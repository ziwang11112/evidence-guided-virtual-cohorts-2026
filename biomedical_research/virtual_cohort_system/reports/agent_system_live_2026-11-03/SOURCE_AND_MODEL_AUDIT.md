**Supported quantities versus assumed laws.** A uses the source BMI mean of 24.1, the unadjusted BMI difference 26.0−24.8=1.2, the unadjusted HbA1c difference 7.7−5.8=1.9, and source SD scales as contextual anchors. Using them as conditional parameters in a target latent mixture is explicitly assumed. Its age-uniform design, female probability 0.55, latent prevalence/logit age slope, BMI sex/age shifts, HbA1c slopes 0.008/year and 0.012 per kg/m², female shift −0.03, lognormal BMI law, gamma HbA1c law, and 3.5% floor are not source-estimated relationships. The female median is not subtracted from the male mean as an estimated effect. The 5.6% conditional center is declared a chosen arithmetic mean, not a conversion of the source median.

B assumes its shifted-lognormal BMI law, age/sex composition, 0.10 reference elevated-component prevalence, logit slopes, conditional copula correlations 0.50/0.65, and all covariate pathways. Its component moments are chosen analogues of source subgroup moments. The code converts mean/SD to lognormal parameters correctly; away from reference covariates, its multipliers scale conditional SD as well as mean. Neither the source marginal Spearman 0.529 nor Pearson 0.619 is silently substituted for a conditional Gaussian correlation. The 15% meal-affected fraction and lognormal meal increment with mean 1.5 and SD 0.8 mmol/L have **no extracted source support**. They are declared scenario assumptions; unknown fasting adherence does not estimate that fraction or effect. The 0% and 30% alternatives are mentioned but not evaluated in the audited artifacts.

Material unverified transports remain in both models: source subgroup moments become conditional residual moments; reported clinical diabetes definitions become unobserved mixture membership; older rural and younger urban summaries inform an age-35–74 population with unspecified geography; complete-measurement selection is assumed rather than modeled; treatment/history and nonglycaemic determinants are marginalized without identification. B additionally transports local fasting-serum scales to central serum with unknown fasting adherence. These are disclosed limitations, not discovered extraction defects. Their disclosure does not make the resulting adjusted coefficients empirically supported. Source SDs are not parameter uncertainty, and repeated draws cannot quantify the missing source, structural, or transport uncertainty.

**Independent finite-moment check, without sampling.** In A, age, sex and latent state take finitely many values. Conditional BMI is lognormal with positive arithmetic mean at least **23.0125** and fixed SD 3.5, so every positive polynomial BMI moment exists. The HbA1c conditional mean has infimum **5.12%** as BMI tends to zero; its excess above the 3.5% floor is at least **1.62**. Writing that excess as x and the component SD as s, the submitted gamma shape and scale are x²/s² and s²/x. For integer k≥1 its conditional kth raw moment is the product over j=0,…,k−1 of **(x+j s²/x)**. Because x is bounded below and grows linearly in BMI, this is bounded by a constant times (1+BMI)^k. Integrating over BMI and the finite states proves all positive integer HbA1c moments exist. The shift preserves them. This also supplies the cross-moment existence needed for the descriptive OLS population projection.

In final B, BMI is 16 plus a lognormal factor with bounded age/sex location shifts, hence has all positive polynomial moments. Its serum and HbA1c BMI multipliers are respectively **1+0.012(BMI−24.5)>0.898** and **1+0.002(BMI−24.5)>0.983**. Age/sex multipliers are bounded. Conditional on covariates and component, the Gaussian residual factors are lognormal with one of two finite parameter sets. Thus each conditional kth outcome moment is bounded by Ck(1+BMI)^k, even though component probability depends on BMI. Averaging over BMI preserves finiteness; the additive Bernoulli-lognormal meal term also has every positive moment. Rounding by four decimal places cannot destroy moment finiteness.

The preserved B draft instead contains positive **exp(k·BMI)** links. A lognormal variable has no finite moment-generating function at any positive argument. Because the component conditional-mean factors remain bounded below by a positive constant across the finite age/sex/component states, these links give infinite outcome means and higher moments. The documented repair is mathematically substantive and correct. It preserves the local mean derivative at the centering BMI while changing the global law; it is not evidence-based calibration. All these proofs concern real-valued model laws, not a guarantee against every floating-point overflow or proof of plausible clinical tails. Both final models still allow unbounded outcome tails; finite moments do not establish biological support limits.

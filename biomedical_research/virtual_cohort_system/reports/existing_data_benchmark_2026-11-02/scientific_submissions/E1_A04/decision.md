# Single initial whole-cohort model

The candidate is one conditional mixture distribution for 3,000 rows. Integer age follows a restricted discrete normal kernel; female membership depends weakly on age; BMI is conditionally lognormal with modest demographic dependence. A latent high-glycemia state depends on age, BMI and female membership. Serum glucose and HbA1c then follow a bivariate lognormal distribution with state-dependent medians, individual variation and residual correlation. All settings are in `model_spec.json`, and every seed uses the same law.

The supplied Chinese BMI and glycemic summaries inform scale choices. They are not target margins, pooled empirical data or quantities the generator is required to reproduce. The ordinary-state reference medians differ from unconditional model medians. The latent state allows a joint upper tail under the task's absence of diabetes and treatment exclusions; its assumed probability does not estimate diabetes prevalence. Demographic effects, predictor dependence, mixture proportions, tail shapes and conditional residual dependence remain assumptions.

The relationship evidence informs positive BMI/glycemia dependence and a moderate shared glycemic component. Bao's observed-scale Pearson correlation and MIND's rank correlation describe different populations and specimens; neither is numerically installed, pooled or transformed into the model's log-scale residual correlations. The two Jansen coefficients are separate age/gender-adjusted projections. They are not combined into an equation, and their interval levels, estimator SEs and original regression sample sizes remain unresolved. The model does not simulate source contexts or claim source fit.

Source selection is materially different: Bao excludes known diabetes, MIND includes known diabetes in an older rural sample, and Jansen excludes diabetes and anemia in selected western-European adults. MIND's prior-year BMI and reported imputation are additional limitations. The chosen age and female distributions are not inferred target composition. Female=1 preserves the documented category without guessing original source coding. The Jansen binary nuisance-label invariance qualification does not recover its sex coefficient or identify target composition.

The glucose output is an assumed law for the released central-serum field. MIND serum is closer specimen context but has different laboratory handling and population; plasma FPG from the other studies remains distinct. No plasma-to-serum correction is used. HbA1c is generated directly in percent, so the NGSP IFCC relation requires no computation here and does not imply assay equivalence. Source SDs describe individual variation, quartiles describe quantiles and count fractions describe selected samples. None supplies estimator precision for these assumptions.

BMI and the two glycemic measurements are positive by exponentiation, with unbounded lognormal tails and no output-based clipping or recalibration. Age is sampled only on 35-74, as required. There is no missingness, sampling weight, community/family structure, treatment model or pregnancy model. Conditional independence of residuals from upstream draws and independence across rows are explicit simplifications, not conclusions from missing evidence. The model can produce discordant glycemic measurements, but their real frequency is unknown.

This is a method-benchmark candidate for an existing analytical population. It is unsupported for clinical prediction, diagnosis, causal inference, target prevalence estimation, sampling-precision claims or fresh independent scientific confirmation. Random seeds quantify draw variability only. Distributional validity, empirical source diagnostics, runtime/output behavior and target utility remain untested. No generator, pilot, calibration sweep, source-context table or numerical diagnostic has been executed. Construction stops after this single submission because the assigned procedure has one scientific stage; the controller owns static validation, freezing and prescribed isolated executions.

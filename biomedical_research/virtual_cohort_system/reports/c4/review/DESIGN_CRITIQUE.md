# C4 independent design critique

Prepared 31 October 2026. This is a design review, not an experiment or a source of numerical scenarios. Only the closed C3 result report was consulted for background. No C3 candidate, output table, target data, generator, or numerical scenario was executed or imported. The recommendations below require a separate frozen protocol and independent artificial source/truth authoring before execution.

## Recommendation

Proceed with two explicitly artificial tasks and a shared candidate pool within each task. This can discriminate acceptance behavior and expose false assurance within a controlled mechanism. It cannot establish a new method, clinical validity, or general superiority of analysis-aware generation. A pure gate-pass demonstration would be insufficient.

Task A may use an identified Gaussian moment system and audit a conditional regression coefficient that is not directly reported in the source packet. Task B should supply the same admitted pairwise information under two compatible hidden laws whose omitted dependence changes that coefficient. The source-equivalent laws are an identification witness, not independent replications.

The Task A endpoint is source-unreported but derivable under the stipulated model. That distinction must appear in the result headline or its immediate qualification. It tests whether a selection rule preserves an analytical consequence of available information. It does not show recovery of independent, unknowable biological information. Task B is essential because it shows where the same acceptance logic lacks enough information.

## Shared pool and separation

Have a fresh author receive the artificial admitted packet and intended scientific uses, author an initial generator, then author at most three revisions using only prespecified calibration feedback. Do not deliberately weaken the initial generator, require a harmful revision, or tune a scenario after seeing candidates. A revision may duplicate its predecessor or decline to change it. Preserve these events as outcomes, without substituting more favorable candidates.

All three policies operate on the same frozen pool: retain initial, minimize aggregate source loss, and minimize that same loss subject to intended-analysis gates. A revision sequence is candidate production; the selector comparison begins only after the sequence is frozen. Make clear that this is selection from one Agent-produced pool, not a comparison of independently resourced Agents or revision strategies.

Use three disjoint, precommitted seed sets:

1. Calibration: produce feedback for the initial generator and each revision. No election or final rows are opened. If a proposal is changed after feedback, its earlier version remains logged and consumes its proposal/run allowance.
2. Election: evaluate every frozen pool member on identical paired draws. Apply all policies once. Do not revise code, thresholds, source weights, or candidate membership afterward.
3. Final test: replay every pool member on untouched paired draws. Score the selected outputs and the full pool against the frozen endpoint references. Do not select again from these results.

Common random numbers are appropriate within a phase if the generators support a common latent construction. They improve precision of paired differences, but do not turn seed replicates into new studies. Do not share seed streams between phases. Preserve the complete seed list and pairing rule in the commitment.

With four candidates per task and three draws in each phase, the planned budget is 2 tasks × 4 candidates × 9 draws = 72 isolated generator runs. The final phase contains 2 × 4 × 3 = 24 evaluated synthetic tables. Reuse each table across selector scoring; do not generate a second table for a policy that selects the same candidate. Count every attempted generator launch, including failures and retries, against the absolute limit of 120. The remaining allowance is a ceiling, not a reason to add scientific trials.

## Acceptance rule to freeze

Use the same aggregate source-loss formula, weighting, improvement requirement, baseline, and deterministic tie-breaking in both active selectors. Let the minimum-loss policy choose the smallest election loss from the whole pool. Let the use-preserving policy choose the smallest election loss from the eligible pool. Eligibility must be determined only from admitted analyses and prespecified structural validity checks.

A simple defensible engineering rule requires every declared intended-analysis gate to pass on every election draw. This is an operational reproducibility criterion, not a confidence interval or clinical equivalence statement. If a different aggregation rule is used, fix it explicitly before outcomes. Avoid an informal judgment that a boundary miss is close enough.

Freeze the actual numerical margins, source-error scales, loss-reduction threshold if any, strict versus inclusive inequalities, missing/undefined-estimate handling, invalid-table handling, and tie order. Tie-breaking should favor retaining the initial version; fixed candidate order may break any remaining tie. Do not derive thresholds from C3 discrepancies or from the observed candidate distribution. Engineering margins should be justified in the artificial task's own units and declared as conventions.

When no eligible revision satisfies the common improvement requirement, retain the initial generator. Record separately whether that retained initial generator satisfies the gates. Retention of a failing initial generator is an inability to approve a change, not a certificate that the initial generator supports the intended use. A genuinely changed candidate that passes the gates must remain distinguishable from a duplicate or no-op acceptance.

The gate must not inspect the withheld endpoint's numerical truth, error, pass/fail status, or an equivalent algebraic target. An admitted statistic can be related to the withheld endpoint; that is the mechanism under study. However, explicitly gating the same conditional coefficient under another name would make the primary result tautological.

## Final evaluation and honest interpretation

Freeze the exact source-unreported analytical operator, coefficient convention, standardization, error scale, and failure margin before candidate authoring. Under the Gaussian example, specify the regression adjustment set and which coefficient is scored; do not choose the better-looking coefficient afterward. The custodian should calculate the reference from the artificial law independently of candidate analysis code.

The primary selector comparison is paired final-draw error on this ungated endpoint. Report its signed estimates, absolute errors, predefined failure indicators, and differences from both minimum-loss selection and retaining the initial generator. Show each task and draw; a pooled score alone can conceal improvement in one task and serious degradation in the other. Gate pass rate and source loss are secondary process outcomes.

Evaluate the full frozen pool on the same final draws. This reveals whether the selected candidate missed a useful revision, whether all proposals were poor, and whether apparent protection is merely the advantage of never changing the initial generator. A retrospectively best candidate is only an oracle diagnostic and may not be promoted.

The following distinctions must remain explicit:

- Gate pass after gate-based election is expected by construction. Replaying the gate on unused seeds measures reproducibility of that property.
- Lower unseen-endpoint error after such election is a genuine observed analytical consequence, conditional on these artificial laws, this candidate pool, and the declared endpoint.
- A source-unreported but analytically identified endpoint adds no new source information. Describe Task A accordingly.
- An unreported endpoint that varies across source-equivalent laws is not identified. Passing the admitted gates cannot resolve it.

For Task B, the same evidence, candidates, and selection decisions must be evaluated against both precommitted compatible laws. Prefer analytic reference values so the second law does not require extra synthetic reference tables. The two endpoint truths should differ enough, under the independently committed utility margin, to make the identification limitation meaningful. That condition must be checked by the custodian before candidate production, never by adjusting the laws to the eventual output.

If a selected table passes the gates and fails the ungated endpoint in either compatible law, report that literal false-assurance case. Do not average the two laws into an invented population, choose the friendlier law, or count them as independent confirmations. If both hidden values happen to be acceptable for the selected candidate, report that the particular margin did not expose failure; do not infer identification.

## What would count as support, failure, or no discrimination

A modest positive conclusion needs a real selection difference, a genuinely revised accepted generator, and improved withheld-endpoint error relative to minimum loss on the prespecified comparisons. Improvement only relative to a deliberately poor initial generator is insufficient. A claim of useful acceptance also requires showing its opportunity cost against retaining the initial generator; do not claim a general advantage if every apparent gain is obtained by always retaining it.

With only two artificial tasks and three test draws, report observed effect sizes and paired consistency rather than inferential claims about broad task distributions. Any new failure beyond a prespecified endpoint tolerance remains visible even if an average improves. A result may support a limited Task A mechanism while Task B demonstrates unavoidable failure under unreported dependence.

If all policies choose the same candidate, the comparison is nondiscriminating. If the gated policy always retains the initial generator, describe rejection behavior and its utility cost; do not declare acceptance-policy superiority. If no proposal can satisfy the gates, report insufficient candidates or incompatible constraints. These are valid outcomes and do not authorize a second favorable pool.

## Freeze and stop rule

Before scientific generation, commit task packets, all analytical operators, true-law references under the custodian's boundary, generator-author instructions, calibration feedback schema, selector code, margins, seeds, sample sizes, candidate limit, run accounting, and reporting rules. Record which roles could read each object; do not call a reference hidden if the candidate author could read it. Gate thresholds and source margins must be committed independently of final candidate behavior.

Run the bounded phases once. Do not extend the candidate sequence or add seeds because the selectors agree, the proposed mechanism fails, an endpoint is near its margin, or a result is disappointing. Complete the prespecified final audit even when selection is nondiscriminating, so false assurance and missed opportunities remain observable.

Only a documented technical defect may justify a retry using the same frozen scientific inputs. Count both attempts. A defect that changes scientific code, endpoint meaning, evidence, or selection logic after election invalidates that run as a fresh confirmation; preserve it as development and stop this pilot rather than silently refreezing. Stop at the first disclosure that crosses the calibration/election/test boundary or before exceeding either the 24-final-table or 120-launch ceiling.

The deliverable should be a compact artificial mechanism result with all decisions and failure cases, followed by an explicit statement of what remains unidentified. It should not be presented as a new patient case or as validation of reusable virtual cohorts.

## Root-protocol adjudication: 54-run design supersedes the preliminary schedule

The root's `PROTOCOL_DRAFT.md` was reviewed on 31 October 2026. Its reduced schedule supersedes the preliminary 72-run recommendation above. Only the initial candidate in each task receives three numerical source-feedback draws. The author then proposes up to three revisions without additional numerical calibration. All four labels are frozen before election. The resulting schedule is six initial-feedback runs, 24 election runs, and 24 final runs: 54 in total, with exactly 24 final tables. This preserves separation of proposal feedback, policy election, and final utility evaluation while reducing the available calibration feedback.

This schedule is acceptable for the intended artificial mechanism pilot. Multiple revisions must be described as proposals following one initial feedback packet, not as an iterated numerical-feedback improvement sequence. Duplicates and justified retention remain valid. No scientific reruns or additional seed draws are authorized within the root protocol.

The root's protected-use rule is also acceptable: a revision must pass every admitted-correlation gate on all three election draws and strictly improve mean source loss over the initial candidate; among eligible revisions, choose minimum loss. Retain the initial version otherwise, with its actual qualification flag. Minimum-loss selection uses the same score and fixed tie order, favoring the initial version. This introduces only the intended source-use eligibility distinction between the active selectors.

Two clarifications are required before the protocol is frozen:

1. Write the source-loss equation explicitly as the arithmetic mean of `((estimate - reference) / scale)^2` across all admitted statistics. The draft phrase “squared discrepancies divided by their declared scales” could instead be read as dividing the squared discrepancy by an unsquared scale. The source scales themselves remain the root's predeclared engineering conventions.
2. State that one failed or nonfinite election draw makes the entire candidate ineligible and gives its election loss positive infinity. Never average only successful draws or silently shrink the fixed denominator. Define the retained initial candidate's unqualified status when it is invalid, and preserve all failures.

The source-unreported, moment-identified Task A projection remains a valid analytical audit with the qualification above. When a fresh author can implement the exact supplied Gaussian moments, its initial generator may already be population-correct, and later selection may mainly reflect finite-draw fluctuation. That is an acceptable possible null or nondiscriminating result; it must not be prevented by weakening the initial model or forcing revisions.

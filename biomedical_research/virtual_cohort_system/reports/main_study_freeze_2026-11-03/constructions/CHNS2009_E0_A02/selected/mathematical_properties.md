# Probability law, support and moments

Let A be discrete uniform on integers 35,...,74 and S be Bernoulli(p), where p=2049/3547. A and S are independent of each other and of independent standard normals F, epsilon_B, epsilon_G and epsilon_H. For j in BMI, glucose, HbA1c, let l_j=(0.30,0.60,0.70), Z_j=l_j F+sqrt(1-l_j^2) epsilon_j, v_j=log(1+s_j^2/m_j^2), mu_j=log(m_j)-v_j/2, and X_j=exp(mu_j+sqrt(v_j) Z_j). All m_j, s_j and l_j are in model_spec.json and are read by generator.py. The selected (m,s) pairs are (25,3.6), (5.2,1.4), and (6.1,1.0). The factor loadings and all dependence, demographic and tail choices are explicit assumptions.

A is task-bounded with E[A]=(35+74)/2 and Var(A)=(40^2-1)/12. E[S]=E[S^2]=p and Var(S)=p(1-p). BMI, glucose and HbA1c have support (0,infinity), satisfying task positivity without clipping, truncation, filtering or rejection. BMI is positive in the mathematical law; all labels retain their native units. The lack of an upper bound for glucose, BMI and HbA1c is an author-selected family limitation, not a known physiological support. The model can assign tiny but nonzero probabilities to implausible extremes and does not claim those tails are established.

Each Z_j is exactly standard normal because l_j^2+(1-l_j^2)=1. For any finite real k, E[X_j^k]=exp(k mu_j+k^2 v_j/2), so the first and second moments, and every finite-order positive moment, exist. In particular E[X_j]=m_j, E[X_j^2]=m_j^2+s_j^2 and Var(X_j)=s_j^2. Exponentiation is applied to an affine Gaussian variable, not a quadratic Gaussian term. The construction includes no infinite-variance mixture. Constants are finite and fixed, and every residual loading is strictly positive.

The log correlation r_ij=l_i*l_j gives Cov(X_i,X_j)=m_i*m_j*(exp(r_ij*sqrt(v_i*v_j))-1). Thus all cross moments needed by raw OLS exist; bounded demographics provide finite cross moments with continuous variables as well. The Gaussian covariance is D[ll' + diag(1-l_j^2)]D, with D=diag(sqrt(v_j)). Positive residual variances make it positive definite. The continuous law consequently has positive density on the positive orthant. No nontrivial affine combination of the three continuous variables is constant. Nondegenerate independent A and S extend this to positive definite population predictor covariance for all three requested OLS uses.

Under this independence assumption the population E1 BMI coefficient is Cov(BMI,HbA1c)/Var(BMI), E2 glucose coefficient is Cov(glucose,HbA1c)/Var(glucose), and E3 age coefficient is exactly zero. These are algebraic model consequences, not supplied empirical coefficients or estimates of target truth. The retained analytic/moments.py script computes only these closed-form expressions, marginal moments and residual-variance checks; it imports no generator, samples no rows and uses no numerical integration. Its output is analytic/moments.json. No source scenario or source-population fit is claimed.

At n=3000 the discrete draws could in principle produce a degenerate demographic column; the law does not guarantee finite-sample OLS rank for every conceivable seed. Given enough independent demographic categories, the positive continuous density makes accidental continuous-column rank deficiency probability zero in exact arithmetic. Static validation cannot guarantee floating-point finiteness for every unbounded normal draw, output rank, or execution success. Those remain the controller's execution checks and failures must remain in the record.

The independent epsilons and shared F describe individual synthetic variability under the fixed scenario. Published SDs are not external-estimate standard errors. No sampling precision, transport variance or structural model probability is supplied or inferred from the 3000 synthetic rows. These sources of uncertainty remain separate and unquantified; repeated seeds quantify neither external evidence precision nor clinical validity.

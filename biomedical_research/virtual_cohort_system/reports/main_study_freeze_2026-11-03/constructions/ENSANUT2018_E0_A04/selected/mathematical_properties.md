# Actual law, support and moment proof

Let a randomly selected design row have cell (S,K), uniform over the eight combinations of female S in {0,1} and age band K in {[25,39],[40,54],[55,69],[70,79]}. Given K=[l,u], A is discrete uniform on the u-l+1 integers. The table has exactly 375 rows in each cell; it is a stratified fixed-allocation sample, not 3000 independent draws of the cell. Random permutation changes order only. The output schema contains no latent or extra eligibility variables.

Independently of A,S and of each other, let B ~ Gamma(k_B,theta_B), H ~ Gamma(k_H,theta_H), and U ~ Beta(alpha,beta), with G=L+(R-L)U. The gamma convention is shape and scale. For each gamma marginal of selected mean m>0 and SD s>0, k=(m/s)^2>0 and theta=s^2/m>0. For glucose L=0, R=126, w=R-L, p=(m-L)/w, c=p(1-p)/(s/w)^2-1, alpha=pc, beta=(1-p)c. The retained analytic script verifies 0<p<1 and c>0 from the actual specification. These are exactly the transforms used by generator.py, not an unattached matrix of moments.

BMI uses m=26.5,s=4.1 and HbA1c uses m=5.6,s=0.3. Thus B,H have support (0,infinity). Their densities are x^(k-1) exp(-x/theta)/(Gamma(k) theta^k). For every nonnegative integer r, direct gamma integration yields E[X^r]=theta^r Gamma(k+r)/Gamma(k), finite because k+r>0 and theta>0. In particular E[X]=k theta=m and E[X^2]=k(k+1)theta^2=m^2+s^2. This proves finite first and second moments and, more strongly, every positive integer moment. Unbounded upper support is disclosed as a modeling choice, not a known physiological support. The generator does not exponentiate a quadratic or transform a heavy-tailed variable.

For glucose m=91.8,s=9.0, the beta law has alpha,beta>0 and is integrable at both endpoints. Its support is strictly 0<G<126, satisfying the task without clipping or rejection of an unselected population. For r>=0, E[U^r]=B(alpha+r,beta)/B(alpha,beta). Therefore E[U]=alpha/(alpha+beta)=p and Var(U)=p(1-p)/(c+1)=(s/w)^2, giving E[G]=m and E[G^2]=m^2+s^2. All moments are bounded by the corresponding powers of 126. The task bound is an eligibility restriction, not evidence of an underlying pre-selection distribution.

A and S are bounded. For a given band E[A|K]=(l+u)/2 and Var(A|K)=((u-l+1)^2-1)/12. Equal-band averaging gives E[A] and E[A^2] exactly; E[S]=E[S^2]=1/2 and Var(S)=1/4. The common age law for both sexes implies Cov(A,S)=0 for a random design row. The finite-cohort quota dependence does not alter these random-row moment identities. For a realized table the age moments and biomarkers fluctuate because the generator does not force empirical means or SDs.

Product-law independence entails each cross-moment E[XY]=E[X]E[Y]. More generally finite second moments also imply finite cross-moments by Cauchy-Schwarz. All required regression inputs are affine transforms of these variables, so their first and second moments exist. The age and sex columns have nonzero variance and are not linearly dependent under the design. BMI and glucose retain independent positive continuous variance. Hence the centered population predictor covariance matrices for Q1, Q2 and Q3 are positive definite. Since H is independent of every predictor, Cov(X,H)=0, and the unique population least-squares slopes are zero. This is a derived implication of the chosen law, not an input calibration target. The intercept is E[H]=5.6 and the population residual RMS is 0.3. The Q2-minus-Q3 contrast is zero by the same derivation; it is not causal mediation. Sample projections are not forced to these values. Full column rank for the finite sample holds with probability one under the continuous laws and fixed demographic support.

Numerical support is checked after sampling. Nonfinite or nonpositive gamma/beta outputs, or a floating-point glucose value at the strict upper endpoint, cause explicit failure. There is no silent resampling, endpoint clipping or output calibration. The ideal-law proof is separate from implementation success; a few finite controller outputs could not prove population moments. This numerical failure policy introduces no claimed scientific upper cap for BMI or HbA1c.

The retained analytic_calculation.py uses only specification constants and closed-form algebra, including a finite sum of the four age bands. It neither imports generator.py nor samples rows. analytic_results.json contains its results. No numerical quadrature or Monte Carlo is used. All source comparisons from target tables remain unavailable, and no individual real measurement is recovered.

# Probability-law properties and limitations

The actual generator's equations are given in `analytic/law_derivation.md`. Target age is a bounded integer random variable (35..74), female is Bernoulli, and the three uniform factors are bounded. BMI is an affine combination of these. Thus BMI has all moments. Its exact minimum is positive, as evaluated without sampling in `analytic/support_bounds.json`. Age and sex satisfy task support by construction; no clipping is applied.

Glucose and HbA1c are sums of bounded affine factors and a nonnegative Bernoulli-lognormal component Q. For every finite real k, E(T^k)=mT^k exp((k^2-k)sT^2/2), so E(T), E(T^2), E(Q) and E(Q^2) exist. All cross moments required for OLS and Pearson exist by the same identity and independence, or Cauchy-Schwarz. There is no unbounded quadratic term inside an exponential, mixture with infinite moments, censoring, truncation or row rejection. The finite first/second law moments follow analytically; finite output from a few seeds is not their justification.

The bounded parts of glucose and HbA1c have strictly positive lower bounds and Q contributes nonnegatively. Thus both are strictly positive almost surely. Their upper supports are unbounded in Bao and target. Jansen has pD=0 and bounded FPG strictly below 7. Source supports are separate modelling assumptions, not established clinical/population limits. The Jansen scenario is already conditioned on source eligibility, so its moment calculation is not an unselected-parent calculation relabelled as selected.

Independent nonzero age and sex variances, positive sB and sG, and |r|<1 ensure positive-definite predictor covariance. The independent positive sH ensures positive population response residual variance. Thus the population OLS projections exist and are unique. A random finite sample could theoretically omit a sex group or otherwise have a deficient design; runtime checks, not the population claim, determine sample usability. No row is silently removed to fix a sample problem.

The lognormal generator uses floating-point arithmetic. The mathematical law is finite-valued almost surely and has finite moments; this does not promise that a mathematically possible extreme draw can never overflow machine arithmetic. No cap or redraw changes that law. The moderate declared log SD is retained as a scientific assumption; any actual nonfinite output would be an execution failure, not hidden cleanup.

Residual factors represent individual variability conditional on fixed settings. Source point-estimate uncertainty is not converted to residual variability. Structural choices (factor family, covariance, latent class, age/sex effects) and source-to-target transport are not probabilistically integrated. Seeds only change synthetic samples and do not create clinical evidence. No individual measurement is recovered, no causal effect is claimed, and synthetic p-values are not clinical findings.

Jansen constraints deliberately fix its two separate source projections after the stated covariance assumption. Target full conditional age and all other analysis coefficients are model implications, with formulas in the derivation. Bao's Pearson is a descriptive law check; MIND rank correlation is uncomputed. The supported use is inspection of a complete assumed joint table for the visible analyses. Accuracy in the released target analytical population and survey-cluster inference are unassessed.

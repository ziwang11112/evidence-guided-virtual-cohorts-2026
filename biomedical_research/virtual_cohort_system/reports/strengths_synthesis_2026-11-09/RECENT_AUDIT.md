# Recent results audit: strengths with their exact scope

9 November 2026. This audit covers 23 recent units, including setup and source-only checkpoints. It reads existing reports and selected aggregate outputs only. No patient or generated row tables, target-input files, new generation, tests, web retrieval, clinical refits or frozen-artifact edits were used. The companion [RECENT_AUDIT.json](RECENT_AUDIT.json) contains one record per unit with the requested input, scale, comparison, numeric support, counterresults, inference scope, strength grade and paper role. Grades are editorial evidence-strength judgments, not biomedical acceptance scores.

**The strongest paper focus is useful, analysis-conditioned whole-cohort construction from scientific evidence.** Recent results support a concrete ability to construct complete tables with several useful associations, choose and implement different joint models, and execute evidence-informed inspection. They do not require a claim that every endpoint, every added component or every revision wins. The final dataset and its intended analyses should lead; retrieval, audits and interfaces explain how it was produced and what supports its use.

## 1. The strongest recent data result is repeated analytical utility across authored constructions

In [core workflow replication](../core_workflow_replication_2026-11-04/RESULTS.md), four fresh scientific authors produced 12 final 3,000-row tables for two requests. All 36 primary seed estimates had the reference direction across six analysis definitions. This is meaningful repetition across four scientific constructions, not 36 independent discoveries. All 366 estimates/diagnostics were independently recomputed without a mismatch. The corrected evaluation uses the original frozen tables.

The quantitative result should be displayed as complete author profiles. For CHNS, age/sex-adjusted BMI–HbA1c is 0.03992 and 0.05338 versus 0.05495, while glucose–HbA1c is 0.37754 and 0.49241 versus 0.40209. Neither author is best on every effect. In ENSANUT, BMI effects before versus after glucose adjustment are 0.09165 -> 0.07509 and 0.07140 -> 0.04733; the reference is 0.08364 -> 0.05664. Every draw preserves that attenuation pattern. This supports a joint associational behavior, not causal mediation.

[Cross-task evidence use](../evidence_use_cross_task_2026-11-05/RESULTS.md) extends the observed capability: six authors, two requests, 18 final tables and 54/54 primary point-direction agreements. Authors used bounded conditional laws, shifted-gamma innovations, moment equations and Gaussian-copula/lognormal constructions. All 513 endpoint/diagnostic calculations agreed with an independent implementation. Different structures are observed Agent modeling choices; no new probability-family claim is needed.

The counterevidence belongs in the same display. Both core CHNS age coefficients are too small; both ENSANUT age coefficients are too large relative to their references. Core ENSANUT residual RMS is about 0.295/0.324 versus 0.488. Cross-task lipid residual correlations are 0.108–0.141 versus 0.512, and HDL SDs about 14.4–14.6 versus 20.18. TC's reference slope interval crosses zero, so matching its positive point estimate is weaker evidence than reproducing a secure positive population direction. H03's closer adjusted BMI coefficient accompanies substantially narrower variation.

The [real-real context](../real_real_calibration_2026-11-04/RESULTS.md) makes the CHNS positive claim more substantive than sign matching. For the frozen evidence-to-generation model, the reduced-model BMI discrepancy has real-real median 0.007239 versus generated-real symmetric medians 0.004383–0.012727; age is 0.001705 versus 0.001055–0.002387. These are similar numerical scales. The full glucose coefficient remains farther away: 0.079642–0.109154 versus real-real 0.023839. The 100 disjoint-community resplits provide descriptive within-sample context; they are neither 100 independent cohorts nor equivalence thresholds. This reuses three tables, so count it as stronger evaluation of one case.

[Iron breadth](../iron_breadth_2026-11-05/RESULTS.md) adds another useful analysis contrast on the same rows. A's joint-low prevalence is 6.66–6.88% versus 7.63%; B's continuous Spearman correlation is 0.391–0.430 versus 0.398. Both laws retain a positive threshold association, but their ORs 4.835–6.565 all fall below the reference's lower interval bound 9.28 (point 17.79). Do not assemble A's prevalence and B's rank result into a fictitious universally successful dataset. The complete A and B profiles demonstrate different useful properties and limits.

## 2. Numerical evidence is demonstrably used to construct scientific laws

The [controlled joint-moment diagnostic](../review_revision_2026-11-08/controlled_joint_moments/RESULTS.md) is a strong positive mechanism check. Fifteen exact supplied summaries let ordinary Agents derive joint coefficients (0.25, 0.65, -0.40) and residual variance 0.6775, even though those outputs were not directly supplied. All four programs encode the same source-compatible Gaussian law. This directly contradicts a generic claim that the Agent merely repeats verbal associations or ignores numerical evidence.

The ordinary and additional-instruction arms have identical mean errors: 0.012439, 0.010241 and 0.013370 for the three standardized coefficients; residual-SD log error is 0.006129. Twelve deliveries contain only six distinct tables and one probability law because paired authors used the same RNG layout. Thus this is positive construction competence with a null component result. It is artificial reconstruction, not biological discovery or transport validation.

The latest [source-conditioned replay](../source_conditioned_replay_2026-11-09/RESULTS.md) provides a stronger operational source link than merely tracing a parameter to a paper. One fresh author chose a shared source/target law with HDL and non-HDL components, four source strata, bounded predictors and explicit nuisance assumptions. Twelve 8,000-row source tables supported 24/24 numerical projections of eight published coefficients; every direction matched. Maximum point differences were 0.0179768 for TC and 0.00811677 for HDL, in mmol/L per BMI SD. Independent source estimators agreed to 5.89e-15.

The same frozen generator then produced three 3,000-row target-scenario tables with all nine requested analyses and 69 descriptive calculations, without HDL>TC violations or post-generation filtering. That is concrete complete-cohort capability. However, all 24 strict source-semantic checks remained unavailable because weighting and BMI standardization were unresolved. Assumed numerical replay is the correct label. There were no reference-outcome reads, so its residual correlation 0.375712 must not be advertised as an improvement over the old 0.5121 target comparison. Both target slopes have only partial evidence support and the residual correlation remains unassessed. Varying the assumed component coupling changes conditional correlation roughly from -0.46 to +0.87 while leaving slopes fixed.

The [source-increment revision] (local audit artifact) shows that new evidence truly changes construction, rather than only generating documentation. Seven parameters changed while program bytes remained unchanged. The glucose-adjusted BMI estimate moved from 0.07509 to 0.04993 against reference 0.05664; absolute discrepancy decreased from 0.01845 to 0.00670. But reduced BMI moved from 0.09165 to 0.06075 against 0.08364, and residual RMS narrowed from 0.29502 to 0.19673 against 0.48848. All 93 endpoints independently agreed. Present this as an informative evidence-to-model mechanism with trade-offs, not a default quality improvement.

## 3. Bounded scientific autonomy and feedback response are observed

The [actual adaptive loop](../adaptive_case_loop_2026-11-09/RESULTS.md) completed 28 dispatched actions, real literature acquisition, two frozen scientific models and six outputs. The fresh author chose the queries, interpretations, parameters, model and source-dependent sensitivity, inspected generated results, then retained the initial model before reference evaluation. The sensitivity changed the TC coefficient from 10.6997 to 14.7893 and residual correlation from 0.1383 to 0.1986. One reference discrepancy improved and another worsened. Initial-to-selected-final change is zero. This is actual bounded decision-making, not evidence that autonomous revision improves quality.

The [broader evidence loop](../adaptive_evidence_loop_2026-11-09/RESULTS.md) used two papers and an official manufacturer page, completed 22 actions and 78 descriptive calculations, and exposed 66 assumption-labelled comparisons alongside 12 weighting mismatches. The older claim that all unavailable checks reflected missing scientific knowledge was too broad: the [local audit](../retrieval_validation_analysis_2026-11-09/LOCAL_CHECK_AUDIT.md) found 28 of 34 old claim records have ordinary descriptive analogues, including six geometric means lacking an operator. This is a concrete implemented improvement in usable checking, not 28 newly validated source constraints.

A distinct [coherence repair](../analysis_use_rubric_v2_2026-11-09/RESULTS.md) reduced HDL>TC violations from 16/9,000 to 0/9,000 with an Agent-selected projection. It changed only those pairs, and all 156 endpoint values were independently reproduced. Yet it creates 16 exact TC=HDL boundaries; source discrepancies decrease in 18 comparisons, increase in 16 and remain unchanged in 32. The strong claim is a linked detection–decision–implementation capability with transparent side effects. It is not clinical accuracy or novelty of projection.

Autonomy should be stated at the tested boundary: the host dispatches authors and provides request/interface/budgets; authors select scientific content and actions; reviewers check sources and implementation; the generator runs in an isolated worker. The author process's broader file tools have procedural restrictions, not OS isolation. No recent unit establishes a standalone autonomous model-launch service or direct token-by-token row-generation quality.

## Runtime and verification: useful support, separate from scientific quality

Recorded adapter/execution time is 10.842 seconds for 16 core attempts, 35.83 seconds for 24 cross-task attempts, 3.797 seconds for three source-increment attempts, and 24.64 seconds for 18 rubric-pilot slots. These establish short numerical sampling durations in the measured local execution layer. They omit Agent reasoning, acquisition, review, orchestration/reporting and often evaluation; tokens, monetary cost and researcher time are not fully measured. Do not convert them to end-to-end speedups or patient-collection savings.

Independent arithmetic checks make the reported analytical findings more credible: core 366/366, cross-task 513/513, rubric pilot 513/513, source-increment 93/93, coherence 156/156, adaptive case 156/156 and broader adaptive evidence 78/78. They establish execution/calculation validity at those scopes. They are not additional clinical confirmations, nor proof that source transfer is valid. Tail-moment corrections and support checks likewise establish mathematical properties; they must not be substituted for final-data utility.

The current recent evidence base is strongest when the paper keeps complete author-by-analysis panels, relates utility to the information supplied, and uses the actual adaptive/source-replay records to explain the capability. No universal model winner, overall instruction benefit, general retrieval benefit, clinical substitution, calibrated power/design validity or end-to-end efficiency claim is needed.

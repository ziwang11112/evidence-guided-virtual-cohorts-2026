## Source records and proposed decisions

**E_age_nhanes — PMC2551641, Pani et al., “Effect of Aging on A1C Levels in Individuals Without Diabetes.”** Original `sources/gateway/fac9d24329896a04d65a0f5858c64238365c01bc0b9616e64ee5bc00271c64f6.xml`; Results, paragraph beginning “There was no difference in BMI…”, and Research Design and Methods. NHANES 2001–2004, n=3,270, age >=25, no self-reported diagnosed diabetes and fasting plasma glucose <126 mg/dL; no OGTT criterion. HbA1c is DCCT-standardized HPLC, glucose hexokinase plasma assay. The reported age slope is **0.010 HbA1c percentage points/year**, adjusted for sex, BMI and fasting glucose, P<0.001; no numerical CI/SE for this coefficient in the admitted main article. Survey-weighted US association, not a causal aging effect. Proposed use: transport this conditional linear coefficient as a structural assumption for Q1, with no claim of known transport uncertainty. Continuous age, adjustment variables and units correspond, but country, serum/plasma, assay, fasting eligibility, balancing and selection details differ.

The same paper's FOS result is **0.012 percentage points/year**, additionally adjusted for 2-hour glucose; it is a contextual check, not an independent replicate of the exact estimand. FOS is a separate predominantly white cohort, not pooled with NHANES. The article's 0.014 unadjusted FOS slope and longitudinal annual changes are not used as conditional Q1 coefficients. Table 1 uses **mean ± SE**, not individual SD: NHANES age 50–54 mean 5.40%, SE 0.02%, 97.5th percentile 6.0%; >=70 mean 5.51%, SE 0.02%, percentile 6.2%. Percentile algorithm is not fully identified for exact replay. The Results also reports BMI **28.01 ± 0.14 kg/m²**, but the tiny plus/minus quantity is not used as person-level SD regardless of the text's ambiguous “mean ± SD” label. The BMI mean provides a rough level anchor only.

**E_bmi_hba1c_finnish / E_bmi_glucose_finnish / E_sex_finnish / E_agegroup_glucose_finnish — PMC6458679, anthropometry and HbA1c in Finland and migrant groups.** Original `sources/gateway/e36df343e42bf6e96a6c392fd5f3409a0eaa612a6f4797c1e626ba7917d327fb.xml`, Methods (selection, clinical measurements, variable definition, statistical analysis), Table 1 and Table 3; Additional file 3 Tables S1/S2, preserved in `sources/supplement/894ba816d1198a3b49156ed1870b76040f193f54d5636eafc367a6891b24773d.txt` and independently readable page extraction `sources/supplement/480cb548ca6a9b5bb9810cb6e9d136c620b3d58e31e469e82ac631ca7d8b283f.txt` pages 1–2. These are repeated representations of one source, not separate evidence.

Use only the **general Finnish population**, n=653, aged 30–64, complete cases, 4–17-hour fasting. Diabetes exclusions include register/medication/diagnosis evidence, HbA1c >=6.5% and fasting glucose >=7.0 mmol/L. This is stricter than H02, especially HbA1c and medication eligibility. General Finnish glucose uses serum, while migrant groups use fluoride citrate plasma. HbA1c uses immunoturbidimetry. Models use age **30–44 vs 45–64**, sex, and a single anthropometric measure; glucose analyses additionally account for fasting time. The displayed S1 model does not provide its fasting-time coefficient or reference value, so its intercept is not a fully specified transportable conditional mean.

S2 age+sex+BMI: BMI **0.0125 HbA1c percentage points per kg/m²**, P<0.0001; age 30–44 vs 45–64 **-0.1120**, P<0.0001; men vs women **-0.0230**, P=0.2884; intercept **5.1281%**. These magnitudes and the approximately 5.4% means establish that S2 is on percent HbA1c, not mmol/mol. S2 does **not** adjust for glucose. Proposed Q2 component: transfer the BMI coefficient after explicitly bridging age categorization, eligibility, assay and country. It cannot be used directly as Q3.

S1 age+sex+BMI: BMI **0.0240 mmol/L per kg/m²**, P<0.0001; age 30–44 vs 45–64 **-0.1985 mmol/L**, P<0.0001; men vs women **0.2472 mmol/L**, P<0.0001; intercept **4.2966 mmol/L**. The conversion 18 mg/dL per mmol/L gives BMI **0.432 mg/dL per kg/m²**, age contrast **3.573 mg/dL**, and male–female contrast **4.4496 mg/dL**. Coefficient CIs/SEs are not supplied in these tables. Table 1 intervals are confidence intervals of adjusted means, not marginal individual SDs. Table 3 R² is not a residual variance without an outcome variance. No residual SD will be reverse-engineered from either.

The Finnish sample supplies coherent, same-cohort BMI slopes for the two glycemic measurements, but it does not provide their conditional association or a glucose-adjusted BMI–HbA1c slope. Migrant estimates are not averaged or used as independent confirmations; their variation argues against assured population transport. Extrapolation outside 30–64 and converting a binary age contrast to a continuous age gradient remain assumptions.

**E_kerman_r / E_kerman_sd_h / E_kerman_sd_g — PMC3075530, “A Comparison of HbA1c and Fasting Blood Sugar Tests in General Population.”** Original `sources/gateway/b4f5cd47d62879840dc389a99c671814bf9890f9f8b01260e8c7a910dd92e9a6.xml`, Methods, Results, Table 1/T0001 and Table 2/T0002. Kerman, Iran, cluster-sampled adults 18–75 who attended the laboratory. Single enzymatic fasting glucose and Biosystem HbA1c. The FBS<=126 mg/dL subgroup has n=525, **Pearson r=0.23**, P<0.001, **glucose mean 91.2 mg/dL and marginal SD 12.4 mg/dL**, **HbA1c mean 5.61% and marginal SD 0.73 percentage points**, age mean 43.8 and SD 14.6, BMI mean 24.5 and SD 3.8. Importantly, **30/525 (5.71%) report diabetes**, unlike H02; there is no HbA1c selection cutoff. Fasting duration is insufficiently specified for H02's 8–12-hour eligibility. No correlation CI is reported.

For an ordinary simple least-squares projection on the same data, **b = r SD(H)/SD(G) = 0.01354032258 percentage points per mg/dL**. This is a deterministic derived **marginal** slope from rounded summaries, not a paper-reported adjusted slope. Table 2 prints crude forward 0.13 and reverse 0.38 and adjusted forward 0.09 and reverse 0.27 without a clear rescaling that reconciles these with Table 1. The reverse slope derived from r and SDs is 3.9068493 mg/dL per percentage point. The product 0.13*0.38 is approximately r², but the magnitudes suggest an unreported reciprocal rescaling. **Decline all printed Table 2 slopes for parameterization; do not silently correct them.** The proposed use of the moment-derived marginal slope as a conditional HbA1c–glucose coefficient is explicitly an unverified bridge, not recovered adjustment. An independent conditional relation is missing.

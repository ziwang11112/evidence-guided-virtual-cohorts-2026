### E_PANI_AGE — PMC2551641, Pani et al., Diabetes Care 2008

Authoritative admitted XML: `sources/gateway/fac9d24329896a04d65a0f5858c64238365c01bc0b9616e64ee5bc00271c64f6.xml`; readable text: `sources/gateway/77b5f7f6715e9a07e28eab219ed09f29f266e59d803003ba4b0240bedc5ace64.txt`. Original methods, results and Table 1 were read.

* NHANES 2001–2004: 3,270 adults aged at least 25, no self-reported diabetes, fasting **plasma** glucose below 126 mg/dL, available HbA1c; no OGTT. Analyses use survey selection/design weights, unlike the requested equal-cell design. HbA1c is DCCT-standardized HPLC; glucose is hexokinase-assayed plasma. The paper does not supply a verified fasting-duration match to 8–12 hours or the target anthropometry/documentation restrictions.
* Results paragraph immediately before longitudinal analysis: age coefficient **0.010 HbA1c percentage points/year**, adjusted for sex, BMI and fasting plasma glucose, p<0.001. No numerical CI/SE is reported in the available main-text statement. This is the selected Q1 relationship, transported with explicit population, selection, measurement and design assumptions; 0.100 percentage points/10 years is only a unit conversion.
* FOS nondiabetic cohort, n=2,473, age at least 25: excludes antidiabetic treatment or FPG>=126; main-text adjusted age coefficient 0.012 points/year additionally controls 2h postload glucose. This is corroborative context, not a pooled independent replicate of the selected coefficient. FOS NGT is a nested subset, and the longitudinal sample overlaps FOS; these are not separate independent precision contributions.
* Table 1 NHANES HbA1c means (percentage points) are 5.20, 5.28, 5.37, 5.40, 5.44, 5.46, 5.50, 5.51 for <40, 40–44, 45–49, 50–54, 55–59, 60–64, 65–69, >=70. Corresponding 97.5th percentiles: 5.7, 5.8, 6.0, 6.0, 6.0, 6.1, 6.1, 6.2. Table heading is **mean ± SE**, not individual SD. These are check-only magnitude/tail context; they will not be treated as residual SD or exact target quotas. Abstract/body/table young-age percentiles differ for some cohorts; the explicit table entries take precedence for these checks.
* Results reports NHANES BMI mean 28.01 kg/m² with ±0.14, and says BMI did not differ across age categories. The prose's `mean ± SD` label for small population-summary uncertainties is problematic. Only 28.01 is used as a discretionary location anchor; 0.14 is not used as an individual SD or converted with sqrt(n) under a complex survey.

### E_FIN_BMI and E_FIN_G_BMI — PMC6458679, Finland/Maamu study

Original XML: `sources/gateway/e36df343e42bf6e96a6c392fd5f3409a0eaa612a6f4797c1e626ba7917d327fb.xml`; text: `sources/gateway/7d7d2097fee484d2aa288bf98550aa3435f4feb2aa9b3fa9675cc1680a35109f.txt`. Supplement read in both admitted representations: `sources/supplement/894ba816d1198a3b49156ed1870b76040f193f54d5636eafc367a6891b24773d.txt` and `sources/supplement/480cb548ca6a9b5bb9810cb6e9d136c620b3d58e31e469e82ac631ca7d8b283f.txt` (same supplement, not independent studies).

* Selected source group is Health 2011 general Finnish population, n=653 (277 men, 376 women), ages 30–64 in six corresponding cities. Diabetes exclusion includes registry diagnosis/reimbursement, self-reported diagnosis/medication, **HbA1c>=6.5%**, or fasting glucose>=7 mmol/L. Complete cases and fast 4–17 hours; mean fast 9 hours. This excludes high-HbA1c cases that the target permits, has stronger medication ascertainment, and does not cover target ages 25–29 or 65–79.
* Measured standing height/weight; HbA1c immunoturbidimetric Abbott Architect assay (interassay CV 3.9%). Finnish reference glucose is **serum**, unlike migrant fluoride-citrate plasma. Survey strata, finite population correction and nonresponse weights are used. Age is a **binary 30–44 versus 45–64 indicator**. Methods say fasting-glucose analyses additionally adjust fasting time; supplement table displays age/sex/BMI and does not list fasting-time coefficient. I retain this documented adjustment rather than claiming the models exactly match the target.
* Supplement **Table S2, Age+sex+BMI, general population**: HbA1c BMI coefficient **0.0125 percentage points/(kg/m²)**, p<0.0001; **not glucose-adjusted**. Men versus women coefficient -0.0230 points, p=0.2884; young versus older age group -0.1120 points, p<0.0001; intercept 5.1281. No coefficient CI/SE is available here. I use the BMI value to anchor Q2 and the sex difference as a nuisance reference; the intercept is not transported as a literal target intercept.
* Supplement **Table S1, Age+sex+BMI, general population**: glucose BMI coefficient **0.0240 mmol/L per kg/m²**, p<0.0001; men versus women **0.2472 mmol/L**, p<0.0001; young versus older -0.1985 mmol/L, p<0.0001; intercept 4.2966 mmol/L. Conventional conversion 18.0 mg/dL per mmol/L gives BMI coefficient **0.432 mg/dL per kg/m²** and female-minus-male **-4.4496 mg/dL**. Conversion rounding is explicit. These coefficients are not correlations or SDs.
* The proposed glucose kernel uses the converted BMI and sex coefficients as pre-truncation location coefficients. Thus their final selected-population regression values change slightly. This is disclosed, not silently called exact source reproduction.
* Table 1 means are predictive age-adjusted margins with 95% CIs. Finnish BMI 26.2 (25.7–26.7) in men, 26.1 (25.5–26.6) in women; HbA1c around 5.4%; glucose 5.2 (5.1–5.3) and 4.9 (4.9–5.0) mmol/L. CIs are not individual dispersion. Migrant S2 BMI slopes 0.0116, 0.0097 and 0.0054 show context dependence; no pooling or selection by p-value is proposed. Different populations from one paper share methods and are not multiplied as independent evidence of Mexican transport.

### E_KERMAN_COV — PMC3075530, Ghazanfari et al. 2010

Original XML: `sources/gateway/b4f5cd47d62879840dc389a99c671814bf9890f9f8b01260e8c7a910dd92e9a6.xml`; text: `sources/gateway/09f088c0700c20587dd303915658f04df429f7a2d8e1546ce5eb04e8b1c4faa4.txt`. Methods, results and Tables 1–2 were read.

* Kerman, Iran, household cluster sample ages 18–75, total n=604. The n=525 group is classified by **FBS<=126 mg/dL**, not no-known-diabetes status: Table 1 explicitly includes 30 self-reported diabetic people (5.71%). This differs from both the target strict `<126` and the no-prior-diagnosis criterion. FBS enzyme method, single measurement; HbA1c Biosystem kit. Exact fasting duration, serum/plasma mapping and DCCT calibration are not sufficiently documented for an identity claim.
* Table 1 n=525: age mean/SD 43.8/14.6 years; BMI 24.5/3.8 kg/m²; FBS **91.2/12.4 mg/dL**; HbA1c **5.61/0.73%**. Results reports **Pearson r=0.23** for FBS–HbA1c in that subgroup. These SDs are marginal, not conditional. No CI is supplied for r. Cluster sampling precludes pretending an iid Fisher interval is a reported uncertainty interval.
* Deterministic covariance identity gives the **unadjusted** HbA1c-on-FBS slope r*SD(H)/SD(G)=**0.01354032258 points/(mg/dL)**. This is a derived marginal coefficient, not the adjusted slope that Q1/Q3 require. Reverse slope is 3.906849315 mg/dL/point.
* Table 2 instead prints crude forward 0.13 and reverse 0.38, and adjusted forward 0.09/reverse 0.27 (adjusted for age, BMI, sex). The literal crude coefficients are incompatible with Table 1 SD units by approximately an order of magnitude; their product 0.0494 is near r²=0.0529 and could be compatible after rounding, so the product alone is not proof of error. Unknown scaling/transcription cannot be repaired by guessing. **All Table 2 numerical slopes are declined for model use.** Statistical nonsignificance of covariates is not treated as zero effect.
* I choose the marginal covariance-derived scale as an **assumed conditional glucose coefficient**. This requires an unverified covariance/adjustment bridge and source-to-target transport. It is not a recovered conditional relationship. Under the resulting joint model the marginal glucose–HbA1c correlation is about 0.437, not 0.23; I explicitly do not claim to preserve the Kerman correlation or SDs.

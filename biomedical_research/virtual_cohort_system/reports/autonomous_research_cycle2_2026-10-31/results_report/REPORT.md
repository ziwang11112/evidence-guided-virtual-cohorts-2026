# Evidence-only whole-cohort generation: ENSANUT analytical-use pilot

31 October 2026 · Cycle 2 · Aggregate-only results report

The prespecified workflow completed and independently reproduced its numerical results. The two evidence-only constructions recovered positive directions for the primary associations, but transferred their magnitudes incompletely. Source agreement was also uneven: Lifelines summaries were numerically close, while both constructions missed Pani's older-age HbA1c plateau. Both authors retained their initial scientific models after source feedback. This experiment therefore demonstrates a reproducible feasibility workflow and its limitations; it supplies no evidence of iterative improvement.

## Design, evidence and information boundary

Two fresh-context scientific authors independently built generators using the same audited 32-item packet drawn from Pani (2008), Jansen (2013), Liu (2015), and unit-conversion guidance. The setting was full generation: age, female indicator, BMI, fasting glucose and HbA1c were all generated. Neither source individual participant data nor target individual rows, empirical biomarker summaries or target effects were supplied to the authors. The measured target was reserved for evaluation after both final freezes. Authors' file access was instruction-scoped, whereas program execution used the inspected isolated runtime; these are distinct protections. This report reads aggregate outputs and retained scientific records only.

Pani's NHANES 2001–2004 analysis included 3,270 adults aged at least 25 without self-reported diabetes and with fasting plasma glucose below 126 mg/dL. Its survey-adjusted age coefficient and eight survey-weighted age-band HbA1c means were used. Jansen's Lifelines sample comprised 2,921 unrelated western-European adults selected for a genome-wide association study, favoring older relatives and excluding diabetes, significant anemia and relevant missing measurements. Its marginal summaries and separate age/sex-adjusted BMI and glucose projections supplied constraints. Published row-specific regression counts and printed interval confidence levels remained unresolved. Liu's NHANES univariate glucose equation remained contextual; it was not combined with adjusted coefficients as a structural equation. Pani/Liu survey years overlap and are not independent replications. Jansen's differently adjusted Table 3 remained quarantined.

The target reference comprised all 6,931 eligible linked ENSANUT observations under the frozen preparation rules. Eligibility required ages 25–79, no reported previous doctor diagnosis, recorded fasting of 8–12 hours, positive fasting serum glucose below 126 mg/dL, available HbA1c, and the specified standing-anthropometry documentation. BMI used the first complete valid same-ordinal standing weight/height pair, with a second-pair fallback; this was an investigator-defined rule. Pregnancy/lactation exclusion followed documented questionnaire routing for women aged 25–49. Anemia and medication exclusion were not verified, and older women's unasked status remained unresolved. An outcome-blind amendment resolved contradictory measurement documentation before effects were inspected, excluding one contradictory record in the authorized second preparation attempt.

Four age bands (25–39, 40–54, 55–69, 70–79) crossed sex to form eight equally weighted analytical strata. Reference cell counts ranged from 222 to 1,570; each person received weight 1/(8 × cell count). Each fictional target table contained exactly 250 rows per cell, or 2,000 rows. This finite age/sex-standardized reference is not Mexico's national population. Uniform generated ages within each band and balanced sex allocation were design assumptions, not learned target distributions.

## Constructions and actual work

Although independently authored, A and B used the same linear-Gaussian family and assumed BMI–glucose residual correlation of 0.30. They are two parameterizations, not structurally diverse alternatives. Each jointly solved the two separate Lifelines projection equations under its assumed residual covariance, then used Pani's adjusted age term of 0.010 HbA1c percentage points/year. Both assumed zero direct conditional female effect, homoscedastic normal HbA1c residuals and shared conditional laws across contexts. Residual HbA1c spread came from Lifelines' reported individual SD; Pani survey SEs were never converted to individual SDs.

A assumed BMI/glucose age trends of 0.025 kg/m² and 0.10 mg/dL per year, with female shifts of +0.6 kg/m² and −1.5 mg/dL. B used 0.04, 0.12, +0.5 and −1.0 respectively. A anchored HbA1c through a count-weighted mixture of Pani's survey age-band means; B used the 50–54 mean of 5.40% at assumed age 52. These are approximate location choices, not recovered survey joint distributions. Both transferred their own identical Pani anchor to the target, without a Mexico-specific repair. Lifelines age support 18–95, Pani support 25–95 and older-age tails were assumptions. Positivity and glucose-threshold rejection conditioned biomarker draws while preserving demographic allocations.

Each author received one source-only feedback opportunity. Four source tables were actually generated: two contexts per construction, 6,000 fictional rows each, at seed 18101. All 11 Lifelines and nine Pani checks per author were computable as approximate diagnostics. Both authors retained their generator and model specification byte-for-byte, explicitly leaving source discrepancies unresolved. Source outputs were reused. Six unique target tables were evaluated, using seeds 18203, 18211 and 18223 for each construction. The schedule preserves 12 initial/final labels, but retained finals duplicate initial outputs and add no independent information. All ten biological generator runs and all 12 analytical slots succeeded. Metadata-only controller corrections are preserved in `CONTROLLER_ACTIONS.jsonl`; they changed no scientific model or estimator and added no draws.

## Operators and empirical stability

Weighted least squares used age10 = (age − 50)/10, BMI5 = (BMI − 25)/5 and glucose10 = (glucose − 100)/10. Q1 was the age coefficient in HbA1c ~ age10 + female + BMI5 + glucose10. Q2 was BMI in the model omitting glucose; Q3 was BMI in the full model. Q2 − Q3 measured the descriptive coefficient change after glucose adjustment. Secondary glucose used HbA1c ~ age10 + female + glucose10. Q1/Q2 are source-indexed cross-population transfers; Q3 is a prespecified conditional analysis not directly supplied as a calibration target. It still depends on the authors' shared joint assumptions and is not an independent biological discovery.

The 31 fixed endpoints also included all five variables' weighted means, SDs and 10th/50th/90th percentiles, plus full-model residual RMS. Target SDs and RMS used normalized weights without degrees-of-freedom corrections; source checks used their specified sample-SD convention. Weighted quantiles used the inverse empirical CDF. Source regressions used raw units and the original adjustment sets, separately from scaled target coefficients.

The declared stability procedure resampled eligible laboratory PSUs within represented laboratory strata and recomputed the eight-cell standard. All 399 attempted replicates at seed 18301 were valid, with no empty-cell or singular-design failures. The frame contained 317 represented strata and 2,450 eligible PSUs, including 36 singleton strata. The displayed 2.5th–97.5th percentile bands are empirical stability bands conditional on this selected finite reference and perturbation scheme. They are not confidence intervals, national design-based inference, equivalence limits or population truth. Seed ranges describe fictional-row variation, not model, parameter or transport uncertainty.

## Source reproduction

All 40 source results are retained in the [complete source appendix](APPENDIX_ALL_SOURCE_CHECKS.md). Lifelines HbA1c mean/SD were 5.599/0.296 for A and 5.596/0.295 for B, against 5.6/0.3. Its BMI projection was 0.010533/0.012153 versus 0.011892 HbA1c percentage points per kg/m²; the glucose projection was 0.011549/0.011399 versus 0.011638 per mg/dL. These differences are descriptive, with no predeclared pass threshold or pooled quality score.

Pani's adjusted age coefficient was 0.009918 for A and 0.009649 for B against 0.010 per year. Nevertheless, the marginal age profile was incompletely reproduced (Figure 2). At ages ≥70, means were 5.63261 and 5.65743 versus 5.51%, discrepancies of +0.12261 and +0.14743 percentage points. The reported source SE was 0.02. A also underestimated the youngest mean by 0.05776 percentage points; B underestimated it by 0.02850. This age pattern was not dismissed as simulation noise. Unknown covariate age relationships, nonlinear aging, source age tails, survey weighting and selection prevent identifying a unique repair. Approximate source fit neither establishes the joint distribution nor validates transfer.

## Target analytical results

Every primary coefficient had the same positive direction as the finite reference. Magnitude agreement was substantially weaker (Figure 1). All six Q1 age estimates exceeded the reference band's upper endpoint; every Q2 and Q3 BMI estimate fell below its lower endpoint. These comparisons describe discrepancies without defining a hypothesis test or equivalence rule.

Q3 remained positive but was markedly smaller than the reference. This shortfall cannot be attributed solely to an incorrect unknown covariance. Descriptively, Q3 = Q2 − (Q2 − Q3): most of each Q3 shortfall was already present in Q2, with an additional discrepancy in the coefficient change. Q2 − Q3 was positive in every seed; all A values and two B values exceeded the reference band's upper endpoint, while B seed 18211 lay within it. This is neither causal mediation nor identification of the mechanism causing transfer failure.

Secondary glucose estimates were numerically close and all lay within the reference stability band. This is a limited favorable transfer result, not equivalence or whole-cohort validation. The marginal diagnostics expose further limitations (Figure 3). Generated BMI means were 26.21–26.42 kg/m² versus reference 28.66; BMI SDs were 3.98–4.22 versus 5.52. Glucose SDs were 8.98–9.21 mg/dL versus 11.94, and HbA1c SDs 0.328–0.338 versus 0.527. Generated HbA1c means were 5.381–5.424% versus 5.314%, while full-model residual RMS was 0.254–0.260 versus 0.488 percentage points. The mean and SD differences are properties of these analytical datasets, not statements of known population parameters.

All age, female, BMI, glucose and HbA1c summaries and quantiles appear in the [31-endpoint appendix](APPENDIX_ALL_ENDPOINTS.md), including every seed. The exact 50% female mean and 0.5 SD are imposed by standardization, so their agreement supplies no learned validity. The female median of zero follows the fixed inverse-CDF convention. Within-band age assumptions remain observable design differences despite equal age-band mass.

## Verification, limitations and decision

The prewritten independent checker ran once and recorded MATCH with zero mismatches: all 31 reference endpoints, six unique generated outputs, 12 scheduled labels and 31 stability bands agreed, including all 399 valid-replicate accounting. The maximum absolute numerical difference was 5.68434 × 10⁻¹⁴. The independent audit verified target arithmetic; its scope did not independently adjudicate source reconstruction. Reporting code additionally cross-checked all 403 aggregate CSV rows against JSON without accessing participant rows, fitting models or resampling.

Transport remains unestablished across US survey, European selected-source and Mexican analytical populations. Serum/plasma comparability, assays, fasting duration, ancestry, anemia, medication, pregnancy/lactation routing, missingness and standing-measurement selection are unresolved. More fictional rows do not supply missing clinical information. Shared Gaussian form, linearity, covariance 0.30 and source anchors leave substantial untested joint-structure uncertainty; agreement between authors cannot demonstrate robustness across model families.

Continue only as a bounded methodological feasibility case with complete discrepancy reporting. Stop the claimed iterative-improvement branch for this cycle: both constructions were retained, so no improvement was tested or observed. Any future genuinely different construction needs its own evidence, preregistered assumptions and a new untouched evaluation target. This exposed ENSANUT reference is permanently closed to tuning and new-confirmation claims. These fictional cohorts cannot substitute for patient measurements, establish national prevalence or causal effects, or justify synthetic biological significance tests.

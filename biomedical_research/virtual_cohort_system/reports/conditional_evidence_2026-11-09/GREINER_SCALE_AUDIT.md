# Greiner 2021: unresolved regression scale

Root review, 9 November 2026. No target outcomes or generated tables were used.

## Original sources

- [Article and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8141440/), DOI 10.1136/bmjgh-2021-005222.
- [Publisher supplement](https://gh.bmj.com/content/bmjgh/suppl/2021/06/08/bmjgh-2021-005222.DC2/bmjgh-2021-005222supp001_data_supplement.pdf), Table 3.
- [Author-deposited article PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/181857/1/e005222.full.pdf).

The methods describe log transformation of nonnormal variables and geometric summaries, then linear/spline analyses. Table 3 reports sex-specific African HDL regression coefficients, including BMI and non-HDL predictors, with age/smoking and additional alcohol adjustments. Neither the inspected text nor table caption unambiguously specifies each regression's outcome and predictor transformation. Plot axes and plausible coefficient magnitudes do not resolve this ambiguity. Selection excludes diabetes, pregnancy, HIV and missing sex/lipids. Malawi includes MEIRU participants also represented in the Soares papers; these are not independent evidence replications. Laboratory methods differ from the current target assay. The published correction concerns an author's name, not statistical scale.

## Decision

Retain the existing context-only classification. Do not use these coefficients as raw mmol/L per BMI unit, and do not silently treat non-HDL associations as TC-HDL residual associations. An unresolved scale is a source-definition gap, not evidence that the proposed generator is wrong. Further copying or searching the same table cannot resolve an omitted definition.

This root assessment is not counted as independent confirmation of an admitted numerical constraint. Independent review focuses on the strongest Soares and Liu candidates.

# Body-composition source admission review

2026-10-30. Source research only; no target rows, generator, calibration, or target evaluation was run.

**Verdict: usable external evidence, but do not yet admit an identity-measurement NHANES 2017–18 validation case.** Pongchaiyakul 2005 is the simplest core because age/sex/BMI and its source outcome fit the current operators directly. Gallagher 2000 supplies a useful alternative published equation. However, the target's documented body-composition correction creates an unresolved measurement gate; common “DXA %fat” labels do not establish equivalence. These papers also lack the complete source covariate distribution. An assumption-dependent transport stress test is technically possible, but an exact measurement/source replay claim is unsupported.

**Target measurement gate, metadata only.** [Official DXX_J documentation, Data Processing and Editing](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2017/DataFiles/DXX_J.htm#Data_Processing_and_Editing), paragraph beginning “During the editing process,” states that NHANES BCA adds 5% of lean mass to fat mass. Protocol/Scan Analysis identifies Hologic Discovery/Apex v4.0; total percent fat is DXDTOPF. This BCA operation is separate from phantom QC corrections. None of the admitted sources above establishes output on that exact corrected scale. Do not blindly apply the Hologic correction to Lunar outputs. A percent-fat summary alone does not identify a lean-to-fat conversion unless the denominator/lean convention and requisite component proportions are known. Required next evidence: an external matched measurement protocol or defensible cross-calibration with uncertainty, or a different public target demonstrably matching the source measurement. The identity-only adapter cannot itself supply that missing scientific mapping.

**Best directly replayable source.** [Pongchaiyakul et al., Obesity Research 2005, 13:729–738](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1038/oby.2005.82), DOI 10.1038/oby.2005.82. Locators: Methods—Study Design, Measures of Body Composition, Statistical Analyses; Tables 1, 2, 4; Results—Predictive Equations Using BMI and Age; Figure 5 caption. Rural Thai adults, 20–84 years, randomly sampled; illness, weight-affecting medication and weight-training exclusions. Whole-body fat percentage: Lunar DPX-IQ DXA. Random development/validation split; Table 1 reports means ± SD:

| Sample | n | Age, years | BMI, kg/m² | DXA fat, % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Development men | 98 | 50.7 ± 17.0 | 22.3 ± 2.7 | 14.1 ± 6.5 |
| Development women | 125 | 49.4 ± 17.4 | 23.8 ± 4.0 | 31.8 ± 8.4 |
| Validation men | 83 | 47.2 ± 17.9 | 22.4 ± 3.0 | 14.0 ± 6.3 |
| Validation women | 130 | 51.8 ± 14.1 | 24.3 ± 4.1 | 32.2 ± 8.6 |

Reported adjusted OLS: `BF% = −10.672 + 1.648*BMI + 0.065*age − 15.3*male`; male=1, female=0; R²=.83. Table 2 development Pearson BMI–BF: men .72, women .77. Validation agreement limits −8.9 to 8.6 percentage points; coefficient SE/covariance absent. The text’s positive mean difference conflicts with its negative-centered interval and Table 4; exclude that discrepancy from calibration.

**Alternative equation, with an operator limitation.** [Gallagher et al., AJCN 2000, 72:694–701, original article PDF](https://zakboekdietetiek.nl/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/Am-J-Clin-Nutr-2000-Gallagher-694-701.pdf), DOI 10.1093/ajcn/72.3.694. Locators: p695 Methods/Table 1; p696 Table 2 and Equation 1; p697 distinguishes the separate four-compartment models. Convenience sample n=1626, BMI≤35, screened healthy adults at US/UK/Japanese sites; vigorous-training exclusions. DXA: Lunar DPX/DPX-L and Hologic QDR1000 with site-specific software. Equation 1 estimates DXA percentage, with BMI kg/m², age years, M=male indicator, A=Japanese-Asian indicator:

`BF% = 76.0 − 1097.8/BMI − 20.6*M + 0.053*age + 95.0*A/BMI − 0.044*A*age + 154*M/BMI + 0.034*M*age`.

Multiple R=.90; SEE=4.31 percentage points. Tables 1–2 give sex/ethnicity counts and age/BMI margins, not measured DXA-fat mean/SD. Coefficient SE/covariance absent. The later four-compartment equation and BMI-derived “healthy” ranges must not be relabeled DXA observations. An age/sex/BMI-only target needs an explicit ethnicity restriction or an assumption to use this equation.

**Additional DXA summary evidence, not a matching adjusted equation.** [O’Connor et al., TIGER study, 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2925069/), DOI 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181dc2e71. Locators: Subjects/Measurements; Tables 1–3. Sedentary Houston students aged 17–35; 705 women/428 men; Hologic Delphi-A v11.2 and Discovery W QDR v12.3. Table 1 pooled DXA-fat mean/SD: women 32.0/7.5%, men 19.8/7.7%; pooled age 21.4/3.1 and 21.8/3.2 years; BMI 26.3/6.3 and 27.0/5.5 kg/m². The women's pooled n is misprinted as 428 in the HTML table; subgroup counts and Methods total 705. Table 2 BMI coefficients .849 (SE .026), .608 (SE .038) adjust for linear/quadratic skinfolds and ethnicity in repeated-measure mixed models. These are not age/sex/BMI-only OLS slopes. Use only clearly scoped marginal summaries for optional approximate replay; do not borrow those conditional coefficients or mixed-model errors.

**Familiar equations excluded from the identity-DXA core.** [Deurenberg et al., 1991, original PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/9C03B18E1A0E4CDB0441644EE64D9AA2/S0007114591000193a.pdf/div-class-title-body-mass-index-as-a-measure-of-body-fatness-age-and-sex-specific-prediction-formulas-div.pdf), abstract p105 and Methods p106: `BF%=1.20*BMI+0.23*age−10.8*male−5.4`, adult R²=.79, SEE=4.1 percentage points; underwater weighing with density-to-fat conversion, not DXA. [Gómez-Ambrosi et al., CUN-BAE, 2012](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc11-1334), abstract Methods/Results: air-displacement plethysmography, n=6510, measured 39.9±10.1%, SEE=4.66%; separate validation n=1149. That measurement mismatch suffices to exclude it here; its full model was not re-extracted because full-text access became inconsistent. Neither error estimate can be imported as a DXA residual SD without a separate cross-method model, which the identity-only adapter does not provide.

**What current source replay can honestly check.** Interface documents inspected: `docs/SOURCE_REPLAY.md` and `docs/SOURCE_CHECKS.md`. With numeric male coding, percentage-point outcome and BMI in kg/m², the Thai source permits sex-specific arithmetic means/sample SDs/Pearson correlations, plus pooled OLS coefficients adjusted for age, BMI and male. Reconstructing covariate-only rows from margins requires declared age–BMI dependence, distributional shape, truncation and selection assumptions; mark every such context `approximate`. No actual source individual covariates were found. Distinct DXA manufacturers/software also require a measurement-transfer assumption: common units do not prove scanner equivalence.

The contract must allow the source age support even if target inclusion is narrower. For example, a universal age≤59 column bound would prevent replay of the full Thai source. Do not silently truncate source ages while retaining the original source statistics. Gallagher's reciprocal-BMI and interaction coefficient checks require prepared design columns; the existing operator does not create transformations, and source inputs must match the task's observed-column contract. Consequently those checks are unavailable in a strict age/sex/BMI-only contract. SEE, R² and agreement limits also lack direct current replay operators; retain as reported evidence rather than changing their statistic type. The agreement-limit width can only motivate a clearly labeled residual-scale derivation, not coefficient uncertainty or target uncertainty calibration.

**Minimum defensible analysis plan.**

1. Resolve the measurement gate before admission. Then the custodian freezes one target cohort and outcome definition, holds all measured body-fat values outside builder access, and releases only age, numeric sex, BMI and an identifier. Target eligibility, scan-validity decisions and exclusion of body-fat-derived companion variables are fixed first.
2. Admit hashed original source artifacts, exact locators and the corrections/ambiguities above. Use Thai development summaries/model as the primary source context; keep source validation summaries distinct. Include Gallagher only with a predeclared ethnicity treatment and compatible target support. No source-statistic feedback becomes target feedback.
3. Compare a locked published equation, that equation plus a predeclared residual model, and the Agent construction under the same inputs. Deterministic published equations already implement the central age/sex/BMI enrichment task; adding random noise is not itself a new method.
4. Freeze a small target analysis panel before any outcome access: sex-specific mean/SD and quantiles, BMI-stratum distributions, age/BMI adjusted associations, and within-sex rank association. Most analysis *types* may occur in sources, but their target values are unprovided. Label truly unprovided functionals separately. If a stronger biomedical use is wanted, add an evaluator-only clinical endpoint and predeclare its analyses; current evidence cannot support claims about its residual association with fat.
5. Evaluate once through the custodian after freeze, separating source agreement, target distribution/association error and individual prediction error. Survey-weighted population claims require a separately specified survey-aware evaluator; current unweighted source operators do not supply one. Repeated seeds quantify simulation variability, not new clinical evidence or calibrated transport uncertainty.

**Bounded final check and revised recommendation (2026-10-30).** This addendum distinguishes the requested assumption-dependent transport pilot from the stronger identity claim discussed above. Three primary studies were checked; no target outcome paper or participant rows were opened in this check.

- [Schoeller et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15883424/), DOI 10.1093/ajcn/81.5.1018, Abstract—Design/Results/Conclusions: seven external research datasets compared Hologic QDR4500A with dilution, densitometry or four-compartment criteria. The published abstract reports n=1195, ages 19–82, BMI16–44 and fat-free-mass overestimation averaging 5±1% (SE). Its recommendation is to reduce **lean soft tissue mass** by 5% and add that mass to fat. This is neither a fixed five-percentage-point increase in body-fat percentage nor a universal Lunar-to-Hologic conversion. Full-text publisher retrieval was blocked; the primary abstract and the already verified official metadata agree on the correction rationale.
- [Shepherd et al. 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jbmr.1654), DOI 10.1002/jbmr.1654, Methods—DXA scanning/Scan analysis/Data conversion and Abstract—Results: paired Lunar **Prodigy/enCORE14.0** and Hologic **Discovery/Delphi/APEX3.0** scans, n=199 retained participants. Percent fat was fat mass divided by total mass, including BMC. Percent-fat correlation was .96 and average absolute difference 1.4 percentage points; regression cross-calibration reduced systematic differences. This demonstrates that calibration can work for specified systems. It does not establish a coefficient for the older DPX-IQ source to Discovery/APEX4.0 with BCA. The retrieved Methods did not establish the BCA toggle; later table retrieval failed, so no conversion coefficients are admitted.
- [Pearson et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21600823/), DOI 10.1016/j.jocd.2011.03.008, Abstract: 21 adults underwent QDR2000/Prodigy comparison. Percent fat differed systematically; phantom calibration did not adequately reproduce in-vivo percent-fat calibration. The authors caution that differences vary among centers/instruments. Abstract-only evidence supports caution about generalization, not a numeric mapping for this case.

For the *same* uncorrected Hologic scan, the recommendation implies `F*=F+0.05L`, `L*=0.95L`, with total mass unchanged, hence `p*=p+5L/T` when p is in percent and L/T is a fraction. This is algebra, not an estimated transfer equation. It cannot be applied to a source percentage without the requisite components/conventions, and applying it to Lunar results would introduce an additional unsupported equivalence assumption. The reported 5±1% SE is not an individual residual SD or a ready-made transport prior.

**Recommendation for the actual method scope: proceed with one explicitly assumption-dependent transport pilot.** Perfect source joint knowledge and proven scanner identity are not absolute gates for this purpose. Use the published equation and the Agent construction as competing transport assumptions; preserve the operational outcome definition as target DXA body-fat percentage, and state that measurement and population shifts may both cause failure. The primary untransformed application is a testable zero-additional-measurement-bias assumption, not proof that the instruments agree. Freeze it before target access. Predeclare source age–BMI dependence scenarios and measurement shift/scale sensitivity scenarios; numerical sensitivity choices must be labeled assumptions, not extracted calibration or probability distributions. Do not invent or select a correction using target outcomes.

Under the current adapter, source-context mean/SD/OLS/Pearson checks can remain `approximate`, with these assumptions recorded. Source replay shows numerical consistency under the source context; the separate held-out target assessment tests transport. A nonidentity measurement transformation cannot be hidden inside an `identity` declaration or selectively applied outside the frozen code/specification. If such a transformation becomes central, it needs a documented preparation/model route with uncertainty, or an explicit adapter extension. Baseline transport without that transformation is already a defensible falsifiable pilot. Report failures as failures, keep the published-equation comparators, and avoid interpreting source fit or sensitivity robustness as validated calibration.

## Model and scientific scope

The model is a two-component bounded lognormal mixture for baseline glucose and HbA1c, joined by component-specific Gaussian copulas. The broader component is a mathematical distributional device, not diabetes, treatment status or another observed clinical category. Discrete maximum-entropy age matches supplied moments and specified quantile-bin probabilities; bounded lognormal BMI approximates supplied mean/SD; female is Bernoulli at the selected-sample proportion. Age, BMI, female and mixture innovations are independent by explicit simplifying assumption. No female biomarker effect is specified. These unidentified relations are not inferred from silence in literature and are not supported analytical uses.

The exposed CHNS sample reference supplies all numerical fitting targets. Small age and BMI shifts support the declared unweighted adjusted E1, E2 and E3 projections through explicit covariance algebra. The equations are not causal mechanisms. Marginal parameters use deterministic moment/CDF optimization; copula covariance uses deterministic Gauss-Hermite integration, with no random integration or synthetic-row calculations outside the isolated runtime. The covariance solver was originally executed inline and retained afterward; its first unclipped calculation failed numerical bracketing, then clipping corrected that calculation before submission. Both analytical scripts are retained and hash-bound. Sample-size 3000 is a design choice.

All moments are finite. Baseline glucose is bounded 2.5-25 mmol/L and HbA1c 3.5-16%; BMI is bounded 10-55 kg/m^2 and age 35-74. Additive shifts preserve strictly positive biomarker support (glucose >2.2 and HbA1c >2.4). These tail bounds are assumptions, not observed CHNS extrema. The broad-component mixture probability .16 sits at its optimization bound, showing the representation is not uniquely identified. Glucose rounding to two decimals and HbA1c to one is an unverified resolution approximation. Tail shapes, interactions, subgroup distributions, weights and cluster effects remain unassessed.

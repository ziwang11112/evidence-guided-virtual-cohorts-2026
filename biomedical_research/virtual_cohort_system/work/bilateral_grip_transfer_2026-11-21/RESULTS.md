# When the paper describes each hand separately, can the agent estimate their difference?

Four GPT sessions saw only Australian means and standard deviations for each hand. All four chose a within-stratum Gaussian copula with correlation 0.80. Their virtual adults had a mean absolute between-hand difference of **3.81 kg**. The same calculation on measured participants gave **3.30 kg in the US and 3.78 kg in rural South Africa**. Randomly pairing the very same one-hand values gave **7.93 kg** in expectation. The prespecified primary criterion was at least a 1 kg reduction in absolute error at both sites. The reduction exceeded 3.65 kg across both full measured intervals. [Frozen design](PROTOCOL.md) · [Predictions sealed before disclosure](BEFORE_DISCLOSURE.md) · [Measured results](RESULTS.json)

## Three different recruitment settings

The main analysis used 1,423 US and 2,434 South African participants aged 50–69. The two groups used the first two measurements of each hand. A separate measurement stress test used 464 Rio Branco participants, for whom the release gives each hand's maximum across attempts. These are 4,321 measured participants, of whom 3,857 belong to the primary comparison. For every estimate the four sex-by-decade groups have equal weight. US and Brazilian estimates use the applicable survey weights within groups. The rural South African estimate describes eligible measured participants with village-based uncertainty. All intervals are pointwise 95% design-based jackknife intervals. ## What the agent chose and what the paper supplied

The Australian source supplies separate right- and left-hand means and standard deviations by age and sex. We fixed the same marginal quantile grids for all groups and methods. Each author wrote an executable rule for assigning the two measurements to one individual. Each of the four M sessions independently gave latent correlation 0.80. Their four programs differ in text but produce byte-identical assignments at every shared seed. This amounts to one numerical prediction. Its generated rank correlation is 0.784. The four J sessions additionally saw a German study's mean and standard deviation of individual nondominant/dominant pressure ratios. The German authors pooled ages and sexes and used a different instrument. Three J authors used the ratio dispersion to derive a dependence parameter. The fourth chose 0.85 as a working correlation. All four yielded distinct assignments. [Australian primary study](https://doi.org/10.1186/1756-0500-4-127) · [German primary study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnagi.2024.1368052) · [Decisions and program hashes](AUTHORS.json)

Improvement in the primary contrast is a reduction in absolute error. Point gains from the M assignment relative to independence were 4.12 kg in both main populations. The full-interval gains were 4.12 kg in the US and 3.65–4.12 kg in South Africa. The point gains coincide because both measured gaps were below both predictions. Giving the agents the German summary improved the median author's gap error by 0.43 kg in the US (range over its measured interval +0.16 to +0.43 kg). The South African point error rose by 0.37 kg (gain range −0.43 to +0.16 kg). The Brazilian stress-test point gain was 0.43 kg (+0.09 to +0.43 kg). Positive transfer of the extra evidence therefore depended on the measured population. The prespecified ratio-based statistical rule estimated 3.42 kg. Its absolute error differed from the median J author's by only 0.04 kg at each of the three estimates. [Frozen numerical comparisons](PREDICTIONS.json) · [Full results](RESULTS.json)

## What the marginal distributions leave open

The finite source grids permit a mean gap anywhere between the minimum of 1.75 kg for equally ranked hands and the maximum of 11.12 kg for reversed ranks, allowing mixtures between pairings. Both extremes use precisely the same published marginal summaries and the same marker values. A retrospective Gaussian sensitivity calculation illustrates why the data support a range of plausible decisions. Common within-group correlations of about 0.762–0.883 meet the 1 kg absolute use margin over both principal reference intervals. This calculation used a continuous normal approximation after disclosure and generated no new participant data. It supplies neither a fitted parameter nor a new confirmation. [Calculation and exact endpoints](SENSITIVITY.json) · [Sensitivity figure](figures/grip-correlation-sensitivity.pdf)

A prespecified diagnostic retained the frozen permutations and supplied each target's two separate empirical marginal distributions, without its original left–right pairings. The bilateral means then tracked their measured values to within 0.002 kg by construction. M's mean gaps became 3.60, 3.97 and 3.97 kg in the US, South Africa and Brazil. Correcting the marginal levels reduced the M point error in the US but increased it in South Africa and Brazil. This is a within-sample diagnostic with descriptive point discrepancies. Measurement posture, trial summary, exclusions, equipment and source-to-target heterogeneity can all affect interpretation. ## Cost, independent computation and disclosure

Eight completed GPT-6.1 Sol calls at medium effort cost an estimated **US$0.262162** at the recorded tariff. Each executable ran on the same three prespecified seeds with 16,384 virtual rows per seed table. The 24 tables describe simulation variability under eight authored decisions. Independent analysis from the original releases reproduced the four measured estimates and intervals at all three sites, the source predictions, target-marginal diagnostic, rank-permutation hashes and tariff total. The independent US and South African computations used different file readers. The Brazilian SPSS computations share the ReadStat parsing backend. Only aggregate results, reproducible methods and provenance are public. [Independent verification](VERIFICATION.json) · [Verification code](verify.py)

The US and South African survey families had previously contributed other measurements. This was their first registered paired-grip comparison. Rio Branco was a separately recruited sample whose maximal-trial measure was designated as a stress test in advance. The investigator had seen ancillary single-hand descriptions during admission. The paired target results were kept from constructors, and the prospective packet and all predictions were published before opening paired outcomes. Model pretraining exposure is unknown. All three paired targets are now disclosed.

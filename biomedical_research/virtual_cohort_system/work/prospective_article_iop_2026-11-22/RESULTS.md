# Which eye-pressure analyses survived a switch to virtual participants?

Given the original article text and one research request, a scientific agent independently identified two relevant measurements, wrote a paired generator and distinguished a source-supported average from a threshold probability requiring an assumption about the distribution of individual discrepancies. On the same 100 left eyes, the observed Pascal-minus-Goldmann mean was **1.8321 mmHg** and **29%** had a difference strictly above 3 mmHg. The agent's primary virtual tables gave **1.83 mmHg and 30.83–30.98%**. Only this primary model met all prespecified source and plausibility checks. The experiment therefore succeeded for the primary data and for the author's prospective interpretation. It did not produce two admissible models demonstrating the prespecified sensitivity contrast. ## What kept the stronger claim from passing

The author acknowledged the absolute tail count and used it as a diagnostic. It chose to match the two marginal means, two SDs and Pearson correlation. Matching those five quantities across every model did not ensure an eligible tail. The skewed model moved the requested rate by 7.29–7.58 percentage points relative to the primary on the same seeds, enough for the prespecified change threshold. Its absolute tail fell below the accepted band on every seed. Zero of the three scenario pairs passed the complete sensitivity criterion. The source's measured mean difference is directly supported, the observed primary tail prediction is close in this study, and the scientific claim about sensitivity under *two eligible* alternative models remains unproven on this case.

# What happens to the scientific analysis when the data are replaced?

A researcher wants to compare lifestyles after adjustment for age and sex. Two agents received the published Turkana study and its available summaries. They could retrieve additional papers. Each wrote an executable generator, including its assumptions about the group differences, age effects and variation among participants. The models generated the complete table without individual participant inputs. The published conclusions were available during construction. [Original article and Figure 2A](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7577730/) · [Measured study release](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.2z34tmpjk) · [Frozen task and analysis](../lifestyle_application_2026-11-11/PROTOCOL.md)

For this edition we ran the original frozen evaluation function on the original measured file and the six previously sealed virtual tables, two authors and three seeds per author. The program reproduced all 189 previously archived contrasts, including the nine prespecified additive comparisons on each table, the sex-specific results, and all 21 interaction tests. All seven input hashes and the executable analysis files matched the original receipt. A separate computation from the matched-size study agreed on all 63 additive coefficients in native units. The procedure reimplements the authors’ reported method for three available traits. The versioned code from the published study was unavailable. The urban BMI association retained its direction and approximate magnitude in both constructions. Its mean absolute difference across two matched resamples of real participants was 0.316 kg/m². The corresponding virtual–real discrepancies ranged from 0.314 to 0.428 kg/m². The urban cholesterol direction also carried over, although both authors understated the measured point estimate in mg/dL. Dividing each table’s effect by its own outcome SD gives 0.451 and 0.496 against the measured 0.475. Expressed in the *same measured* SD, the two virtual effects are 0.349 and 0.386. Measuring the discrepancy in two units exposes how a narrow synthetic distribution can make standardized effects look close. The measured rural cholesterol interval includes zero. Its point discrepancy matters for reproducing the estimated magnitude, while the data leave the population contrast uncertain. All three HDL comparisons are in the figure inputs. We also replayed the established source closures, 54 draw receipts, paired analyses and original measurements for the CHNS iron experiment. The external Zhu article and newly calculated summaries from 1,123 women in source communities were available. The 1,082 women in 107 held-out communities contributed no inputs to the agent. Six final models gave hemoglobin–ferritin Spearman correlations of 0.297–0.304, against 0.317 measured [0.259, 0.373]. The overall rank correlation was absent from the source tables. Threshold odds ratios were 4.99–5.12, against 4.22 measured [2.98, 6.04], under a distinct use-specific tolerance and with direct source evidence about threshold counts. The additional conditional statistic did not meet the original criterion for an incremental accuracy gain in either arm. A separate Nigerian sample in the existing study had a measured correlation of 0.010 compared with a median of 0.308 from six local reconstructions based on the transferred sources. These analyses distinguish within-survey transfer from a change of recruitment population. This was a read-only replay and a change of presentation. It used no new cohorts, model calls, parameter fits for generation, or revised decisions. The real–real resamples measure conditional variation within one observed study. The reuse of existing estimates provides no new independent validation. All participant rows remain local. To reproduce the public comparison from the project root, run `python -B work/kdd_v45_build_2026-11-21/analysis_substitution.py` and `python -B work/chns_iron_conditional_holdout_2026-11-19/public_replay.py`. Local custodians with the registered input files can additionally use the first script's `--private-replay` option and the original CHNS `verify.py`.

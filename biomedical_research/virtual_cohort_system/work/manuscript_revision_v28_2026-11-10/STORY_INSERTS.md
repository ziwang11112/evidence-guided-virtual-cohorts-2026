## Abstract

Biomedical studies often publish aggregate findings while the individual measurements needed for a new analysis remain inaccessible. We study whether a scientific agent can turn such evidence and a research request into analyzable virtual cohorts. Within a host-reviewed workflow, the agent interprets study conditions, chooses a joint stochastic model, and records numerical evidence and assumptions; statistical tools execute the model and inspect its outputs. In a developmental open-retrieval evaluation on previously exposed references, four constructions produced twelve complete cohorts. All six requested glycemia analyses retained their sample-reference point directions across draws, with mixed magnitude agreement. In a separate two-case experiment with twenty agent constructions, additional published relationship evidence was associated with smaller mean absolute discrepancy for five of six regression coefficients. A new prespecified blood-pressure task in a previously exposed PNS release added five authors and fifteen cohorts, sealed before the three requested reference analyses were revealed. Agreement differed across analyses: systolic-pressure slopes were closer to the reference than diastolic-pressure slopes, while residual dependence was consistently weaker. A retrospective audit of thirty constructions distinguished source statistics, adopted parameters, full-model implications, sampled estimates and measured references. Lipid and iron examples showed how explicit model choices can account for generated behavior without establishing target validity. Provenance categories described the origin of inputs, not an ordering of accuracy. The sealed iron odds-ratio comparison and earlier prospective criteria remained unsuccessful. These results demonstrate evidence-to-model construction and partial agreement for specified analyses without target individual-level training records. They support making modeling choices executable and inspectable; reliable inference for a new population remains unestablished.

## Introduction contributions

Our contribution is an implemented scientific-agent procedure for authoring joint biomarker models from incomplete, heterogeneous published evidence, together with evaluation of the scientific analyses performed on its generated records. The agent interprets populations, measurement scales and adjustment sets, chooses how to combine findings, and supplies the relationships that the evidence leaves unspecified. The resulting cohort supports several analyses of the same joint construction, while its executable model and provenance record make the underlying choices available for inspection.

We evaluate this procedure through repeated glycemia constructions, a comparison with and without additional relationship evidence, and iron-status, lipid and blood-pressure tasks with different information boundaries. The new blood-pressure task uses prespecified analyses in an already exposed dataset family, with construction closed before those reference analyses were revealed. We also retrospectively trace selected scientific analyses through five distinct quantities: the source statistic, the parameter adopted by the author, the analysis implied by the complete model, its estimate in generated records, and the measured reference estimate. This account shows what a construction inherits from evidence and what its additional choices determine. It supplements the empirical comparisons without treating source provenance as a validated measure of target accuracy. Published-model reconstruction and earlier prospective tests retain their separate roles.

## PNS methods

Placement: optional alternative or supplement to the existing PNS manuscript insert.

### A new blood-pressure analysis task in an exposed dataset family

Five fresh author contexts received the same target description, requested analyses and reviewed external evidence packet. Each author constructed a complete cohort containing age, sex, BMI, systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP), without access to target measurements or the three target reference results. The analyses were the BMI coefficients in SBP and DBP regressions adjusted for age and sex, and the Pearson correlation between their residuals after adjustment for age, sex and BMI. Slopes were expressed in mmHg per 5 kg/m² BMI. Generated records received equal weight; the reference used the released PNS laboratory-substudy weights in the prespecified eligible sample of 8,352 adults.

Each retained law produced three cohorts of 3,000 rows using fixed seeds. All five authors retained their initial reviewed laws; all fifteen final outputs were sealed before the reference analyses were revealed. The PNS release had already been used for HbA1c analyses, and its column names were known. The history audit found no previous numerical exposure of the three requested blood-pressure analyses. This was therefore a new prespecified task within a previously exposed dataset family, not a wholly unseen cohort or a globally blind validation. The selected weighted reference sample was not asserted to represent a known national population distribution.

We report all authors and seeds. Reference intervals were the existing 95% conditional IID row-resampling stability intervals, retaining each sampled row's weight. They were not survey-design confidence intervals or equivalence margins. To describe author agreement, we took each author's median across its three seeds, then calculated the mean and sample standard deviation of those five medians. The absolute difference between their mean and the reference, divided by this sample standard deviation, summarizes how the shared discrepancy compares with author spread. This ratio is descriptive, not a test statistic or a calibrated uncertainty measure: the five authors shared a host workflow, an evidence packet and one reference sample.

## Attribution methods

### Retrospective attribution of requested analyses

After the evaluations, we assembled an attribution audit from frozen model specifications, generator source, evidence-use records and existing aggregate results. The audit covered twenty CHNS/ENSANUT constructions, five PNS blood-pressure constructions, three lipid constructions and two iron constructions. Each had three requested analyses, giving ninety requested-analysis records; six lipid marginal-SD records supplied additional dispersion diagnostics. These thirty constructions were a defined subset of the study record. HRS, HAALSI, statistical controls and other historical workflow versions were not added to this attribution table. Their original results remain separate.

For each requested analysis, we distinguished the following quantities.

| Quantity | Meaning and role |
|---|---|
| Source statistic | What the study reports, including its population, variables, scale, adjustment, weighting and uncertainty. |
| Adopted parameter | The numerical value or rule placed in the generator, with any unit conversion, derivation or assumption used to obtain it. |
| Full-model implication | The requested statistical analysis applied to the complete probability law, including its covariate distribution, dependence, nonlinearities and selection. This need not equal a literal regression coefficient in the code. |
| Generated estimate | The requested analysis applied to a finite draw from the frozen law. Seed variation describes sampling under that law. |
| Measured reference | The same requested analysis in the eligible reference sample, with its own weighting, selection and uncertainty. |

Several source statistics and assumptions can contribute to one model parameter or analysis. We therefore retained source-to-parameter links and named the additional modeling choices, rather than assigning one label to an entire cohort. REPORTED, DERIVED and ASSUMED describe origin. A derived value may depend on assumed inputs, and a reported coefficient can require an assumption when carried into another population or adjustment context. These labels do not form an ordered credibility scale.

The audit reused available analytical derivations and numerical summaries. Numerical full-model implications were available for seven of the ninety requested-analysis records and all six supplementary SD records. The remaining eighty-three requested-analysis implications were left uncomputed; they were not filled with literal parameters, source coefficients or generated estimates. This incompleteness limits comparisons between theoretical and sampled results; it does not mean that the requested analyses were unavailable in generated data.

We inspected existing artifacts without reading individual rows, executing generators or rerunning clinical analyses. The underlying frozen specifications document choices made during construction where available; the cross-case taxonomy and interpretations were assembled retrospectively. Generated summaries retain their original conventions: medians across three seeds for PNS and means for the other audited cases. Seed ranges are not confidence intervals. We did not fit or validate a provenance-based error predictor, rank constructors using retrospective risk labels, or identify a causal decomposition of discrepancies.

## PNS results

### Blood-pressure cohorts support different analyses to different degrees

All five PNS authors used the same two published BMI coefficients in their pre-selection conditional blood-pressure location equations. Rescaled to the requested BMI unit, those source coefficients were 3.8770 mmHg for SBP and 2.9835 mmHg for DBP. The generated age/sex-adjusted slopes did not simply reproduce these numbers. Across all fifteen retained cohorts, the SBP slope ranged from 3.0814 to 4.2756, compared with a weighted reference of 3.5240. The DBP slope ranged from 2.8863 to 3.6906, compared with 2.2599. Residual SBP–DBP correlation ranged from 0.4658 to 0.6462, compared with 0.7172. Every generated DBP slope exceeded its reference point estimate, and every residual correlation was lower. The SBP slopes lay on both sides of their reference.

| Requested analysis | Mean of five author medians | Between-author sample SD | Weighted reference | Absolute mean-to-reference gap / author SD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BMI–SBP, mmHg per 5 kg/m² | 3.7549 | 0.2259 | 3.5240 | 1.02 |
| BMI–DBP, mmHg per 5 kg/m² | 3.0783 | 0.1601 | 2.2599 | 5.11 |
| Residual SBP–DBP Pearson correlation | 0.5482 | 0.0573 | 0.7172 | 2.95 |

The larger DBP and residual-correlation ratios show that close agreement among these authors coexisted with a shared discrepancy from the reference. They do not estimate the probability of an error or account for reference uncertainty. In particular, the author SD is not a standard error for the reference comparison. All five constructions used shared evidence and conventions, so their spread cannot be interpreted as the full uncertainty in evidence selection or population transfer.

The source and requested regressions also differed. The published BMI coefficients came from within-centre regressions pooled across centres and adjusted for additional urinary and alcohol variables. The requested PNS projections adjusted for age and sex. The generators added nonlinear age terms, age-dependent residual variation and feasibility selection. The five conditional innovation correlations, 0.50, 0.65, 0.50, 0.55 and 0.55, were assumptions informed by context; a correlation derived from published unadjusted SDs was not a reported adjusted residual correlation. Consequently, literal uptake of source coefficients does not prove exact reproduction of the final requested analyses, and these comparisons cannot assign all discrepancy to population transfer. They demonstrate complete construction and analysis under a sealed task boundary, with analysis-specific limits on agreement.

## Attribution results

### From evidence to a joint law and its scientific analyses

The thirty-construction audit helps explain why an evidence-informed cohort can agree on one analysis and disagree on another. Adding relationship evidence reduced mean absolute discrepancy for five of the six main-study coefficients, but this was not five instances of an assumed endpoint becoming a reported fact. The CHNS fully adjusted age coefficient improved at arm level even though no age relationship was supplied. The ENSANUT BMI coefficient adjusted for glucose was derived from separate reported projections together with assumed covariance; it was not directly reported in the source. Moreover, the E0 and E1 authors were unpaired. The observed contrast therefore characterizes these evidence conditions and constructions, without identifying a universal provenance upgrade or a unique mechanism of improvement. The CHNS BMI deterioration likewise cannot be attributed exclusively to transfer, because the authors differed in the relationships constrained, distributional completion and target-law choices.

The lipid construction L03 provides a case in which the full-model implication can be separated from both the source and the sampled result. Published unadjusted Spearman correlations were converted to a latent Gaussian dependence model and combined with lognormal margins and assumptions about sex and age. The complete law implied a Pearson correlation of 0.1322 between TC and HDL residuals after adjustment for age, sex and BMI. Its generated mean was 0.1413 across three draws, whereas the measured reference was 0.5121. Thus the weak generated residual association was already a property of the chosen law. It was not merely a difference between one sampled cohort and its intended model. The source rank correlations and the requested residual Pearson correlation were different statistical quantities, so their difference cannot be read as a direct source-reproduction error. The reference residual-correlation interval was unavailable.

The iron models show a different, identifiable link between an author's decision and a scientific analysis. The reported joint threshold counts implied a crude odds ratio of 6.653. Author B retained that threshold structure. Author A first reduced the relative weight of low ferritin, which changed prevalence while preserving the odds ratio, then independently redrew hemoglobin in 20% of rows from the reweighted hemoglobin marginal distribution. That second operation reduced the model odds ratio to 4.646. The generated means were 4.855 for A and 6.362 for B, compared with the weighted reference of 17.790. These are consequences of the frozen probability laws; they do not establish a biological mechanism for the population difference.

Conversely, B's continuous rank relationship depended on unreported within-band modeling choices, yet its generated mean Spearman correlation was 0.411, close to the reference point estimate of 0.398. In the main study, the assumption-dependent ENSANUT E0 author A05 also had a smaller discrepancy for the age/sex-adjusted BMI coefficient (Q2, before glucose adjustment) than any E1 author. These observations rule out a guarantee that reported or derived inputs always give more accurate target analyses than assumed inputs. They do not show that provenance is unrelated to error in general. The present cases are too few, heterogeneous and retrospectively examined to establish that predictive relationship.

The audit also separates evidence uptake from a completed numerical check. In Q5, all 132 declared source-check entries remained unavailable under the required source operators. Numerical source values still entered the laws through reviewed derivations and named assumptions, but the batch did not provide measured numerical source-fit feedback. Retention of the initial laws therefore cannot be described as all source-fit checks passing or as evidence of a revision benefit. Together, these findings locate which scientific relationships follow from the construction and which remain commitments needing further evidence.

## Discussion

The scientific value of the workflow lies in turning a research request and fragmented evidence into a joint model that can be sampled and analyzed. A complete cohort allows several regressions, conditioning choices and dependence summaries to be evaluated on the same modeled individuals. The accompanying executable law makes it possible to inspect why those analyses agree or disagree, and to formulate alternative assumptions when the evidence does not determine a relationship. Our empirical results support the feasibility of this construction and selected agreement with measured references. They do not yet support replacing target measurements for reliable population inference.

The attribution audit sharpens the distinction between evidence use and target validity. A source estimate, a code parameter and a requested analysis are interchangeable only under additional conditions. Unit conversion may be exact, while transport across populations remains assumed. A parameter derived from several published summaries can still depend on an invented covariance. A sampler may realize its intended law while that law implies residual dependence substantially different from the measured target reference, as illustrated by L03. The iron example goes further: a documented redraw operation changes the model odds ratio in a calculable way. Such inspection identifies an actionable modeling decision without claiming that it explains all disagreement with the clinical reference.

PNS adds a prospective task boundary to an exposed dataset family and reinforces the need to assess each requested analysis. Authors could agree reasonably closely with one another while sharing an offset from the measured reference. The same concern applies to repeated seeds, which measure variability within a law, and to shared evidence, which can transmit the same limitation to several authors. Neither reproducible generation nor author consensus supplies calibrated uncertainty about an unfamiliar target. The descriptive author-spread ratios make that distinction visible without introducing an acceptance threshold.

These results motivate keeping provenance alongside operator definitions and modeling assumptions, rather than turning REPORTED, DERIVED and ASSUMED into a quality score. A prospective test of predictive value would require labels and comparison rules fixed before unseen outcomes, adequate variation in tasks and evidence, and explicit treatment of shared sources and author workflows. The present retrospective audit supplies an inspectable record for such work; it does not perform that validation. Its theoretical coverage is also incomplete: only seven requested-analysis records have numerical full-model implications, leaving eighty-three without that part of the chain.

## Conclusion

Scientific agents can author complete, executable biomedical cohort models from incomplete published evidence and explicit assumptions. Across the evaluated tasks, their records enabled the requested analyses and achieved partial, analysis-specific agreement with measured references. Following a quantity from its source through the adopted law to its sampled analysis makes both useful constraints and unsupported commitments visible. This supports the workflow as a tool for constructing and examining biomedical models. Reliable inference for a new population, calibrated uncertainty and a predictive ordering of provenance categories remain to be established.

### Anatomy of the retrospective attribution record

The supplementary record contains ninety requested-analysis entries and six additional marginal-SD entries from thirty constructions. The requested entries comprise sixty CHNS/ENSANUT entries, fifteen PNS entries, nine lipid entries and six iron entries. Each entry preserves its original aggregate summary, units, adjustment set, reference definition and pointers to the frozen specification and supporting evidence. Original grouped provenance is retained; an evidence label on a group is not automatically propagated to every numerical member. The audit does not count identifiers, seed values or bookkeeping constants as biological assumptions.

The table should show the source statistic and its statistical meaning, the adopted parameter and parent assumptions, the requested full-model implication where available, the generated summary and seed range, and the measured reference with its reported uncertainty. Missing numerical theory stays explicitly missing. The seven populated requested-analysis implications comprise L03's two adjusted slopes and residual correlation, and the odds ratio and joint-low prevalence of each iron model. All six lipid marginal SDs have numerical theoretical implications. No other requested-analysis value should be filled by borrowing a source estimate or sampled result.

For PNS, distinguish copying a source BMI coefficient from reproducing the requested projection. Source SBP and DBP slopes were 0.7754 and 0.5967 mmHg per 1 kg/m², with SEs 0.0359 and 0.0257. Multiplication by five gives the values used for comparison in the main text. These SEs quantify precision of the source estimates, not individual variability or uncertainty in transport. The contextual raw SBP–DBP correlation was derived from rounded SBP, DBP and pulse-pressure SDs, assuming paired observations and a common variance convention. It did not determine the requested adjusted residual correlation. All five generators used nonlinear mean structure, age-dependent residual scales and BP feasibility selection; A02 rejected complete proposals, whereas the other four redrew BP conditional on retained covariates. The full-model requested projections were not numerically derived in this audit.

For lipids, retain the distinction between pooled unadjusted Spearman evidence and adjusted raw-scale slopes or residual Pearson correlation. L01 and L03 handled the published marginal variance by subtracting the modeled between-sex component before selecting within-sex variances; their construction was not a confusion of marginal SD with regression residual SD. L02's mean TC SD, 43.678 mg/dL, was near the reference 42.847, while its mean residual correlation was 0.108 versus 0.512. Thus dispersion and dependence require separate evaluation, and the lipid results do not support saying that every modeled quantity was too narrow.

For iron A, write the structural operation as a mixture of the reweighted joint distribution with the product of its marginals: 80% joint draws and 20% independently re-paired hemoglobin draws. This preserves the reweighted margins while changing dependence. For B, retain the source-determined threshold structure separately from assumptions about continuous values and their within-band pairing. The source OR of 6.653, B's generated mean OR of 6.362 and the reference OR of 17.790 must occupy different columns. B's rank-correlation agreement must remain an outcome of the whole assumed construction, not a reported source target.

Present these records as traceable accounts of construction, not as ninety-six independent confirmations. No overall provenance-error correlation, causal attribution percentage or inferred error-risk label is reported.

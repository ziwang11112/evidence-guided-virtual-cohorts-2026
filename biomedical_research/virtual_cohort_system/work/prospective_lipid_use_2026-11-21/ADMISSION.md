# Participant source and exposure audit

The community endpoint validation uses the public Central Virginia screening data supplied by John Schorling at the University of Virginia and hosted by Vanderbilt Biostatistics. The archive covers 403 screened people from 1,046 people interviewed in a rural African American community study. The analysis uses 400 unique adults aged at least 20 with both lipid measures and a known county, 198 in Buckingham and 202 in Louisa. The counties are two strata of one study. Estimates apply to this screened sample. The nominal virtual records use the older Mwanza source law. A larger synthetic draw does not increase this sample size. [Study archive](https://hbiostat.org/data/repo/diabetes) · [Original community research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9258308/) · [Public CSV](https://hbiostat.org/data/repo/diabetes.csv) · [Original S data dump](https://hbiostat.org/data/repo/diabetes.sdd)

The native S labels identify total cholesterol, HDL and the individual TC/HDL ratio. A documented analysis of the same release by the Publish package authors names TC in mg/dL. We corroborated common units using the separately recorded ratio. Two eligible records differed by more than 0.075 in this QC arithmetic, and both remain in the analysis. Target ratio estimates will be calculated from the two measurements for every eligible participant. Individual fasting status, lab platform, social sampling clusters, survey weights and lipid medication use cannot be standardized to the Tanzanian article from this release. A common US teaching dataset may have appeared in model training. Fresh sessions see only an anonymous description of the target population, measurement and selection, without its name, URLs, patient values or endpoint estimates. [Documented analysis and variable label](https://cloud.r-project.org/web/packages/Publish/refman/Publish.html) · [Counts and digests](ADMISSION.json)

Ten primary study candidates were adjudicated using methods, availability or prior exposure. Gondar had only aggregate statistics and questionnaires. Dessie diabetic outpatients had HDL and triglycerides without TC. The Tigray paper gave research instruments only. Mizan Aman had no measured lipid pair in its dictionary. The Ugandan rural GPC family was exposed. The other Dessie adult follow-up paper enrolled a mixed hospital population. The Bahir Dar paper offered no participant file through its public supplement. The Bangladesh dataset's actual CSV header contained glycemic measurements and no cholesterol. The Virginia community release qualified. The Iraqi registry documented diabetes classes but its depositor did not establish type 2 status for the diabetic class or separate it by subtype. We closed the type 2 recruitment frame at the prespecified limit. Title-only hits and the mistaken archaeology DOI were not candidate cohorts. Thus there is one new study.

# Proposed use-focused addition; v0.30 is unchanged

## One analysis operator applied to real and virtual cohorts

We evaluated scientific reuse by applying one fixed analysis to existing real and synthetic records. Following the age- and sex-adjusted biomarker-correlation procedure described in Lea et al. (2020, supplementary Methods), we fitted the same nuisance models and calculated residual Pearson correlations for the three biomarker pairs available in the generated schema: BMI–total cholesterol, BMI–HDL cholesterol, and total–HDL cholesterol. All six initial Agent constructions and their three saved draws were included. Two previously completed repairs of one construction were evaluated separately as dependent sensitivity analyses. The protocol and implementation were frozen before these calculations; no generator was changed or executed.

The processed real sample contained 396 complete adult records after the specified age >18 rule. Its three correlations were 0.070, -0.117 and 0.498, respectively. Across the six initial constructions, corresponding three-draw means ranged from 0.185 to 0.314, -0.147 to -0.0005, and 0.121 to 0.363. Thus, the same virtual tables supported several recognizable association directions, but did not uniformly preserve the relative magnitudes of the real-sample relationships. One construction's BMI–HDL estimates changed sign around zero. The dependent repairs did not remove the quantitative differences. Every pair and draw was retained, and a separate covariance-based implementation reproduced all estimates to numerical precision.

This analysis makes the intended data product directly assessable within an existing biomedical analysis, rather than treating executable delivery or marginal agreement as sufficient utility. It is a Methods-guided three-variable reanalysis, not execution of unchanged author code or reproduction of the original paper's full ten-biomarker, lifestyle, mediation and early-life analyses. Common-case selection is an explicitly specified implementation choice where the original report did not resolve the rule. The cohort was already exposed in project development, the real sample is not population ground truth, and generated-draw ranges do not measure population uncertainty. No equivalence or universal superiority claim follows from this comparison.

Suggested figure: a three-panel plot with every saved virtual estimate and the real-sample estimate; retain all six constructors in fixed order, and put both dependent repairs in an accompanying table. A common-scale correlation-matrix panel offers a visual overview. Do not label its diagonal or repeated cells as additional analyses.

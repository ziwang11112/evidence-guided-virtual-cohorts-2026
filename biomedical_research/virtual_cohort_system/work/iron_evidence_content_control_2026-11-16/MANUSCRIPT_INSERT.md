# Proposed manuscript addition — second content-control case

Editorial proposal only. The current v0.32 manuscript and prior frozen experiments are unchanged.

## Main-text Results

**Joint evidence improved a threshold association while exposing transfer-sensitive trade-offs.** We repeated the three-condition evidence-content comparison on an existing iron-status case. All authors received the same research request and external study context; the marginal condition additionally received single-marker low counts and a ferritin geometric mean, and the joint condition additionally received paired threshold counts from the same source population. Nine fresh GPT constructions delivered 27 fixed-seed cohorts. No target outcome or host-selected dependence parameter was provided to constructors.

Relative to marginal summaries, joint counts reduced the mean absolute natural-log OR discrepancy from 1.7620 to 0.9373 (46.81%). The generated OR increased from 3.064 to 6.973, compared with the exposed measured-sample estimate 17.790. However, joint-prevalence discrepancy increased from 0.519 to 2.594 percentage points and rank-correlation discrepancy increased from 0.0447 to 0.1736. The complete profile, including two marginal diagnostics, accompanies the primary result.

All joint-informed authors independently fitted a Gaussian copula to the source counts. This converted the supplied binary association into a coherent sampling program but also extended it to an unreported continuous rank relation. The source low-ferritin prevalence was 30.16%, compared with 12.15% in the target sample; direct source-margin transport persisted in both augmented conditions. Thus, successfully compiling source evidence did not establish general target validity. The result supports analysis-specific evidence use alongside the PNS residual-correlation finding, while retaining the adverse analyses.

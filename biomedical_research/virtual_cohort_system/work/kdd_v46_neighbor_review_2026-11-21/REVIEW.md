# What can we attribute to the agent's biomedical model?

KDD reviewer assessment of v0.46 · 21 November 2026

**Verdict.** The paper has a defensible empirical contribution. A scientist can run the same analysis on measured and virtual participants, then change one identified decision in the agent's program to test how much that decision contributed. Paired handgrip and iron status provide the strongest positive evidence. The external iron and lipid comparisons limit the stronger claim that an agent learns a better joint model than a reasonable statistical constructor. I would keep the analysis-level contribution and promote handgrip from the appendix. [Reviewed manuscript](../../manuscript/kdd_draft_v46_2026-11-21/external_review.pdf) · [Current contribution statements](../../manuscript/kdd_draft_v46_2026-11-21/main.tex)

**Patient-Zero's useful precedent is its fixed downstream task.** On the same GPT-5 backbone its static record consistency score rises from 70.76 with direct generation to 99.12 with hierarchical generation. On a fixed Qwen3-8B, training on synthetic records scores 85.0 on MedQA and 83.0 on MMLU. Training on real data scores 83.5 and 85.5. These measure two distinct uses of the output. For our researcher the corresponding downstream task is a specified regression, gap or screening fraction. [Patient-Zero, Tables 1 and 3](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1149.pdf#page=7)

**KMGen's most relevant comparison is inside its own pipeline.** Table 2 reports integrated survival-curve discrepancies of 0.049, 0.051 and 0.040. Recovery of an individual's adverse-event burden is weaker. In one trial the generated mean is 6.9 events per patient against 12.8 measured at the main setting. Appendix D switches off patient risk multipliers while retaining the marginal survival model. Appendix E holds the sampler and seeds fixed and substitutes a curve calculated from the measured participants for the curve extracted from the paper. The latter isolates the contribution of curve extraction in a diagnostic run. The authors also explicitly qualify their comparison with the previously published KM-GPT results because it used different plots. [Main results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v340/main/assets/jiang26a/jiang26a.pdf#page=12) · [Figure 10 and Tables 4–5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v340/main/assets/jiang26a/jiang26a.pdf#page=25)

Both predecessors use agents. KMGen already investigates how a dependence mechanism affects a patient-level outcome at a fixed marginal. Our distinctive question is what a scientific analysis gains when an agent **chooses and implements** the relationship left open by a paper, and where that choice continues to work on measured participants. It can be tested across biomarkers and populations, with the source, code choice and consequence visible together.

## The one comparison that could change the verdict

**Can the agent choose a scientifically useful joint model when two credible models already allow dependence?** The present paper can support an empirical account of analysis-specific utility and attribution. A stronger contribution about the agent's comparative modeling ability requires a fair opportunity for its decision to matter.

1. Register one paired-measurement analysis on a scientifically eligible target whose paired endpoint has stayed concealed. Select it from the population, measurement protocol and source coverage before viewing effects. Use one primary estimand that depends on pairing, and one companion estimand determined by the marginal values. An example is a pre-specified fraction exceeding a paired-difference threshold, accompanied by the mean of the two measurements. The threshold must have a use-based justification.
2. Supply the same paper and audited source statistics to both constructors. Share exact marginal values, strata, units, sampling weights where applicable and seeds. The agent selects and codes a joint model with a recorded rationale. The other arm uses a conventional nonzero model fixed on the same information before dispatch, such as a pooled association supported by the source. Both may use the same admissible conditional information. A post-hoc choice of a favorable correlation in the published grip data would invalidate the prospective comparison.
3. Seal all estimates, the smallest worthwhile difference and the paired analysis before disclosure. Record how far apart the two predictions are in the scientific outcome's units. If they coincide to within that margin, report the convergence and retain the case. Increasing simulation size cannot manufacture a meaningful difference between two identical laws.
4. Run the identical estimator on the sealed virtual records and on measured participants. Compare absolute errors with uncertainty appropriate to the participant sampling design and report the invariant endpoint alongside the primary. One measured target counts once regardless of the number of agents or seeds. A gain, convergence, or a statistical rule doing better all answer the same question. The currently exposed handgrip, CHNS and Nigerian outcomes can be used for developmental reanalysis only.

## Head-to-head decision and presentation

Document both methods in related work. A numerical Patient-Zero comparison would require a shared patient-record or dialogue task. A numerical KMGen comparison would become meaningful for a shared oncology arm, identical public sources, and an agreed survival or adverse-event endpoint with an evaluable individual-level reference. Clinical consistency scores, survival-curve discrepancies and handgrip errors cannot be ranked on a common scale. KMGen is the closer methodological precedent. Its component intervention and measured reference offer more to this draft than an adaptation of its trial sampler to handgrip. [Patient-Zero](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1149/) · [KMGen](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v340/jiang26a.html)

Keep the Kenyan result as a short use case. Put handgrip beside CHNS in the main results, showing the measured interval, agent prediction, and re-paired prediction in each endpoint's own units. Show an invariant outcome next to the pairing-sensitive one. Give Nigeria or Ethiopia visible space as a test of transport and of the nonzero statistical comparator. This makes the computational decision and its scientific consequence legible in the same figure. For a KDD Research submission, the general contribution is the attribution experiment. The conference explicitly distinguishes general methodology applicable across domains and domain-led scientific applications.

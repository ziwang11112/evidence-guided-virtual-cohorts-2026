# Can the same virtual lipid records support two population analyses?

One fresh GPT-6.1 Sol author and a prespecified statistical generator received six estimates from the same Tanzanian paper and the same marginal lipid values. The author derived the same Gaussian-copula calibration from the published mean TC/HDL ratio, without receiving the comparator code or coefficient. Replaying the unaltered submitted program gave 25.53% at or above non-HDL 160 mg/dL. The statistical law gave 25.52%. Measured proportions were 21.18% in community residents and 20.96% in university staff. Three seeds per law describe Monte Carlo variability. These are two geographically distinct recruitment frames within Ethiopia and one new Agent author.

Intervals describe each measured sample under the frozen age-stratified resampling procedure. The identical virtual tables are used at both sites. For the agent the percentage-point excess was +4.34 in Mekelle (95% difference interval −0.02 to +8.70) and +4.56 in Haramaya (+2.33 to +6.89). The preregistered planning illustration allowed ±2 percentage points (20 people per 1,000). The community comparison remains unresolved and the staff comparison lies outside the tolerance. For the statistical law the respective gaps were +4.34 (−0.02 to +8.70) and +4.56 (+2.32 to +6.88). The interval for Mekelle includes zero, as well as discrepancies larger than the tolerance. The mean had no pass margin. Its signed gaps were +3.65 and +5.19 mg/dL.

## What was supplied and what the agent contributed

The published Mwanza stratum contains 170 municipal adults with nonfasting serum measurements. Its weighted means are TC 4.72 and HDL 1.39 mmol/L. Their difference, also reported, fixes 3.33 mmol/L or 128.77 mg/dL at the published precision. The source also reports a mean of individual TC/HDL ratios of 3.66, plus two marginal threshold frequencies. It supplies no non-HDL fraction at 160 mg/dL in the shared packet. [Original Tables 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0223189) · [Exact source extract](SOURCE.json)

Both laws share a prespecified lognormal shape for each margin and identical order statistics. The positive upper-tail log-SD equation for TC has two solutions; the smaller root was fixed in advance. The agent chose its own dependence model, distinguished a mean ratio from a ratio of means, and solved for the latent correlation from the former. Both arms give a latent correlation of approximately 0.05855 and simulated mean ratios of 3.65936 and 3.65787 respectively. The two implementations differ in drawing latent ranks. Their threshold estimates differ by 0.00625 percentage points (3 of 48,000), a small realization-specific difference. Selecting a scientifically interpretable law was feasible here; the equivalent statistical model gave the same practical answer.

Across all permutations of these particular fixed marginal arrays, the fraction can range from 9.02% to 41.54% without imposing the source ratio or a physiological constraint. These are outer limits, not confidence intervals or an estimate of achievable accuracy subject to all source constraints. Both measured fractions lie inside them. The observed discrepancies do not by themselves distinguish marginal shape, source uncertainty, dependence, fasting and population transfer. HDL exceeded TC in 56/48,000 Agent records, 47/48,000 statistical records, 0/321 Mekelle records and 7/1,164 Haramaya records. The prespecified complete-positive-data analysis retained them. ## Interface, exposure and verification

The original receipt rejected the JSON because the prompt omitted five inherited field names for the decision record. The raw Agent arm had zero successful draws. Before either outcome was opened, a separately registered validation view mapped the existing narrative keys into the required slots. The response, executable source and scientific choices were untouched. The three isolated executions of that code succeeded. One paid call consumed 1,000 input and 8,865 output tokens at an estimated tariff of US$0.09065. No further calls or scientific revisions were made. The Mekelle source is the published SPSS supplement of Gebreegziabiher et al. The Haramaya source is the published Stata supplement of Motuma et al. The recruitment places, sampling frames and assay instruments differ. The studies have no known shared recruitment; anonymized files cannot establish cross-study linkage. The evaluation includes fasting participants aged 20 or older with a unique study ID and both positive concentrations, regardless of medication. Target-specific documents and rows remained on the evaluation side. Separate SPSS and Stata readers reproduced row membership and both estimates. All six sealed virtual arrays, unchanged API code and settings, common margins, counts, uncertainty and planning decisions were recomputed. The independent checks use separate code on the same research team.

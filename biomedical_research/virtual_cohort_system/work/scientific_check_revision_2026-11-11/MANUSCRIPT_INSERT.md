## Proposed Methods subsection

### Scientific-law inspection and revision

We extended the executable evidence-to-data workflow with mathematical, physiological and joint-analysis inspection tools. The Agent records distribution support, transformations and moment claims; proposes declarative physiological rules with their measurement interpretation and justification; and identifies the parameters and assumptions supporting each requested analysis. A deterministic worker evaluates declared rules on isolated generated output, reports generated-only analytical summaries and checks that review records refer to the selected version of the model. A heuristic code screen locates potentially problematic transformations. These checks do not certify source extraction, transportability or general mathematical correctness. The Agent retains authority to obtain more evidence, revise its generator, retain its model or report an unresolved relationship.

We evaluated review on all twelve frozen generators from the preceding autonomous construction study. Each law was imported unchanged into two fresh sessions using the same GPT configuration, scientific artifacts, tools and resource ceilings. Ordinary review could freely inspect and revise; structured review explicitly covered all three dimensions and required an inspection receipt for the selected law. Both policies could use all checks and supplementary literature retrieval. The comparison therefore evaluates a review policy and completion gate under shared capabilities, rather than exclusive access to quality-control tools.

No clinical target estimates or host-selected numerical repairs were supplied. All 24 sessions closed before final generation and clinical comparison. Each selected generator produced 3,000 fictional rows under three fixed seeds. Initial outputs were the 36 previously saved tables. An independent source/code audit was frozen before comparison with the previously exposed clinical sample references, followed by independent recomputation from all saved synthetic tables. We report every requested analysis and dispersion/coherence diagnostic, including retention and unfavorable changes. This is a developmental paired revision study in two existing contexts, not a new-population validation or a fresh end-to-end policy comparison.

## Proposed Results subsection

### Scientific repairs did not uniformly improve analytical agreement

All 24 review sessions finalized and all 72 planned final tables were delivered. Review used 343 API calls at a known token-tariff estimate of USD4.87. Every Agent used the inspection tool. Both review policies retained ten of twelve starting laws and revised the same two laws.

For one lipid generator, both Agents repaired an ideal continuous-law defect: a positive exponential link applied to shifted-lognormal BMI yielded divergent TC moments. Ordinary review replaced the BMI contribution by a positive affine term. Structured review used a log-BMI predictor and additionally constructed TC as the sum of positive HDL and non-HDL concentrations. Independent algebraic review supported finite positive polynomial measurement moments for both fixed revised specifications. The structured change also imposed an ideal component-concentration interpretation; its reference covariance remained assumption-dependent.

For one blood-pressure generator, both Agents moved half-unit rounding before pair acceptance, preventing a potential delivery exception after continuous acceptance. The ordinary version retained a finite retry cap, while the structured version used rejection until acceptance without a deterministic runtime bound. All six saved outputs for this pair were byte-identical to their corresponding initial outputs. The sixty outputs from explicitly retained laws were also byte-identical. Thus empirical analysis changes were confined to one lipid starting law.

For that law, the adjusted BMI-TC coefficient changed from 14.615 to 14.106 after ordinary review and to 13.364 after structured review (mg/dL per 5 kg/m2), versus an existing sample estimate of 5.683. Residual TC-HDL correlation remained approximately 0.359 under both repairs versus 0.512 in the sample reference. TC SD decreased from 36.687 to 36.503 and 36.418 mg/dL, respectively, compared with 42.847 mg/dL in the reference. HDL dispersion likewise remained low. These changes demonstrate a mathematical repair with mixed analytical consequences, rather than a general scientific-quality gain. The complete figures retain every starting law, all requested analyses and all prescribed seeds.

No final BP table had SBP below DBP. A separate retained lipid law reproduced its original single HDL-above-TC row in each review arm; it was not removed. The preclinical audit found no newly identified residual-dependence constraint for the revised laws and retained a minor source-country wording defect. Independent numerical recomputation passed 13,419 checks with a largest arithmetic discrepancy of 9.33e-14. These implementation checks are not independent experimental replications.

## Proposed Discussion paragraph

The revision experiment separates properties that are often conflated in synthetic-data evaluation. An executable program can generate plausible finite samples while its ideal sampling law has undefined moments. Repairing that law can improve mathematical validity without supplying missing cross-variable information or resolving population transport. Similarly, enforcing a physiological component identity changes the measurement model and does not by itself validate downstream associations. Ordinary review performed the same principal repairs as the structured policy, so these findings support reusable inspection tools rather than a claim that added workflow structure generally improves data. The contribution remains evidence-guided construction of analyzable virtual cohorts with inspectable modeling assumptions; review documents which defects can be repaired and which analysis limitations persist.

## Methods

We contextualized virtual–real analysis discrepancies using matched-size real–real comparisons in the existing lifestyle case. For each trait, we drew 500 pairs of real bootstrap samples and 500 bootstrap samples from each of six frozen virtual tables, preserving the reference's six lifestyle-by-sex cell counts. Available-case sizes were 1,189 for BMI, 400 for total cholesterol and 405 for HDL cholesterol. All samples were evaluated using the same frozen age/sex-adjusted OLS operator; effects were expressed in original units. Each virtual comparison used the same real B resample as its corresponding real–real comparison. We report the complete signed and absolute difference profiles for all three pooled contrasts per trait, with sample SD as a companion diagnostic. No generator was revised. The conditional empirical ranges describe sampling variation within these fixed tables; they are not population-valid equivalence intervals.

## Results

At matched sample size and group composition, the virtual cohorts reproduced the principal urban BMI group comparisons at a useful approximate scale. For the urban–pastoralist contrast, matched-resample mean effects across the six virtual tables ranged from 1.649 to 2.262 kg/m², compared with 1.896 kg/m² in the real reference resamples. Mean absolute virtual–real discrepancies were 0.314 to 0.428 kg/m², versus 0.316 kg/m² for real–real. Urban–pastoralist total-cholesterol discrepancies were also similar in scale (8.556–9.479 versus 8.848 mg/dL), although the small measured urban group limits precision. This did not extend to every analysis: rural–pastoralist effects remained lower by 7.800–9.545 mg/dL for total cholesterol and 4.074–6.208 mg/dL for HDL. Mean virtual-resample SDs were 31.486–32.674 and 14.496–14.889 mg/dL, respectively, compared with original measured-sample SDs of 42.811 and 20.228 mg/dL. Thus, selected group-comparison uses are supported more strongly than variance-dependent uses; agreement on a contrast is insufficient to establish adequate population heterogeneity.

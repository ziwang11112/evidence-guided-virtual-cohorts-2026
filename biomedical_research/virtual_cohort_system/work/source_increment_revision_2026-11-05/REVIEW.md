## What the source adds and where it applies

The original PMC6458679 Methods identify the Finnish Health 2011 six-city reference, age 30–64, n=653, complete cases and 4–17-hour fasting. They specify survey weighting, binary age, serum glucose, and exclusion of diagnosed, registered or treated diabetes plus HbA1c >=6.5% or glucose >=7 mmol/L. The source therefore differs materially from the E01 equal-cell Mexico design. The original Methods also say glucose analyses adjust fasting time; the supplement omits that coefficient/coding. These details are retained accurately in the proposal.

I verified the supplement's continuous-BMI general-population coefficients, 0.0125 for HbA1c and 0.0240 for glucose, against the admitted visible text. Main Table 3 supplies the corresponding nested R-squared pairs. Outcome units and coefficient SE/CIs are not printed in the supplement. Intercepts and measurement context make percent and mmol/L reasonable working interpretations; the proposal labels them as interpretations and preserves the printed values separately. It correctly avoids deriving precision or individual dispersion from threshold p-values. The supplement and main table are one cohort, not independent replication.

The equation `beta/r = SD(Y residualized on Z)/SD(BMI residualized on Z)` is valid for a common weighted projection, and the partial-correlation identity is valid for conventional nested R-squared when adding exactly BMI. The source's reported methods and complete-case description support the proposed alignment, but do not fully verify its software R-squared convention. The proposal makes that condition explicit. It also expressly bridges the different HbA1c and glucose base adjustments and BMI-residual denominators to one target continuous-age/sex base. That bridge is a transport assumption, not a hidden algebraic simplification.

The original NHANES text supports the retained adjusted age coefficient; Taiwan supports the age-adjusted male contrast only; Kerman supports a raw Pearson correlation and marginal SDs with known diabetes cases included in its low-FBS subgroup. Their stronger uses in the target law remain assumptions and are described as such.

## Substantive change and limits on the interpretation

This is **not provenance-only**. Seven scientific parameters change. The outcome variances conditional on preceding predictors fall to 0.3311976 (glucose) and 0.4461668 (HbA1c) of the baseline variances, and the BMI/glucose coefficients change. At the same time, the positive-correlation signs acquire source support without changing their numerical values. Both facts should appear in the change record.

The main remaining issues are non-blocking for the explicitly limited construction, but have concrete effects:

- Absolute dispersion remains anchored by Kerman's marginal BMI SD promoted to a target residual SD. The new source evidence identifies ratios under assumptions, not absolute conditional outcome variance. Narrower generated distributions may worsen quantiles, thresholds or conditional diagnostics even if source-based coefficient algebra is better aligned.
- Q3 still depends on importing the raw Kerman HbA1c–glucose correlation as a residual correlation and on the common-base transport. It is not newly identified. Neither gamma tails, homoscedasticity nor individual HbA1c variation is recovered from the supplement.
- The retained age/sex coefficients and mean anchors do not reconstruct the Finnish regression equation. Its intercept, full R-squared, sex coefficients and predictive margins are not jointly matched. The proposal acknowledges this; implementation must not reintroduce a stronger exact-source claim.
- Source-compatible descriptive restrictions on target synthetic rows do not recreate source weights, fasting covariates or selection mechanisms. Any supplied source-statistic check must use `semantic_feedback_v1`; unsupported source operators should remain unsupported/non-computable with reasons. Algebraic equality of Q2 to the interpreted Finnish coefficient is not source replay or independent validation.
- Three seeds describe simulation variability around one frozen law. They do not measure coefficient, population-transport or distribution-family uncertainty. The full prespecified diagnostics must be reported, including any deterioration against retired scale/quantile anchors.

The implementation should carry over the proposal's documentation changes, preserve the original source evidence, freeze this exact scientific law before any target evaluation, and honor the one-candidate/three-seed protocol.

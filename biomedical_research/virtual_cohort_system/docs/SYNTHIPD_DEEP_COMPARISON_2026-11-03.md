# SynthIPD: deep method, evidence and implementation comparison

Audit date: 3 November 2026. This is a primary-source reading and static-code inspection, not a reproduction experiment. No generator was executed, no patient rows were acquired or inspected, and no frozen study artifact was changed. The comparison uses the local `manuscript/DRAFT_v0_5.md` as the statement of our completed study.

**Main assessment.** SynthIPD is a substantial, directly relevant predecessor for making analysable biomedical records from published information without training on individual records. Its contribution is a specified survival reconstruction and constrained categorical-covariate optimization method, with simulation studies, real trial applications, and a deterministic error bound for the digitization stage. Our present study addresses a different information problem: constructing a five-variable joint law from heterogeneous external studies and transporting it to a requested cross-sectional population. That difference does not establish superior quality or novelty for our method. SynthIPD offers concrete improvements to our workflow: explicit numerical fitting objectives, source-coherence checks, information ablations, reconstruction ensembles, and controlled simulations that distinguish optimizer error from insufficient information. Direct application of the unmodified method to CHNS/ENSANUT is unsupported; a fair comparison requires a common supported task or an explicitly named adaptation.

## 2. What is actually reconstructed, and from which inputs?

The output is a finite synthetic dataset of observed time `U=min(T,C)`, event indicator, treatment assignment, and categorical subgroup membership. It is one member of a class of datasets compatible with available summaries, not recovery of the original participant identities or a uniquely determined true dataset. The paper explicitly acknowledges this nonuniqueness in Section 3.

| Input or capability | What v2 actually specifies | Consequence for our comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Overall survival curves | Separate treatment/control KM plots, with extractable event drops and censoring marks. | This supplies a far richer outcome constraint than a mean, SD or a few regression slopes. |
| Graphics format | Section 3.2.1 prefers vector graphics, convertible to SVG using tools such as Inkscape; SVG itself is not strictly required. It permits WebPlotDigitizer with IPDfromKM or KM-GPT when vector graphics are unavailable, at lower precision. | “Works only with SVG” is too strong. The special precision claim belongs to the vector route, not every fallback. |
| Risk grid/counts | Shared follow-up grid and arm-level numbers at risk. Appendix A lists the risk table as required for the proposed DIGITIZE route. | Required by the preferred inverse-survival problem, not available in our cross-sectional cases. |
| Event/censor counts within grid intervals | Algorithm 1 conditions on interval event totals `D_iota` and censor totals `C_iota`. The censor table is “preferred” in Appendix A. Section 3.2.1 says missing censor totals make reconstruction less stable and prevent guaranteed count matching; Appendix B notes that the theorem must change. | Risk counts alone determine total removals, not their event/censor split. A reproduction must record which additional counts are genuinely available and how missing counts are handled. |
| Covariate inputs | Subgroup sample sizes by treatment arm, subgroup median survival and HR, with their available CIs. ## 3. Algorithm, constraints and meaning of “training-free”

**DIGITIZE.** Event-drop times, censoring times and KM step ratios are extracted. The algorithm estimates nonnegative integer multiplicities for drops and censor marks, with at-risk counts propagated through each interval. Equation 3 minimizes squared differences in KM step ratios plus a penalty shrinking multiplicities at visible censoring times toward their interval average. When available, the separate event and censor totals are equality constraints. Distinct compatible solutions may remain. This explicitly uses the product-limit algebra of KM, rather than fitting a general neural generator. [Algorithm 1 and Section 3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#S3.SS2.SSS1)

**Cov-Generation.** The reconstructed survival records stay fixed. The algorithm initializes categorical labels with exact subgroup-by-arm sample counts, proposes swaps that preserve the admissible counts, and recomputes subgroup median-survival and HR discrepancies. The losses are maximum relative absolute errors across the point estimates and CI limits supplied as targets. Thus confidence limits enter as additional fitting targets, not merely as permissible ranges. Strict improvement in both losses is accepted. An exploratory move can worsen the median loss if HR loss improves and a probabilistic acceptance test succeeds. With `f=0`, this is randomized local search; the alternative is inspired by simulated annealing. [Algorithm 2, Equations 1–6 and Sections 3.2.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#S3.SS2.SSS2)

Typical recommended settings are `H≈50–100` accepted-update iterations, `R≈500,000–1,000,000` proposal attempts per outer iteration, perturbation percentages within approximately 1–20%, and target losses around 2% for medians and 1% for HRs. N9741 uses `H=100`, `R=10^6`, `f=0` and zero thresholds. Appendix D.3 explores other settings and says several configurations may be run and the best summary fit retained. These are meaningful numerical optimization and candidate-selection operations. “Training-free” does not mean “no fitting,” “no search,” or “no computational cost.”

**Assessment of assumptions.** The paper's introduction says statistical-modeling-assumption freedom follows from absence of training. That implication is not generally valid. Here, avoiding a learned generator does remove that particular modeling layer, and the simulations include non-proportional-hazards cases. However, input coherence, same-population compatibility, observable event/censoring representation, the censoring regularizer, count constraints, the selected solution among nonunique joint assignments, and interpretation of fitted Cox HR summaries all remain consequential choices. Under nonproportional hazards an HR is still a fitted summary, not a constant causal effect. The careful description is “no IPD training and no required parametric event-time model for reconstruction,” with the practical and identification assumptions retained.

The method is not an LLM authoring framework in its formal algorithms. Related applications may use LLMs for evidence extraction, but the reconstructive mechanism here is mathematical digitization plus numerical optimization. That is already a strong precedent for the core no-IPD, published-evidence-to-analysable-records objective.

## 4. Data, validation units and reported quantitative results

### 4.1 Real applications

**N9741.** The application reconstructs two arms of a metastatic colorectal cancer trial: FOLFOX `n=421`, IROX `n=383`. Mayo Clinic supplied the overall PFS curves and subgroup summaries. The method authors state that true IPD were unavailable. Sex-specific curves supplied by Mayo are explicitly reserved for comparison; age `<70` versus `≥70` is a separate reconstruction in Appendix E. These are two categorical views of the same trial, not demonstration of one joint continuous-age-and-sex table. [Sections 2.1 and 4.1; Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#S4.SS1)

Table 4's synthetic and source summaries agree at displayed precision. For males, FOLFOX/IROX median PFS is 8.2/6.5 months and IROX-versus-FOLFOX HR is 1.26 (1.05–1.51); for females, 9.7/6.9 months and HR 1.29 (1.04–1.61). These medians, HRs, CIs and sample counts are construction targets, so their agreement is a fit check. Subgroup event counts are also available in the source table, although the formal Cov-Generation loss/constraint equations do not list a separate subgroup-event-count constraint. Figure 5's comparison-only subgroup curves provide evidence beyond the scalar targets. Appendix E reports median/HR mRAEs below 1% for the age-category example, with comparison-only subgroup curves in Figure 16. Because the N9741 metadata were supplied by Mayo, this is not an exclusively public-literature extraction demonstration.

Table 3 calculates previously unreported subgroup survival probabilities and RMSTs. For example, female FOLFOX patients have reported synthetic 12-month survival 0.36 (0.29–0.44) and 12-month RMST 8.33 months (7.81–8.99). These are useful analytical outputs; the table does not supply corresponding true-IPD values or a formal coverage assessment for these new quantities. The comparison-only subgroup curves support the plausibility of some unprovided functionals, but do not turn every new endpoint into independently validated truth.

**Six multiple-myeloma trials.** ALCYONE, CASSIOPEIA and MAIA concern newly diagnosed MM; CANDOR, CASTOR and POLLUX concern relapsed/refractory MM. Figure 2 arm counts sum to 4,061 participants. Cytogenetic risk is the reconstructed covariate, including unknown status where applicable. Trial inputs come from publications; no true trial IPD are analyzed by the method authors. Table 1 includes HRs, event/sample counts and medians with varying completeness (some medians are not estimable or absent). The methods' real-world handling of all unavailable medians/CI terms is not fully specified in executable form. Initial and updated reports must not be mixed silently: Figure 4's CANDOR worked example cites updated 2022 results, whereas the six-trial source listing includes the 2020 CANDOR report. [Sections 2.2, 3.2 Example 1, 4.2 and data availability statement](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#S4.SS2)

The pooled high-risk HRs are 0.76 (0.53–1.10) for newly diagnosed MM and 0.43 (0.29–0.61) for relapsed/refractory MM. The cited random-effects meta-analysis gives 0.76 (0.53–1.10) and 0.43 (0.29–0.62). This is useful agreement between aggregate and reconstructed-data analyses, but is not independent patient-level validation because individual trial HRs informed reconstruction. Table 5 supplies pooled daratumumab median-PFS benchmarks; for relapsed/refractory high-risk patients (`n=123`), 16.9 months (10.2–23.7). Transporting those pooled values to a new control population requires additional assumptions about eligibility, treatments, follow-up, calendar period and case mix; reconstruction alone does not establish that transport.

### 4.2 Simulations: three different evaluations

**Digitization comparison.** Appendix D.1 evaluates one generated `n=500` dataset. Table 8 reports true treatment/control medians 22.55/3.55 months; DIGITIZE reproduces both displayed point estimates, while IPDfromKM gives 22.83/3.95. Counts are 121/227 and 224/273 for truth and DIGITIZE, versus 119/227 and 220/273 for IPDfromKM. The control-median discrepancy is approximately 11% in this example. The paper also reports broader experiential ranges of 5–20% maximum mRAEs for IPDfromKM and below 1% for DIGITIZE, but Table 8 itself is a single example. It does not separate the mathematical reconstruction algorithm from the superior information obtained through vector extraction. A fair algorithm-only benchmark must match the digitized inputs; a fair end-to-end benchmark may intentionally compare extraction routes. [Appendix D.1, Figure 6 and Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#A4.SS1)

**Covariate reconstruction.** Appendix D.2 explicitly assumes perfect digitization: true observed time, event status and treatment arm are supplied unchanged, and only covariates are regenerated. Two binary covariates are independently generated in three cases: Cox proportional hazards; log-normal AFT; and a time-varying hazard scenario with crossing arm curves. Censoring is stated to be 30–50%. Table 6 summarizes marginal and joint-stratum reconstructions. Selected means (SDs):

| Scenario / reconstruction | NAUC | KS | Median mRAE | HR mRAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cox / first covariate | 0.018 (0.0042) | 0.062 (0.017) | 0.0051 (0.0047) | 0.00052 (0.0011) |
| Cox / joint covariates | 0.022 (0.0054) | 0.072 (0.015) | 0.017 (0.0054) | 0.0020 (0.00077) |
| AFT / joint covariates | 0.016 (0.0047) | 0.069 (0.012) | 0.021 (0.0045) | 0.0018 (0.00094) |
| Crossing hazards / joint covariates | 0.018 (0.0066) | 0.061 (0.013) | 0.014 (0.0037) | 0.0033 (0.0019) |

Across the nine Table 6 rows, NAUC means range 0.0099–0.022, KS 0.053–0.072, median loss 0.0051–0.028 and HR loss 0.00052–0.0042. These are strong small-summary-loss results under favorable survival inputs; they should not be summarized as universal “below 1% error.” KS refers to a maximum vertical gap within a subgroup curve, subsequently aggregated across subgroups; it is not an average probability error. The appendix also reports tail KS deviations around 0.30 in some runs for a small subgroup. [Section 5/Table 6 and Appendix D.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#A4.SS2)

**Harder eight-stratum optimization.** Appendix D.3 uses `n=1,200`, three binary covariates, treatment interactions and approximately 73% censoring. Table 9 has 96 replications. At one million cumulative proposals, the smallest perturbations (1–5%) give median mRAE 0.150 (SD 0.034) and HR mRAE 0.068 (0.025); the largest (20–50%) give 0.213 (0.032) and 0.119 (0.047). The SA comparison reports fewer proposals for reachable error thresholds, but none of 96 runs in either setting reaches below 0.06. Figure 9's mean time-to-threshold is conditional on reaching that threshold; failures are separately reported. These results establish sensitivity and a meaningful difficulty boundary rather than uniform near-exact multivariate reconstruction. [Appendix D.3/Table 9/Figure 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#A4.SS3)

### 4.3 Use as training data for other generators

Appendix D.4 evaluates five downstream generators trained on original simulated IPD, SynthIPD alone, or their combination. It uses the same `n=1,200` setting and one selected reconstruction with median mRAE 0.06 and HR mRAE 0.11. The reference C-index is 0.6219. Table 11 reports absolute C-index discrepancies:

| Downstream generator | Original-IPD training | SynthIPD training | Combined training |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| survivalGAN | 0.0027 | 0.0118 | 0.0005 |
| CTGAN | 0.0083 | 0.0041 | 0.0040 |
| TVAE | 0.0064 | 0.0060 | 0.0006 |
| SurvDiff | 0.0066 | 0.0088 | 0.0045 |
| Conditional tree | 0.0115 | 0.0141 | 0.0234 |

Combined training is closest for four models and worse for the conditional tree. This supports a bounded pseudo-training-data use case. It is not a five-method competition in which all methods receive the same published-summary-only input, nor a verified external prediction experiment on a new trial. A close C-index is one scalar property and does not establish calibration, full joint fidelity or the validity of every coefficient. Repeated training uncertainty and an untouched test-cohort evaluation are not established by Table 11. [Appendix D.4/Tables 10–11](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#A4.SS4)

## 5. Which downstream quantities are genuinely additional tests?

| Quantity | Relation to reconstruction inputs | Evidential interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Arm-level KM curve | Direct digitization target. | Reconstruction fidelity. |
| Arm-level survival at a time or RMST at a truncation | Numerically determined by the supplied arm-level KM curve over the observed follow-up. | An unreported calculation can be useful, but it is not new independent information or a strong held-out validation target. |
| Subgroup median, HR and supplied CI limits | Explicit covariate-fit targets. | Good fitted-summary agreement; do not count again as independent validation. |
| Full subgroup KM curve when only overall curves and subgroup scalars are input | Not uniquely determined by those inputs. | A meaningful additional functional check when a curve is withheld from construction, as described for Mayo's sex/age curves. Still a same-trial comparison, not transport validation. |
| Subgroup RMST or fixed-time survival | Depends on the reconstructed subgroup curve; not uniquely fixed by a subgroup median and HR. | An additional model implication. It becomes a checked endpoint only if compared with withheld source truth; Table 3 mainly supplies estimates. |
| Pooled HR across MM trials | Built from source datasets constrained by trial HRs. | Aggregation-consistency evidence, not independent ground-truth confirmation. |
| C-index after training another generator | Not a stated covariate-fit objective. | Additional functional evidence in the selected simulation, with limited replication and evaluation detail. |
| A coefficient in a different external target population | Not directly evaluated by SynthIPD's same-population reconstructions. | Requires transport assumptions and separate target evaluation. This is where our present study operates, with its own development-exposure limitations. |

This classification is central to a fair comparison. It is wrong both to describe all reported accuracy as independent validation and to dismiss every result as tautological fitting.

## 6. Uncertainty and unresolved reproduction details

**What the paper establishes.** Appendix B gives a finite-sample deterministic bound for DIGITIZE's reconstructed survival curve. It separates the best achievable discrepancy in the admissible reconstruction class from errors in extracted KM step ratios. It controls a uniform curve difference and normalized integrated absolute curve difference under its assumptions, including known interval censor/event counts and correctly observed unique censoring positions. Appendix D.2 supplies across-reconstruction percentile bands for subgroup curves.

**What is not established.** This is not a confidence interval for the entire reconstructed joint distribution. It does not propagate all uncertainty in unknown covariate assignments, published source estimates, study selection, transport, new-cohort analyses or pooling. Fitting reported confidence limits does not automatically propagate source uncertainty. Repeated optimized solutions are not automatically posterior draws or a valid multiple-imputation distribution. The paper's partial-identification acknowledgment in Section 3 is important and should be retained when interpreting its stronger downstream language.

The following details must be resolved or explicitly selected before a faithful computational reproduction. They do not invalidate all findings, but they prevent silently claiming exact replication from the prose alone:

1. **Replications:** Section 5 says each of 100 runs generates independent `n=500` IPD; Appendix D.2 says Cov-Generation is run 100 times using the same survival input and applies that description to Section 5 and D.3. PDF text confirms both statements. Conditional optimizer variability and repeated-dataset sampling variability are different quantities.
2. **Stopping settings:** Appendix D.2 writes arguments ending in `0.02,0`, then says `S_m=0`. Under Algorithm 2's conjunction, a zero threshold with strict `<` already prevents early stopping because losses are nonnegative. A reproduction must choose and document the intended setting, including what happens when no proposal is accepted within `R`.
3. **Missing/non-estimable summaries:** Tables include absent or non-estimable medians/CIs. The finite-target inclusion rules, tie conventions and software-specific median/CI calculations need specification; one cannot divide by infinity or an absent target as if it were an ordinary estimate.
4. **SA/global guarantees:** The algorithm allows a worse median loss, so the Section 3.3 assertion that median loss necessarily decreases across outer iterations does not follow in all accepted paths. A two-objective common minimum also need not exist in general. The paper gives an optimization heuristic and calibration analysis, not a demonstrated global optimum for all problems.
5. **Loss range:** Equations 1–2 annotate relative absolute error as belonging to `[0,1]`, but an untruncated relative error can exceed 1. Near-zero targets also make relative loss unstable. The executable scoring rule should use the equation, with any clipping separately declared.
6. **Metric counting:** Appendix D states categories as `0,...,K` but writes `2K` in the subgroup-average KS normalization. Implementations must resolve the number-of-levels convention rather than reproduce an indexing ambiguity.
7. **Hard simulation censoring:** Appendix D.3 says an exponential censoring distribution has mean 0.012 while also describing a 36-month administrative limit and approximately 73% censoring. The rate-versus-mean convention needs checking; no correction is assumed here.
8. **Unavailable core code:** No dedicated author study implementation or runnable reproduction package was located in the paper, author pages, or GitHub repository-name search. Table 10 gives a dash for SynthIPD's code source. This is a search/access finding, not proof that no code exists anywhere.

## 7. Static inspection of the available author demonstration

The first author's website loads `js/viz-synthipd.js`. Its header explicitly describes a live simulated trial and an illustrative pipeline. Static inspection found:

- Simulated two-arm Weibull survival with dropout and administrative censoring; no real trial records.
- The “SVG” branch copies step coordinates already computed in memory. It does not parse a published SVG or PDF.
- The comparison branch constructs synthetic manual-click errors with hard-coded noise, timing and missed-step distributions. It is not an invocation of the R IPDfromKM package.
- `reconstruct` is a Guyot-style interval inversion with rounded events and evenly placed within-interval censoring. It does not implement Equation 3's joint integer optimization with the censoring penalty.
- `attachCovariates` matches fixed baseline means/SDs and binary proportions using chosen latent rank associations. It does not minimize the paper's subgroup median/HR/CI losses. The age mean/SD example therefore demonstrates illustrative moment matching, not a validated continuous-covariate extension of the paper.
- The resolution-sweep display fixes the SVG reference error to `0.0001` and smooths/constrains the synthetic manual-error series to be non-increasing with resolution. Those curves are pedagogical outputs, not an empirical benchmark of the paper algorithms.

These observations concern only the [website file at the inspected commit](https://github.com/ananke1999/zixuan_site/blob/aa933c5c486a09ad687e60a0323e7e54491544eb/js/viz-synthipd.js). They are not allegations about the unreleased study implementation or evidence that the paper's table results were produced by this demo. A future reproduction must not cite execution of the demo as replication of SynthIPD.

## 8. Comparison with our actual v0.5 study

| Dimension | SynthIPD v2 | Our frozen v0.5 study | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scientific problem | Reverse same-trial survival summaries into compatible finite records. | Construct a complete cross-sectional joint law from external biomedical evidence for a requested target population and analyses. | Related inverse problems with different identifying information. |
| Individual inputs | No true IPD for the real reconstruction applications; simulations use known truth for evaluation, and some bypass digitization. | No individual-level construction records or reference coefficients supplied to authors; existing target references were exposed during earlier development. | Both separate construction from inaccessible individual records; neither comparison warrants claiming untouched external validation. |
| Variables | Survival/event/treatment plus discrete covariates; joint extension needs stratum summaries. | Age, sex, BMI, glucose, HbA1c with continuous joint laws. | Our output scope is broader in variable type, but broader scope is not demonstrated superior fidelity. |
| Evidence compatibility | Same-population requirement, reporting coherence. | Heterogeneous sources with declared transport/context approximations. | Our central added difficulty is transport and incompatible/incomplete operators. |
| Numerical mechanism | Specified constrained optimizer and loss functions. | Agent chooses law, parameters and completion assumptions; numerical software samples it. Fixed statistical constructions supply comparators. | SynthIPD is more tightly specified as an algorithm. Our scientific choices are inspectable, but no unique learning algorithm is claimed. |
| Evidence use | Published medians/HRs/CIs actively optimized. | Constraints, analytic checks, contextual uses and declined operations are recorded; not all admitted statistics are jointly fitted. | We should quantify actual constraint satisfaction rather than equate declared uptake with fit. |
| Evaluation breadth | Real same-trial reconstructions, multiple simulated laws, harder strata, optimizer sensitivity, downstream generator example. | Two exposed development reference families, six prespecified coefficients plus distribution/joint diagnostics, all constructors/seeds retained. | SynthIPD has a substantially richer mechanism-focused simulation evaluation. Our audit/retention protocol is useful but does not substitute for that evidence. |
| Empirical quality | Often excellent fitted-summary agreement; curve and hard-stratum limitations remain. | E1 improves average discrepancy on five of six coefficients but worsens CHNS BMI, leaves one negative conditional-age construction, and systematic ENSANUT magnitude differences. | No cross-paper leaderboard is justified by incompatible endpoints and inputs. |
| Comparators | IPDfromKM on digitization; downstream generators under three training-data scenarios. | One fixed statistical construction per condition versus five Agent authors; two endpoints favor every corresponding statistical E1 construction over Agents. | Neither study establishes general superiority over the other. |
| Revision evidence | Explicit repeated numerical search, multiple hyperparameters, candidate selection by known fit loss. | Zero feedback-driven scientific revisions in the main study. | We cannot present our study as evidence that iterative refinement improves cohorts. |
| Uncertainty | DIGITIZE-specific deterministic bound; reconstruction variability. | Three seeds per fixed law; reference intervals/bands; no full source/structural/transport uncertainty. | Both leave important downstream uncertainty open, for different reasons. |
| Reproducibility | Detailed pseudocode; no verified full author code release located; toy author demo is not the method. | Original code, specifications, frozen aggregates and independent recomputation are present; selected public artifacts are not a complete portable runtime. | An actual shared-input reproducibility package would strengthen either contribution. |

Our v0.5 figures should remain in their original units. For example, CHNS glucose Agent E1 discrepancy is 0.024079 HbA1c percentage points per mmol/L, while its prescribed statistical E1 comparator has 0.012021. ENSANUT conditional BMI has Agent E1 0.025649 versus statistical E1 0.007940 percentage points per 5 kg/m². SynthIPD's HR mRAE and our regression absolute discrepancy are not commensurate, and dividing by a tiny conditional-age target would create an unstable artificial ranking.

## 9. Concrete improvements worth borrowing

These are proposed extensions, not new experiments or validated capabilities of our current study.

1. **Compile evidence operations into explicit numerical targets.** Keep population, measurement, adjustment and operator metadata, then give every accepted numerical relation an implemented model-implied calculation and residual. Distinguish exact structural/count constraints from uncertain reported estimates. For our heterogeneous sources, place each source operator in its declared source-context approximation; do not force every external coefficient to hold exactly in the target cohort.
2. **Use an explicit optimizer after law selection.** Let the Agent propose interpretable families and completion assumptions; let a numerical solver fit admitted quantities under those choices. Preserve the fixed-law statistical comparator. Compare law selection, calibration and their combination separately. This borrows SynthIPD's most useful separation without claiming that generic optimization is novel.
3. **Add a compatibility/feasibility stage.** Check whether all operator constraints can be satisfied together, and identify minimal conflicting subsets. Incompatible evidence should cause documented slack, a changed source-context assumption or refusal of the requested inference. Exact fitting of mutually incompatible populations is not a success.
4. **Use independent information conditions.** Separate reporting precision, uncertainty information, relational information, joint-stratum information and source heterogeneity. SynthIPD's strong curve inputs illustrate why “summary-only” is not a uniform information budget.
5. **Report sets of compatible laws.** Retain diverse solutions within a declared fit tolerance and calculate the range of the requested coefficient across them. Targeted minimum/maximum searches are more informative about identification than random seeds from one chosen law. Treat these as sensitivity or partial-identification ranges unless a statistical coverage argument is supplied.
6. **Build nested uncertainty evaluation.** Separate source-estimate perturbation, structural/completion choice, transport choice, optimizer randomness and finite-row Monte Carlo error. Neither CI matching nor increasing row count addresses all five.
7. **Add controlled simulations with known truth.** Simulate source and target populations with declared measurement shifts, selection and omitted dependence; publish only the prescribed evidence to each constructor; retain true target analyses privately. Include conditional coefficients not algebraically fixed by the supplied summaries. This can determine whether a failure comes from numerical optimization, nonidentification or wrong transport.
8. **Predeclare failure and selection rules.** Retain unsuccessful optimizer runs and finite-budget censoring of time-to-threshold. If selecting best-fit laws, selection must use only construction-visible residuals; final target answers stay unavailable. Report compute per task, source/extraction effort, model reasoning and calibration separately.

## 10. Meaningful reproduction and a fair comparison design

**A. Reproduce the supported survival method first.** Obtain the study implementation or implement the published algorithms independently, resolving the ambiguities in Section 6. Start with purely simulated, same-population data and categorical covariates. Give the reconstructor only the generated KM/risk/censor summaries and subgroup targets. A separate evaluator keeps original simulated records. Run both a perfect-digitization condition and a genuinely end-to-end graphics condition. State “independent reimplementation” unless the authors' released code and configuration are actually used. No such execution occurred in this audit.

**B. Compare constructors on a common survival task.** Give SynthIPD, an Agent-authored generator and a fixed statistical method exactly the same curves, counts, subgroup summaries, target sample size and allowed computation. Report supplied-summary residuals separately from withheld subgroup curves, restricted mean survival, time-specific survival, interactions and other unprovided functionals. Arm-level RMST alone is too weak as a held-out endpoint because the arm-level curve already supplies it. Include correlated covariates, sparse joint strata, absent CIs, raster/rounding noise and incompatible summaries. Respect each method's stated supported scope; inability to run on unsupported data is a scope result, not an accuracy score.

**C. Compare external-evidence transport as a separate task.** To test our claimed use case, generate or choose distinct source and target populations with controlled differences. Unmodified SynthIPD has no transport mechanism. A comparison must therefore name a transport-augmented SynthIPD-like method, spell out its additional assumptions, and give all methods the same evidence. Report same-source reconstruction and target-population agreement separately. Do not call a new adaptation “SynthIPD as published.”

**D. For the existing CHNS/ENSANUT study, use an adapted constraint baseline rather than a forced port.** A SynthIPD-inspired row- or distribution-level optimizer could fit precisely the admitted external continuous-variable operators under explicit completion assumptions. That would be a new baseline against our existing fixed statistical constructions and Agent laws, not a direct reproduction of SynthIPD. Because the existing references were exposed during development, any new improvement tuned to them should remain development evidence and be followed by a newly frozen, independently held-out task.

The credible scientific advance is therefore testable: improve the handling of heterogeneous biomedical evidence, show which new target analyses are identified or stable, and demonstrate those claims against transparent numerical baselines. SynthIPD is valuable both as a predecessor and as a source of stronger methodology and evaluation requirements.

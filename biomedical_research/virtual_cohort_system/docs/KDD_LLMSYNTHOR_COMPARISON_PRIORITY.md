## Biomedical comparison in the manuscript

Filshtein et al. used source-specific fitted models to simulate data for epidemiological analysis. Our HRS comparison uses their published HRS model exports, an Agent-authored generator, a reconstruction of their recipient-stage law and the same longitudinal memory model. For the age interaction with the HbA1c spline below 6.5%, the supplied coefficient was −0.04684, the Agent mean was −0.04464 and the reconstructed-law mean was −0.04050. Above 6.5%, the corresponding values were +0.01287, +0.02649 and −0.01169. The means summarize three draws per construction. This case tests preservation of a supplied model. The separate ENSANUT and Turkana analyses test construction from more limited external evidence. [Filshtein et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8338788/) · [HRS results](../reports/hrs_public_example_2026-11-03/RESULTS.md)

Two biomedical methods clarify the input boundary. SynthIPD reconstructs survival data and categorical covariates from published trial material. A direct comparison would require a common survival analysis. SynDI combines internal individual observations with external regression information across populations. It is relevant when individual data are available and the target is a regression estimate. These methods belong in the positioning discussion for their respective settings. [SynthIPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2#S3.SS2) · [Gu et al., Biometrics](https://doi.org/10.1111/biom.13852)

## Published reference

LLMSynthor Table 2 reports a category–price joint Jensen–Shannon divergence of 0.134 on a simulated e-commerce benchmark. The authors built the input marginals and joint tables from a reference sample of 2,000 transactions, including the category–price relationship itself. Table 3 shows that this score changes with the backbone. These are author-reported results for recovering supplied joint statistics in that benchmark. [LLMSynthor §4.2, Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14752v4#S4.SS2)

If a future study asks about generalization to e-commerce, a numerical comparison would require matching the scored reference distribution or an equivalent evaluator (an exact histogram can suffice for this divergence), the population and sample sizes, macro tables, bin edges and sub-bin access, validity filters, metric definition including the aggregation convention, and repetition/averaging policy. Identify the backbone and report any difference. Label the two rows “reported by the authors” and “run in this study”. A different reference realization defines a separate benchmark with its own scores.

## What belongs to each implementation

The published LLMSynthor selects dependencies, can suggest missing joint summaries, plans proposals, samples records, and uses cumulative discrepancy feedback. A biomedical example makes the additional design choice visible. If one paper gives an adjusted BMI coefficient and another gives a biomarker margin, converting these into a target-population contingency table requires assumptions about transport and the joint distribution. The proposed LLMSynthor-E intake would supply that reasoning on the comparator side. Its inclusion could help or hurt, and an experiment with it would evaluate the combined workflow. [Published method §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14752v4#S3.SS2)

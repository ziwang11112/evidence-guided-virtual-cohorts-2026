# C4: simplify revision selection; preserve the evidence boundary

31 October 2026. This unit is an explicitly artificial mechanism experiment, not a new biomedical validation. The overall project remains evidence-guided virtual data for scientific analysis, including complete-cohort generation without individual input. C3 and all revealed clinical targets remained unchanged.

## Main decision

This pilot provides **no evidence of incremental benefit from the protected-use selector**. The scientific Agent retained every initial generator after reviewing source feedback. Consequently, retaining the initial model, minimizing source discrepancy and applying the new protected-use rule all selected identical scientific models. This nondiscriminating outcome was recorded before election and hidden evaluation; the subsequent identification counterexample must not be presented as selector success.

The practical recommendation is to keep the generator and evidence workflow simple. Further development of this selection component needs a genuinely discriminating candidate pool and independent analytical benefit, not more wrapper components or repeated scores from identical models.

## What actually ran

An independent custodian committed two invented Gaussian three-measurement tasks. Task A supplied the three means, SDs and all three pairwise correlations. Task B supplied the same univariate information and only two pairwise relationships; two different full Gaussian laws were committed that both satisfy its visible evidence. The hidden analytical operators and answers were fixed before a fresh scientific Agent authored its models.

The Agent used an i.i.d. Gaussian construction. Task A used its fully specified joint law. For Task B, the Agent explicitly assumed conditional independence of X and Z given Y, deriving the otherwise unknown X–Z correlation as 0.18. This was an acknowledged scenario, not an observed or identified relationship; Task A's extra relation was not transferred as Task B evidence.

Six initial-feedback tables passed every source check. The Agent found no basis for changing exact source parameters to follow finite-draw noise, so its three proposal opportunities per task were all truthful retentions. Generator, model specification and evidence bytes stayed identical within each task. The four labels per task therefore represent one scientific model, not four independent constructions.

All planned stages were still completed:

- Six initial feedback executions, 24 election executions and24 untouched-test executions: 54 in total, with no failed executions or retries.
- Each table had 4,000 fictional rows. The 54 files contain 18 unique table contents. The 24 final files contain only six unique contents—two tasks by three seeds—because retained labels reproduce identical output.
- All 459 source-statistic entries passed their declared engineering bands. These bands are not clinical equivalence limits or source confidence intervals.
- Policies used the same frozen candidate pool, scores, seeds and tie order. Selection was sealed before any untouched-test execution; the hidden evaluator was opened only after all 24 final output commitments existed.
- No real target, source paper search, paid API, model training, HPC job or scheduler change was involved. This fixture uses constructed population moments, not published patient measurements.

## Source-unreported analysis

The precommitted primary analysis was the coefficient of X in the regression of Y on X and Z. None of its target values or errors entered generation feedback or policy selection. All values below are in invented standardized units, and the mean and mean absolute error summarize three unique simulation draws—not independent studies.

| Task/reference law | Constructed coefficient | Mean generated estimate | Mean absolute error | Direction agreement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: identified joint law | 0.187500 | 0.181531 | 0.007981 | 3/3 |
| B: compatible law 1 | 0.242424 | 0.192380 | 0.050044 | 3/3 |
| B: compatible law 2 | -0.235294 | 0.192380 | 0.427674 | 0/3 |

All three policies have the same entries because their selected data are identical. Secondary adjusted-Z and partial-correlation results, all candidate labels and every draw remain in the complete aggregate report.

Task A is an arithmetic and analytical-use check under fully identifying Gaussian inputs. Its unprovided regression coefficient is mathematically implied by the admitted joint moments; reproducing it is not independent discovery of unknown biology.

Task B is a deliberately constructed identification counterexample. Its two compatible worlds share every supplied source statistic yet imply different, opposing adjusted-X effects. There is no evidence-based probability weight or preferred world. The one generated scenario cannot establish the hidden adjusted relationship in both worlds, even while all supplied correlations are well reproduced. This result does not estimate how often such failures occur in clinical practice and is not a new biological finding.

## Consequence for the data-generation method

The current Agent can produce a coherent complete virtual cohort and can rationally retain it when feedback provides no reason to revise. The present experiment does not show that adding a selector improves that behavior. A source-consistent cohort can remain useful for the particular relations it is constrained to represent while other uses depend on unverified joint assumptions.

The next useful real-evidence question is therefore whether the Agent can identify and obtain the **joint or conditional evidence needed for a specified downstream analysis**, integrate it without confusing populations or adjustment sets, and generate data that support that analysis on an independent reference. If such evidence is unavailable, multiple explicitly assumed cohorts and a sensitivity analysis may be more honest than presenting a single scenario as recovered truth. These are proposed next steps, not improvements demonstrated by C4.

This keeps the project centered on usable virtual data. It does not require a new loss, multi-Agent superiority, or proving that every synthetic individual equals a measured participant. It does require matching the strength of the analytical claim to the information actually supplied.

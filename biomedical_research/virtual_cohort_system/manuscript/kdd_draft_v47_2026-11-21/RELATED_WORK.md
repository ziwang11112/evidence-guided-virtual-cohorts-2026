# Closest work on generating cohorts for scientific analysis

Targeted comparison for the KDD manuscript, checked 21 November 2026. The search covered patient agents built without individual records, statistical reconstruction from published or site-level summaries, and studies that repeated a biomedical analysis on synthetic records. We checked inputs and evaluation against the authors' methods, proceedings, supplements or protocol. Eight additional published papers and one ongoing proposal enter the bibliography.

## What the closest studies actually tested

| Work | What was available during construction | What was evaluated and where it overlaps |
|---|---|---|
| Lai et al., Patient-Zero, EMNLP 2026 | Medical knowledge, stratified attribute sampling, consistency rules and semantic checks produce structured patients without real patient records as seeds. A memory system supports dialogue. | The authors compare selected epidemiological distributions, physician ratings, simulated interactions and downstream medical reasoning. Real-record-free generation and clinical checks are already demonstrated. Our outcome is an adjusted biomedical association or screening proportion after a researcher substitutes the virtual records. [Paper, especially §§4–5 and Appendix D](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1149/) |
| Jiang et al., KMGen, MLHC 2026 | An agent extracts published Kaplan–Meier curves. A language model translates public trial records into trial-arm configurations. A mechanistic sampler couples risk profiles to survival and generates adverse events. | Three oncology trial comparisons and 30 regenerations per trial evaluate survival curves, demographic distributions and adverse-event recovery against patient-level references. This is a close precedent for an auditable agent and patient-record pipeline. In our original-paper tasks, the agent also chooses the mathematical relationship between continuous measurements and implements it. [Proceedings and paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v340/jiang26a.html) |
| Zhao et al., SynthIPD, arXiv v2 | Published survival curves and subgroup summaries constrain survival records and covariate assignment without training on participant records. | The N9741 example compares reconstruction with actual trial data. A separate application pools six myeloma trials for subgroup survival analysis. This already establishes that report-derived records can enable further analyses. Our iron and lipid experiments ask which results change under alternative pairings that preserve the available summaries. [Methods and both case studies](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16466v2) |
| Bonofiglio et al., 2020, and Papadimitropoulou et al., 2020 | The first takes empirical marginal moments and correlation matrices from data holders. The second uses trial-arm sample sizes, baseline and follow-up means and SDs, and their correlation for continuous-outcome analyses. | The first fits a multivariable random-effects logistic regression after Gaussian-copula reconstruction. The second shows that a specified linear ANCOVA can reproduce the individual-data estimate and standard error given the requisite summaries. Both are important precedents for analysis on reconstructed data. They also explain why an estimate already fixed by the supplied statistics provides limited evidence about agent judgment. [Gaussian-copula study](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8470) · [Pseudo-IPD ANCOVA study, Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7754323/) |
| Allen et al., 2016, and Fasseeh et al., 2025 | Allen et al. select virtual patients from a mechanistic lipid model using an empirical NHANES joint distribution. Fasseeh et al. use specified distributions, a correlation matrix and logical constraints. | The former calibrates virtual lipid measurements against observed joint data. The latter compares five correlation-information scenarios on NHANES and Framingham data. For a direct comparison with our lipid task, access to the target correlation would change the inputs. The informative control already present in our study gives both constructors the same summaries and reports the resulting screening proportion. [Lipid virtual population](https://doi.org/10.1002/psp4.12063) · [Five correlation scenarios](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4893/18/8/475) |
| Snoke et al., 2018, and Azizi et al., 2021 | Snoke et al. formalize utility for a specified analysis. Azizi et al. start with the control-arm records of 1,543 colon-cancer trial participants and use partial synthesis. | Azizi et al. rerun the published survival analysis. Overall-survival hazard ratios were 1.56 on measured records and 2.03 on synthetic records. Their validation shows why effect direction and magnitude deserve separate attention. Our Kenyan comparison uses this evaluation principle with the original individual records withheld from the constructors. [Specific utility](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssa.12358) · [Clinical replication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8055130/) · [Synthesis details](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/bmjopen/11/4/e043497/DC1/embed/inline-supplementary-material-1.pdf) |
| Decruyenaere et al., Scientific Reports 2026 | The benchmark reference was reconstructed from published overall-survival curves. Sixteen generators were fitted to the resulting arm, survival-time and event-indicator records. | Across 128 treatment comparisons from 115 phase III oncology trials, naive uncertainty estimates could produce false significance. This is evidence about inference at scale. The Kenyan and CHNS references in our study come from measured participants and allow direct examination of an agent-chosen within-person relationship. [Article and author-held full text, Methods](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/01M6G8J436EZSEN00FA6R64GGR) |
| Tao, YODA proposal 2026-0704, **ongoing protocol** | The proposal compares reconstruction from published marginals with a second strategy that estimates additional covariate dependence from external individual data. | It plans to compare transported treatment effects against the same estimators applied using original trial records. This anticipates the comparison of marginal and joint information. The registry lists an ongoing study and supplies no benchmark results. Its endpoint is a transported treatment effect. Our experiments measure associations in target participants and change individual pairings in frozen generators. [Public research proposal and status](https://yoda.yale.edu/data-request/2026-0704/) |

G-Sim and LLMSynthor remain relevant to how the generator is built. G-Sim proposes simulator structures and estimates parameters against observations. LLMSynthor generates micro-records under marginal and joint aggregate constraints and evaluates mobility, e-commerce and population datasets. Neither paper's published performance numbers measure our outcomes in kg/m², mg/dL, rank correlation or additional people screened. A numerical ranking across those papers and ours would therefore have no common endpoint. [G-Sim, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/holt25b.html) · [LLMSynthor, KDD 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3770855.3818965) · [LLMSynthor methods and experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14752v3)

## What the KDD paper can test directly

| Scientific question | Evidence in this project | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Can a researcher repeat an adjusted analysis using a table built from publications? | Two Turkana generators, six draws, the same implemented regression on virtual and measured participants, and matched-size real–real resampling. [Protocol and replay](../../work/kdd_v45_build_2026-11-21/ANALYSIS_REPLAY.md) | The urban BMI contrast was recovered at approximately the measured scale. The cholesterol contrast was smaller in mg/dL. The original lifestyle results were available during construction. |
| Does the agent's within-person pairing change an analysis beyond what the source table supplies? | In a retrospective diagnostic on six initial CHNS constructions, fixing both columns and removing within-band pairing increased continuous rank error in all six, by a median of 0.0612. The threshold odds ratio stayed fixed. The conventional within-band independence model also met the two prespecified use margins. [Matched comparison](../../work/chns_statistical_comparator_2026-11-19/RESULTS.md) | This attributes a specific effect to pairing on the archived records. The original test of incremental gain from an extra source statistic did not meet its criterion. [Preregistered community comparison](../../work/chns_iron_conditional_holdout_2026-11-19/RESULTS.md) |
| What if equally admissible pairings lead to different scientific decisions? | Four lipid authors each constructed two alternatives with identical component values. Their screening rates differed by 16.37–29.35 percentage points within author while the mean non-HDL stayed fixed. [Study and checks](../../work/kdd_v40_pairing_review_2026-11-20/REVIEW.md) | The spread gives attainable examples under the reported constraints. Estimating which pairings occur in patients requires joint measurements. Target-population measurements provide a separate check on a selected law. |

The comparison supports a specific paper claim. Given a published source and a requested analysis, we can trace an agent's pairing decision into an effect estimate, compare that estimate with independently evaluated measurements, and identify another use of the same records that is insensitive to the decision. The clinical community holdout also used newly tabulated source-community summaries. This distinction matters when describing access requirements. The statistical comparisons reported above already operate with common inputs. A head-to-head with a survival or consultation system would answer a different scientific question and would need a common task and admissible inputs of its own.

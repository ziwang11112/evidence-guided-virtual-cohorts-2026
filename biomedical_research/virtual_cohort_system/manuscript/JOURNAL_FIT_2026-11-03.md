# Journal fit for the frozen evidence-guided virtual-data study

Assessment date and web-access date: 3 November 2026. This is a venue and manuscript-readiness assessment, not an acceptance prediction. No manuscript was submitted, journal contacted, new dataset acquired, or experiment run for this assessment.

**Recommendation: prepare for PLOS Digital Health first, retain Journal of Biomedical Informatics as a conditional methodological alternative, and consider a tightly focused JAMIA Brief Communication if the final contribution remains formative.** These are plausible destinations for the present kind of work after manuscript completion; none is a safe acceptance assumption. Artificial Intelligence in Medicine is less comfortable for the current contribution than its name suggests. npj Digital Medicine and Nature Communications are future ambition paths, not the recommended first submissions for the frozen evidence package.

The distinction matters: **scope fit** means the journal publishes this subject; **submission readiness** means a complete manuscript makes a defensible contribution against the journal's criteria; **possible acceptance** remains an editorial and peer-review judgment that this assessment cannot estimate numerically.

## Evidence on which the assessment rests

The local scientific sources were [manuscript v0.4](DRAFT_v0_4.md), [completed main-study results](https://github.com/ziwang11112/evidence-guided-virtual-cohorts-2026/blob/26ccdfb5a77c665ddce1d1c0c73ce991b7107fb3/biomedical_research/virtual_cohort_system/reports/main_study_freeze_2026-11-03/RESULTS.md), and [the claim lock](https://github.com/ziwang11112/evidence-guided-virtual-cohorts-2026/blob/26ccdfb5a77c665ddce1d1c0c73ce991b7107fb3/biomedical_research/virtual_cohort_system/reports/main_study_freeze_2026-11-03/CLAIM_LOCK.json). The contribution is an executable, study-qualified route from curated biomedical evidence and explicit completion assumptions to an Agent-designed probability law and virtual tables, with analysis-specific evaluation. It is not a new foundation model, a proven superior generative algorithm, or a substitute for patient measurements.

The study has material strengths: 20 Agent constructions and four statistical constructions; all 72 distinct final tables completed; all 2,196 evaluated statistics independently reproduced; explicit numerical evidence-use records; preservation of unfavorable endpoints and historical failures. Relational information was associated with lower constructor-averaged absolute discrepancy for five of six prespecified coefficients.

The limitations materially affect journal readiness. There are two already-exposed development reference families with overlapping evidence, five variables, residual effect-magnitude errors, a worse CHNS BMI result, one negative conditional-age mean, mixed statistical-comparator rankings, and zero feedback-driven scientific revisions. The comparison has five Agent constructions versus one prescribed statistical specification per case/condition. Three random seeds quantify sampling from a fixed law, not source, structural, or transport uncertainty. The 72 synthetic tables are not 72 independent biomedical validations. Technical audit strength does not close these scientific gaps.

## Published precedents that change the positioning

**SynthCraft is a close workflow precedent and a directly relevant PLOS Digital Health example.** Callender and colleagues published it on 9 March 2026, volume 5(3), e0001290. It uses an LLM-based partner and a reasoning engine to organize synthetic-data work, invokes Synthcity generators on individual-level input datasets, and records decisions/code. Evaluation includes NHANES and TCGA, tool-matched reasoning ablations, statistical fidelity, privacy, and downstream performance. Its augmentation analyses did not improve prediction. This is evidence that an agent-supported synthesis workflow and informative negative findings can belong in this journal; it is not an acceptance threshold or a head-to-head result for our method. [Published article](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0001290).

**Our inference:** the defensible distinction is construction from curated, study-qualified external summaries without individual-level construction inputs, coupled to explicit completion assumptions and analytical-use evaluation. “An AI partner generates synthetic biomedical data,” transparency, executable code, and iterative interaction are not sufficient novelty claims. SynthCraft's real-data-input setting differs, so running it on unavailable patient records would not be a fair primary comparator for our no-individual-input claim. That difference also does not establish our superiority. G-Sim and LLMSynthor remain relevant to the simulator-design and aggregate-to-microdata aspects already identified in v0.4; the predecessor comparison must cover all three families.

**JBI has published evaluation-focused synthetic-data methodology.** Kurakova and Homayouni, “A comprehensive evaluation framework for synthetic medical tabular data generation,” JBI 171 (November 2025), 104939, assessed quality, privacy, usability, and computational complexity using six generative methods and three medical datasets. The framework identified shortcomings missed by simpler distribution checks. [Publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1532046425001686), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2025.104939).

**Our inference:** a publication can contribute through evaluation design rather than a new foundation model. Our more specific opportunity concerns evidence semantics and preservation of scientific analyses under missing joint information. The JBI precedent's number of datasets is not a journal rule, and its data-trained models operate under a different information regime. It nevertheless shows why arithmetic reproducibility alone is too narrow a validation argument.

## What can be completed for the current frozen paper

These are manuscript and reproducibility tasks using existing records. They do not require new generation or reference retuning.

1. **Specify the contribution against its nearest alternatives.** Add a compact comparison of required inputs, source qualifications, joint-model authoring, numerical calibration, feedback, analytical evaluation, and uncertainty. Include SynthCraft, G-Sim, LLMSynthor and relevant statistical synthesis/data-fusion predecessors. Distinguish a workflow feature from an empirically demonstrated improvement. If no defensible incremental contribution remains after this comparison, changing journals will not solve that problem.
2. **Make one complete evidence-to-generator example readable.** Show the original statistical operator, source population and adjustment set, admitted role, exact model implication, completion assumptions, and observed target discrepancy, using already retained artifacts. A useful method is easier to assess when the reader can see what the evidence changes in the law and where that law still fails.
3. **State intended utility narrowly.** The current evidence supports transparent, assumption-qualified construction and evaluation for specified analyses. It does not validate clinical substitution, population recovery, calibration of uncertainty, cost savings, or feedback-driven improvement. Report the five-of-six information contrast with its exception and descriptive status. Retain all statistical comparisons and the zero-revision result.
4. **Turn public review artifacts into an intelligible reproducibility route.** Identify exactly which code, specifications, evidence summaries and aggregates are public; state what remains local and why; document allowed reproduction without restricted records. The current draft says the public package is not a complete portable runtime. For a software-centered PLOS submission, open-source licensing, dependencies and install/run instructions are directly relevant to the official tool requirements. Do not imply full reproducibility before checking it.
5. **Finish the actual submission declarations.** Verify bibliographic metadata and source extraction provenance; complete participant-reference ethics and access statements, authorship/contributions, funding, competing interests, and AI-use disclosure. Report observable execution/model metadata and explicitly identify unavailable metadata. No ethics approval or external clinical expert validation can be inferred from Agent audits.

**Decision after these tasks:** if a clear evidence-to-data capability and useful scientific lesson are demonstrable from the frozen records, a bounded PLOS Digital Health Research Article is a defensible first submission choice. If the conceptual method and generalizable lessons are the strongest part, JBI is a defensible alternative. If the claim is mainly a formative demonstration, JAMIA Brief Communication offers a format explicitly compatible with that maturity, subject to its editors finding the contribution worthwhile. A lack of demonstrated utility or distinction from predecessors remains a scientific acceptance risk for all three; editing cannot guarantee its removal.

## What would change the ambitious-path assessment

The following are **future research options, not work authorized or started by this assessment**. The frozen study's prohibitions on new acquisition, new generation, and target retuning remain in force.

| Scientific question unresolved by v0.4 | Evidence that would materially change the assessment |
|---|---|
| Does the procedure generalize beyond exposed development references? | A separately specified external evaluation with previously unused populations and source packets, fixed analyses and retained failures; broader biomedical questions would address more than repeating the same five-variable glycemic setting. |
| What does the procedure add beyond ordinary statistical or agentic synthesis? | Comparable-input and comparable-budget evaluation against credible statistical synthesis and relevant executable-agent methods, with explicit treatment of methods that require individual inputs. A mechanism claim would need a comparison that isolates that mechanism. |
| Are source semantics and transport assumptions scientifically useful? | Independent source-extraction verification and an evaluation that distinguishes source-context constraint fit, source-to-target transport, and effects of alternative completion assumptions. The current numerical-uptake counts do not do this. |
| How should an investigator act on imperfect generated analyses? | A prespecified decision/use case with meaningful error tolerances, appropriate uncertainty assessment, and explicit rejection or abstention when the evidence cannot support that use. Retrospective, computational or other suitable validation may answer this; wet-laboratory work is not a universal requirement in the policies reviewed. |
| Does adaptation improve a scientific result? | Actual controlled revision evidence retaining both improvements and harms, if revision remains a claimed contribution. Zero main-study revisions cannot support this claim, and revision need not be forced merely for narrative appeal. |

For npj Digital Medicine, the central additional question is demonstrated value to digital medicine or clinically relevant research practice. For Nature Communications, it is the importance and generality of the scientific advance. A new foundation model or wet-laboratory experiment is not intrinsically required to answer either question. What is required for a stronger claim is evidence that actually supports it; the current study should be published and described at its existing level of support.

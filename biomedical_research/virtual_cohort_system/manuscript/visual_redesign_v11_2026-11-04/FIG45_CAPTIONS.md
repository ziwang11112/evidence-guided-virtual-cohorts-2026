# v0.11 use-case figures

## Figure 4. Repeated-measure generation retains a published longitudinal analysis

**(a)** The existing HRS example contains 11,509 synthetic subjects at four scheduled visits, 0, 2, 4 and 6 years (46,036 records per table). This structural schematic contains no outcome measurements or inferred trajectories. **(b)** The two prespecified memory-score interactions use the HbA1c spline below 6.5% and at or above 6.5%. Each interaction displays all three fixed seeds from the Agent-authored recursive generator and the corrected published-law reconstruction. Blue circles and violet squares show the 12 stored estimates and conditional 95% Wald intervals from the same native R/lme4 analysis; gray dashed segments mark the two supplied source coefficients. The gray solid line marks a zero interaction. The lower-branch negative relationship is retained; weaker upper-branch directions and significance decisions vary across constructions and draws. Both constructions received aggregate model exports and method code. This establishes source-rich implementation and analysis preservation, not independent clinical confirmation or a method ranking. Source uncertainty, all 16 effects, the upper-HbA1c spread discrepancy and the three invalid initial comparator draws remain in the supporting record. No Agent table was regenerated.

The coefficient is memory-score change per decade per HbA1c percentage-point spline increment. “Law” labels the corrected published-law comparator. Source standard errors are retained in the numeric bindings but are not displayed as intervals.

## Figure 5. Scientific analysis profiles and the effect of an unreported joint relationship

**(a)** All seven nonintercept effects from the requested primary HbA1c model and the prespecified secondary model are shown, with three fixed 3,000-person draws from one Agent-authored generator. Native reference estimates and units appear beside each row; HbA1c is measured in percentage points (pp). To compare the seven scales in one aligned display, each estimate and interval bound is transformed as **(value − reference estimate) / ((reference upper bound − reference lower bound)/2)**. This invertible coordinate change is only a display convention. It is not a test statistic, acceptance region or equivalence criterion. Each reference bound is transformed separately, retaining any asymmetry; the dashed zero denotes the sample reference estimate, not a zero biological effect. Blue points, top to bottom within each row, are seeds 7401, 7402 and 7403; their bars are stored conditional 95% HC3-t intervals. Gray row bands are the transformed 95% community-cluster CR1-t intervals from the exposed CHNS sample (6,631 participants, 218 communities). Generated intervals condition on the model and draw and omit source, structural and transport uncertainty.

**(b)** Exactly two previously computed analytical scenarios change the unreported latent BMI–glucose correlation to 0.10 or 0.40. The primary BMI coefficient becomes +0.03422 or −0.01095 pp/(kg/m²), while the secondary BMI projection, marginal laws and every other bivariate law remain unchanged. This is a model-dependence assumption, not a causal biological edge. The delivered model used 0.25. The two points are not a fitted response curve, new generated samples or calibrated uncertainty limits. The overall case demonstrates partial scientific utility and a specific missing joint constraint, using an exposed developmental reference; it does not provide fresh confirmation or prove equivalence. Initial and final stages alias the same three tables.

## Production and binding

Both figures are new original 7-inch-wide displays of unchanged v0.10 aggregate inputs. Figure 4 is 3.7 inches high; Figure 5 is 4.15 inches high. Navy, blue, violet, coral and slate replace green/teal. No scientific outcome is filtered or colored as a pass/fail decision. Every native value, interval and transformed coordinate is retained in `NUMERIC_BINDINGS_45.json`. `render_scientific_use.py` pins its v0.10 aggregate input and the existing HRS schedule statement, supports local/public source layouts, and accepts `--output` for independent reproduction. No participant record, generator or clinical fit is used.

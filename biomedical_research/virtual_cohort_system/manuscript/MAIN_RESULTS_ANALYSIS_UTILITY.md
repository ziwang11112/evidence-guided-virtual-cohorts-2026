# Which scientific analyses did the virtual cohorts support?

Proposed main-text Results lead. This reading copy uses completed study aggregates only. The [full draft and endpoint tables](DRAFT_v0_17.md) and [iron-status supplement](IRON_STATUS_BREADTH_ADDENDUM.md) retain the complete individual studies.

## Does relational evidence help the agent build an analyzable cohort?

In CHNS and ENSANUT we compared cohorts generated from basic or marginal information with cohorts whose constructors also received external evidence about relationships among the variables. Adding relational evidence reduced the mean absolute discrepancy from the sample coefficient for five of the six prespecified regressions. For the CHNS glucose–HbA1c regression, discrepancy fell from 0.1340 to 0.0241 HbA1c percentage points per mmol/L of glucose. The exception was the CHNS BMI coefficient: discrepancy rose from 0.0204 to 0.0316. All six coefficients are shown together in the information-contrast figure ([Table S1](DRAFT_v0_17.md#s1-complete-external-evidence-contrast)). The contrast concerns the information supplied to the constructors. The two arms used different authors, and the exposed study references do not establish a prospective success rate or an advantage over statistical generation.

## What else can a researcher do with the same generated rows?

In a separate developmental study, two agents constructed continuous hemoglobin and ferritin measurements for nonpregnant US women aged 20–44. Both recovered the crude odds ratio in the external study (6.58 and 6.73 versus 6.65). The three planned analyses then gave distinct answers when applied to the same target deliveries. In 979 NHANES participants, the estimated proportion with both measures low was 7.63%; construction A yielded 6.66–6.88%, and B yielded 10.02–10.44%. For the continuous rank association, B's estimates of 0.391–0.430 were close to the sample estimate of 0.398; A yielded 0.156–0.195. The source packet supplied grouped measurements but no numerical rank coefficient. Both constructions nonetheless underestimated the primary target odds ratio (generated 4.84–6.56; measured 17.79, 95% CI 9.28–34.11). These are two constructions with different transport and dependence assumptions; neither was selected after disclosure. [Three-panel figure and all six deliveries](https://github.com/ziwang11112/evidence-guided-virtual-cohorts-2026/blob/9c665ee9f41349a8bb8855675d5423c1956fe903/biomedical_research/virtual_cohort_system/reports/iron_breadth_2026-11-05/RESULTS.md).

## How far did that usefulness extend?

Across six prespecified glycemia and lipid analyses, all 54 generated point estimates had the same direction as their corresponding sample estimates. These are repeat draws from six laws across two requests. Directional agreement concealed large differences in effect strength: for the residual TC–HDL correlation, the three construction means ranged from 0.108 to 0.141 versus 0.512 in the sample. One total-cholesterol reference interval crossed zero. With fitted longitudinal models available in HRS, the agent recovered the stronger lower-branch interaction (−0.04464 versus −0.04684 supplied); this is reconstruction rather than prediction of a withheld effect. In the earlier prospective HAALSI evaluation, all 15 draws recovered the directions of both main effects but only eight recovered the direction of the sex interaction. The prespecified joint criterion required four of five laws to pass in each case; three passed in HAALSI and none in the companion Turkana evaluation. The planned expansion stopped. [Full cross-task and prospective results](DRAFT_v0_17.md#s7-prospective-evaluation-under-the-earlier-method).

This layout prioritizes study questions rather than scoring individual endpoints as victories or losses. None of the studies estimates an overall clinical accuracy rate, justifies choosing a different generator for each endpoint after reveal, or tests whether virtual measurements can replace patient measurements.

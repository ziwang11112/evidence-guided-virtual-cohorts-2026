# Native-unit paper figures: v0.12

## Figure 4. Longitudinal relationships in a published-cohort reconstruction

Panels (a) and (b) show the prespecified interactions of memory-score change per decade with the HbA1c spline below 6.5% and at or above 6.5%, respectively. Three fixed seeds from the Agent-authored recursive generator (blue circles) and the corrected published-law reconstruction (violet squares) are shown on the same native coefficient scale. Horizontal bars are the 12 stored model-conditional 95% Wald intervals from the same R/lme4 analysis; dashed gray lines mark the two supplied source coefficients, and thin solid gray lines mark zero. Seed rows identify draws, not paired statistical comparisons between constructions. Each table contains 11,509 synthetic subjects at 0, 2, 4 and 6 years (46,036 records). Source standard errors are recorded but not plotted. Both constructions received the aggregate source-model exports and method code. The lower-branch negative interaction is retained; the weaker upper-branch point estimates and significance decisions vary. All 16 effects, the high-HbA1c spread discrepancy and the documented three invalid initial comparator draws remain in the supporting record. This is source-rich reconstruction, not independent clinical confirmation or evidence of a method ranking.

## Figure 5. Requested analyses on generated data and sensitivity to missing joint evidence

Panels (a–d) report all four nonintercept effects from the requested primary model, HbA1c ~ BMI + glucose + age + female. Panels (e–g) report all three effects from the prespecified secondary model omitting glucose. All estimates and interval bounds use native units; pp denotes HbA1c percentage points. Identical predictor types share axis limits across the two models. Blue points correspond to the three fixed 3,000-person draws (seeds 7401–7403), with stored conditional 95% HC3-t intervals. Dashed gray lines and gray bands are reference estimates and 95% community-cluster CR1-t intervals from the exposed CHNS sample (6,631 participants, 218 communities); thin solid gray lines mark a zero coefficient. Generated intervals condition on the chosen model and draw and omit source, structural and transport uncertainty. Interval overlap is descriptive and is not an equivalence test. Panel (h) shows exactly two previously computed analytical scenarios for the unreported latent BMI–glucose correlation: 0.10 and 0.40 yield primary BMI coefficients +0.03422 and −0.01095 pp/(kg/m²). Marginal laws, other bivariate laws and the secondary projection remain unchanged. The delivered model used 0.25. The points do not constitute a fitted curve, new synthetic draws or a causal intervention. Initial and final stages alias the same three delivered tables. This exposed-reference developmental case shows analysis-dependent usefulness and a remaining joint-dependence limitation, rather than fresh confirmation or uniform recovery of all analyses.

## Production

White backgrounds, conventional axes, shared legends and native units replace the previous dashboard. There are no KPI cards, acceptance colors, normalized-distance axes or invented trajectories. Figure 4 is 7 × 2.15 inches; Figure 5 is 7 × 4.05 inches. All 12 HRS estimates/intervals, two source values, 21 latest generated estimates/intervals, seven reference estimates/intervals and two analytical points are retained. `NUMERIC_BINDINGS_45.json` contains every native value and source hash. `render_scientific_use.py` performs presentation-only work and accepts `--output`; no patient record, generator or clinical fit is read or executed.

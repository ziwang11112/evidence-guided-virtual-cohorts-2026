# Focused primary-source checks of the Claude review

Checked 28 October 2026. Input: the user-supplied Claude review in attachment `CLAUDE_REVIEW_USER_SUPPLIED.md`, read in full. The earlier [primary checks](PRIMARY_SOURCE_CHECKS.md) were consulted first. This report verifies selected consequential external claims; it does not endorse every recommendation, audit all citations, or rerun project code. No participant data were downloaded.

## Corrections that matter to the research plan

1. **ModelSMC is published at ICML 2026.** Claude's “arXiv v1; no venue stated” is out of date. The official PMLR record was already checked: volume 306, pages 124224–124278. [Official proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/wahl26a.html).
2. **SynDI is not directly applicable as an unchanged baseline when the entire target outcome is absent.** Gu et al.'s internal data explicitly include measured outcome and covariates. It is an important predecessor, and potentially an adapted or richer-information comparison, but “required baseline” is a design opinion with an information-regime mismatch.
3. **The contamination literature establishes risk, not exposure of our exact models to NHANES.** The 2026 imputation study reports public-versus-synthetic performance differences and interprets them as semantic/prior exposure. That does not causally establish memorization of our target rows. A no-retrieval comparison measures incremental retrieval value; by itself it does not identify or exclude pretraining contamination.
4. **The proposed ensemble is not automatically calibrated.** Preset structural weights, category-based bridge variances and Gaussian parameter draws remain modeling choices. The cited precedents do not validate this particular recipe. Wider intervals or more sampled parameters are not sufficient evidence of correct uncertainty.
5. **Dataset availability needs case-specific approval.** HSE in particular has geographical, institutional and licence restrictions; “available through UKDS” does not mean immediately usable by this user. CHARLS requires registration and application review. No dataset access was obtained during this check.

## Memorization and the 2026 imputation study

**Bordt et al. — verified, but do not overgeneralize.** *Elephants Never Forget: Memorization and Learning of Tabular Data in Large Language Models* reports verbatim memorization of popular tabular datasets, performance differences between previously seen and post-training datasets, and memorization probes. The author record identifies the COLM 2024 camera-ready paper. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06209v3), [author-hosted conference PDF](https://besmiranushi.com/docs/elephants_tabular_memorization_llm_2024.pdf). This warrants controls and qualified claims; it does not demonstrate contamination of every public dataset or our specific Agent versions.

**Mangussi et al. — verified as a March 2026 preprint; journal/venue not established in this check.** *Large Language Models for Missing Data Imputation: Understanding Behavior, Hallucination Effects, and Control Mechanisms* was submitted 20 March 2026. It studies five LLMs, six conventional comparators and 29 datasets including nine synthetic datasets, using MCAR/MAR/MNAR and missing rates up to 20%. It reports strong LLM performance on open real datasets and MICE advantages on synthetic data, interpreting the difference as evidence of semantic/pretraining knowledge. [Primary abstract/status](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22332), [full preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22332v1). These are partial-cell imputation experiments, not complete absence of a target column. The interpretation is not a controlled demonstration that memorized records caused the difference; it should be reported as the authors' interpretation.

## Recommendations not established by the cited sources

- The product of independent Gaussian summary likelihoods assumes a covariance structure and correctly defined estimands. Overlapping cohorts and correlated estimates can violate that product. Merely inflating a variance cannot make an incompatible statistic a parameter on a different scale.
- Preset alternative-model weights and mismatch-category variances need independent justification and calibration; absence of target data makes this harder, not automatic. Treat uncalibrated ensembles as sensitivity analyses.
- “Never report synthetic SEs or p-values” is an overbroad prohibition. The defensible restriction is against presenting conditional simulated-data precision as total empirical evidence. Labeled diagnostics may remain useful; final inferential rules must match the actual observation/synthesis design.
- A reference interval is not a universal equivalence region. Falling outside it does not alone establish unusability; falling inside does not prove analytical validity. Practical tolerances and reference uncertainty require a declared scientific interpretation.
- A no-retrieval arm is valuable, but similar performance may reflect sufficient prior knowledge, redundant retrieved evidence or poor retrieval—not proof that the general literature-to-data task has no value. Cross-study and prospective checks are still needed.

These are methodological cautions derived from comparing the proposal with its assumptions, not additional experimental findings. No retrospective success threshold or new model has been adopted.

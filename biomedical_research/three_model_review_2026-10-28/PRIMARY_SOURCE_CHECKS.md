# Selected primary-source checks of the ChatGPT review

Checked 28 October 2026. This is a focused verification of selected new external claims, not a systematic novelty review, assessment of every recommendation, or replication of another paper. No participant data were downloaded or analysed. Existing experiments were not changed.

## 1. Close precedents

| Claim | Status | Primary-source finding and qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Kramer et al., *Leveraging generative AI to enhance Synthea model development*, JAMIA Open 2026, DOI `10.1093/jamiaopen/ooaf123` | **Verified: identity, publication date and central workflow** | Publisher metadata gives online publication **6 January 2026**, volume 9(1), February 2026. The paper describes four stages: medical-source extraction into functional requirements; an initial Synthea-compatible model; syntax and requirements review; iterative feedback-based revision. Hyperthyroidism is the example, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro. Requirements traceability and focused correction of low-scoring requirements are already present. Crucially, high LLM-judge scores are not clinical accuracy; the paper retains human verification and population-validation limitations. This is a close precedent for our generic evidence-to-code/review loop, but it does not by itself establish the same absent-column, cross-study target-analysis validation task. [Publisher article](https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/9/1/ooaf123/8415656), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41502536/). |
| SASC, *Patterns* 2022, DOI `10.1016/j.patter.2022.100453` | **Verified, with a correlation-preservation qualification** | Full primary text is available in PMC. It generates virtual cohorts from grouped summary statistics using constrained random-number generation, and compares distributions, correlations and survival analyses against a 375-person COVID-19 reference and Synthea. Publication is 2022; article date 9 February and issue date 8 April. This supports summary-to-cohort generation and downstream-analysis comparison as prior art. It does **not** establish that arbitrary marginal summaries identify joint relationships: the paper explicitly notes correlations need not be strictly maintained. Nor is its reference-data-derived construction an independent target-population enrichment demonstration. [Full primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8825316/), [publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666389922000265). |
| ModelSMC, *A Probabilistic Framework for LLM-Based Model Discovery*, ICML 2026 | **Verified: actual proceedings, not merely an arXiv claim** | The official page lists Wahl, Schenk, Farnoud, Macke and Gedon; *Proceedings of the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning*, PMLR **306:124224–124278**, 2026. Candidate mechanistic models are particles, proposed/refined by an LLM and weighted using likelihood criteria in sequential Monte Carlo. The page reports improved posterior predictive checks in scientific-system experiments. This verifies the probabilistic model-discovery precedent. It does not require us to adopt SMC, establish universal calibration, or show the identical evidence-only enrichment use case. [Official proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/wahl26a.html). |

Access note: OUP's direct open sometimes redirected to an inaccessible CDN, and PMC/PubMed sometimes returned a browser check. The search tool nevertheless returned the **publisher's indexed article text**, including methodology and conclusion, and its publication metadata. SASC and ModelSMC primary pages opened directly. No third-party paper summary was used to substantiate the rows above. No source code or numerical claims from those studies were independently reproduced.

## 3. Turkana public-data candidate: metadata only

**Status: public deposit and stated variables verified; experimental eligibility remains unverified.**

The official Dryad page identifies Amanda Lea's *Cardio-metabolic biomarkers measured across a lifestyle gradient*, published 24 September 2020, DOI `10.5061/dryad.2z34tmpjk`. It links one 87.74 KB text file, `22Jul20_for_Dryad_noIDs.txt`, and describes Turkana participants across pastoralist/urban lifestyles. Metadata explicitly list BMI, age, sex, HDL, triglycerides, glucose, other lipids, waist circumference, blood pressure, body fat and lifestyle category, with units and `NA` missing-value notation. Methods refer to Lea et al. 2020 in *Science Advances*. [Official Dryad metadata](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.2z34tmpjk).

Only the landing page was read. The participant file was **not** downloaded or opened. Complete-case counts, age ranges, subgroup sizes, joint missingness, fasting/assay compatibility, recruitment/selection, independence from prospective source papers, licence terms, and readiness for our analysis are therefore **not verified**. The file's name does not remove the need for data handling checks; usage notes still list an individual identifier. This remains a candidate for a custodian's eligibility audit, not a selected or guaranteed clean confirmation cohort.

## 4. What this changes—and does not change

The nearest-work additions are real and should constrain broad novelty language. Generic literature-to-code, iterative LLM review, summary-driven cohorts and probabilistic candidate-model management already have precedents. Whether our specific absent-variable information regime, study-context translation, analytical validation or measured reduction of expert work adds a publishable contribution remains a research question.

The external review's proposed 6–8 tasks, two analytical classes, 200 simulation worlds, coverage screen, timetable and preferred titles are **design recommendations**, not externally established results or journal requirements. This check did not validate those numbers. It also did not verify the model's claim of private GitHub access; that belongs to the browser/tool audit, not literature verification.

# Initial generator decision

Selected route: an Agent-authored numerical program, `initial/generator.py`, exposing `generate(observed, evidence, seed)`. `initial/model_spec.json` is its `evidence` argument. This is an implemented simulation, not direct record generation or a fitted individual prediction model. The function returns exactly `row_id,bmi,age,sex,insulin`, in original order and preserving observed values and dtypes. The output is fasting insulin in uU/mL; the schema name `insulin_uU_mL` is an interface alias only.

The conditional distribution is lognormal. Four age-specific insulin medians and corresponding BMI medians come from the large Brazilian healthy reference study S1. The program interpolates log insulin and reference BMI at age-bin midpoints. Its fifth age anchor multiplies S1's 50-60 median by the older/adult median ratio in the German reference study S2. This uses a dimensionless ratio, so no disputed molar conversion enters generation. End segments are flat at ages below 24.5 and above 70. There is no age step at an arbitrary birthday.

The BMI log slope is `log(6.05/4.29)/(28.96-21.07) = 0.0435705373` per kg/m², from S3's non-diabetic heavier and leaner groups. This is an ecological interpolation between group arithmetic means. Applying it to an individual's conditional log median requires an equal-log-variance assumption and is not an adjusted source regression coefficient. No odds ratio, mediation percentage, partial correlation or standardized waist coefficient was misrepresented as a continuous insulin slope. This single slope also extrapolates beyond the BMI range well represented by the sources.

Conditional log SD is `0.6341865563`. Source S3 reports means and standard errors, so each group SD was first reconstructed as `SE × sqrt(n)`. Each group's lognormal variance was moment-matched as `log(1 + SD²/mean²)` and then pooled with weights `n−1`. The original marginal subgroup widths include BMI variation and unmeasured covariates; using their pooled width as conditional noise is a deliberate approximation that may overstate residual spread. It preserves substantial positive stochastic variation without using target outcomes or forcing any particular generated sample correlation. The lognormal form supplies positive right-skewed draws; source evidence does not establish its tail shape.

The explicit sex coefficient is zero. S1 and S2 judged separate reference intervals unnecessary; S4's age/sex results vary with cross-sectional versus longitudinal estimands and adjustment. Those observations do not identify an exactly zero BMI-adjusted sex effect. Zero is a parsimonious assumption, documented as such. The supplied covariate distributions can still produce marginal sex differences.

S1 supplies the baseline scale, S3 the relative BMI contrast and spread, and S2 the older-age ratio. This modular borrowing is not a meta-analysis. Sources differ in population, healthy-selection criteria, serum versus plasma, and assays. S4 and S6 show substantial scale and association heterogeneity. No paper retrieved provides a numeric calibration to the target Tosoh assay. Accordingly, no correction factor or target-calibration claim is introduced. S5 was screened and reviewed but does not parameterize the model.

There is no clipping, quantile matching, target fitting, disease-label assignment, or attempt to recover participants. No lower detection-limit replacement is simulated: values are latent positive concentrations, and some can lie below 1 uU/mL. This does not reproduce the released laboratory handling of low results. Underdiagnosed disease, medications, ethnicity, physical activity and body composition remain unobserved. The healthy external studies do not identify their distribution in the target.

Parameters are fixed. A local NumPy generator produces independent row residuals for a supplied seed, without file/network access, subprocesses, input mutation or global random-state mutation. The 20 controller draws are repetitions of this one construction; their variation represents conditional simulation noise. They do not quantify source-parameter, model-form or transport uncertainty, and they are not proper multiple imputations with established frequentist coverage. Source uncertainty is recorded in the ledger where reported, rather than replaced with invented precise confidence intervals for this model.

Intended use is a blinded first test of whether this explicitly assumed feature-enrichment construction retains useful scientific summaries. Unsupported uses include individual insulin prediction, diagnosis, treatment decisions, causal effects, claim of recovered truth, calibrated subgroup inference, or unconditional national population estimation. Any subsequent revision must be saved in a new directory and based only on permitted source/code feedback. The initial model must remain retained.

The sources, quantities, transformations and limitations are auditable in `EVIDENCE_LEDGER.json`; all search attempts are in `SEARCH_LOG.md`. Artificial-input verification is recorded in `ARTIFICIAL_CHECKS.json`. No real target outcomes or outcome-derived evaluation answers were accessed or computed during construction.

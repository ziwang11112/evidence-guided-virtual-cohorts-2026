# Initial candidate

Selected a conditional lognormal HDL generator with sex-specific scale and residual variability, a negative BMI term, and a modest positive age term. All input row IDs, values and order are copied exactly. A local NumPy RNG provides person-level stochastic variation. No HDL observations or summaries from the target were accessed, and no generated draw was used to tune the model.

MESA Tables 1-2 in PMC5568454 supply sex-specific HDL means/SDs and predictor moments (E1-E2). PMC3411890 supplies an age-, sex-, and ethnicity-adjusted **Spearman** BMI-HDL correlation of -0.26 (E3). The two articles share MESA participants and count as one cohort. A Gaussian-copula bridge turns this rank association into log-scale slopes using source predictor and outcome SDs. It is an explicit approximation: the reported correlation is neither an adjusted linear coefficient nor proof of equal associations within each sex.

The independent Lithuanian reference sample in PMC3599926 provides sex-specific HDL means at ages 25-44, 45-64 and 65-74 (E4). The initial age slope uses the log ratio of oldest to youngest means divided by the 35-year midpoint difference. This cancels units and avoids interpreting mmol/L as mg/dL. The table reports age/BMI adjustment within each age group, but does not report a shared adjustment reference or age coefficient; cross-group transfer remains assumed. The paper's conflicting SD/SE descriptions prevent using its dispersion for residual calibration.

The lognormal choice gives positive support, a right tail and increasing absolute variability as the conditional mean rises. Total source log variance is decomposed into modeled age variance, BMI variance and residual variance. This avoids using a marginal SD unchanged after adding covariate effects. The algebra reproduces source moments under an assumed independent Gaussian reference distribution of age and BMI. It does not imply that actual source predictors are independent Gaussian, nor that target margins should equal MESA. No percentile or correlation is forced in generated target samples.

Reference source means are 45 mg/dL for men and 56 for women, with SDs 11 and 14. Derived BMI slopes are -0.01620 and -0.01113 log mg/dL per BMI unit; age slopes are 0.003190 and 0.000969 per year. These sex differences partly arise from source scales and the common-rank-correlation assumption, not directly estimated interaction coefficients. Residual log SDs are 0.22982 and 0.23679. Full equations and provenance are in model_spec.json.

Alternatives considered were an additive normal model, which needs handling for negative extreme draws; a flat age effect, which discards the approved cross-age evidence; and additional BMI curvature or age-BMI interactions, which the retrieved aggregate evidence cannot estimate. The selected log-linear model is the initial candidate. It extrapolates continuously rather than imposing unsupported thresholds or a maximum HDL. Source S1 lists an upper range of 100 while S2 reports values to 142; therefore a 100 mg/dL cap would be unjustified.

Observed predictor inspection showed n=4885, age 20-79 and BMI 14.1-77.5. This is used only to identify extrapolation and design numerical boundary tests. MESA contains no ages below 45 and excludes weight above 136 kg; severe obesity is a material unsupported region. No diagnosis, treatment, smoking, drinking, ancestry or menopause information is supplied, so their residual associations cannot be recreated. Fasting plasma precipitation assays differ from the target serum direct assay. No assay or temporal correction can be identified from the retrieved evidence.

Supported uses are transparent simulation, method development, and sensitivity-based association analysis conditional on the stated model. Analyses of BMI, sex, age, tails, thresholds or interactions will reflect these modeling assumptions. They are not recovered patient measurements, independent biological discoveries, causal effects, or validated target-population results. Additional variables correlated with HDL conditional on the three predictors will not retain their true associations. Larger virtual cohorts do not supply additional clinical evidence.

The gateway used 10/12 searches and 7/12 download attempts. Four articles were blocked by the study-exclusion screen; no rejected full text or alternative retrieval route was used. All numerical inputs came from the three approved maintexts. Two queries and five downloads remain for the single review/revision turn. Priorities for review are the partial-rank conversion, the age-contrast bridge, transfer of scale and residual variation, and extrapolation to young adults/extreme BMI.

Actual source-only and invented-input checks are recorded in artificial_checks.json, with a reproducible check script. They assess code and assumptions; no true target-outcome validation was run.

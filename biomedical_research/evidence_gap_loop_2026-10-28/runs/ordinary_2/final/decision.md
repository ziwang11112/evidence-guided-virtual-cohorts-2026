# Final candidate and review response

Retained the initial generator and model specification byte for byte. The final numerical model is a sex-specific conditional lognormal distribution for HDL in mg/dL, with log-linear BMI and age effects and local seeded stochastic variation. The review confirmed the extraction, equations and implementation; it identified material non-identification and transport limitations rather than an error that determines a replacement parameter. Two additional searches did not supply distinguishing evidence, so changing coefficients would introduce another unsupported choice. This is a documented numerical no-op, not a claim that the initial model is optimal or validated.

E1-E2 from PMC5568454 provide MESA sex-specific marginal HDL and predictor moments. E3 from PMC3411890 provides the age-, sex- and ethnicity-adjusted BMI-HDL Spearman correlation. Those articles overlap in MESA participants, and the association analysis additionally excludes lipid-lowering therapy and high triglycerides. They are one evidence stream with differing selection rules. E4 from PMC3599926 supplies adjusted Lithuanian population age-group HDL means; its conflicting SD/SE description prevents use of its dispersion. No new quantitative source was added after review.

The source HDL means are 45 and 56 mg/dL for men and women, with SDs 11 and 14. Under the specified Gaussian reference and lognormal bridges, BMI slopes are -0.01620 and -0.01113 per kg/m^2 on the log scale, age slopes are 0.003190 and 0.000969 per year, and residual log SDs are 0.22982 and 0.23679. These are constructed conditional parameters, not directly estimated source coefficients. Observed values and order remain unchanged; no target outcome was read or used for model selection.

For F1, the Gaussian-copula formula is an assumption applied to an adjusted rank statistic. It is not an exact inversion of arbitrary partial Spearman calculations. Sex-specific marginal SDs do not provide residual SDs after adjustment, nor do they identify equal within-sex rank dependence. The final artificial checks construct four Gaussian examples with identical marginal scales and identical rank correlation of Gaussian residuals under the assumed bridge, while varying an invented omitted adjustment variable. Their implied male BMI40/BMI25 conditional mean ratios range from 0.675 to 1.037; the selected case gives 0.784. The analogous female ratios range from 0.763 to 1.026, with selected value 0.846. These arbitrary examples demonstrate non-identification. They are not biological bounds, probabilities, sensitivity weights, or replications of the source's adjusted-rank computation. The mismatch between source selection rules remains unresolved.

For F2, the age slope is one interpretation of broad age-group contrasts. A flat individual conditional-age relation need not deny or discard the reported group means: composition, standardization or cohort offsets could explain their contrast. The initial decision's stronger wording is withdrawn. The final checks allocate 0%, 50%, 100% or 150% of the endpoint log contrast to conditional age, with corresponding hypothetical group offsets that preserve both endpoint means exactly. For men these constructions give fixed-BMI age20/age62 mean ratios of 1.000, 0.935, 0.875 and 0.818; for women, 1.000, 0.980, 0.960 and 0.941. The retrieved table does not select among these interpretations. The initial slope is retained as the stated transport assumption; missing information is not evidence of a zero effect. Middle-group agreement checks interpolation only.

For F3, fasting plasma precipitation measurements and a Lithuanian serum assay do not establish equivalence with the target direct serum assay. Older disease-free MESA participants, therapy exclusions, participation selection and calendar differences remain. MESA's weight exclusion and lack of young adults leave severe obesity and ages below 45 unsupported; the target predictor range extends to BMI 77.5 and age 20. The two MESA papers' different upper HDL ranges remain unexplained, so no 100 mg/dL cap is imposed. Equal-moment lognormal and gamma examples in the final checks have different tails despite positive support. At invented BMI25/age60, the male probabilities above 100 mg/dL are approximately 0.000317 and 0.000072; female probabilities are 0.00827 and 0.00547. Neither positivity nor these comparisons validate the chosen family.

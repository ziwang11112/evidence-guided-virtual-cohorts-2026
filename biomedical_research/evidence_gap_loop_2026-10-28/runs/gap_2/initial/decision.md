# Initial candidate

The selected model generates positive stochastic HDL in mg/dL from sex-specific BMI mean and SD curves plus a modest common age pattern. A lognormal draw is matched to the specified arithmetic mean and SD. Its random seed changes individual residual draws; the model coefficients remain fixed. No target HDL, target summaries, original IDs or other runs were accessed.

The primary quantitative anchor is Taiwan Biobank (PMC6627528), Table 2. It reports HDL means and **standard errors**, jointly by sex and BMI category. Individual cell SDs are derived as SE × sqrt(n), not equated to SE. The published means are marginal over other factors within their cells. The generator places them at assumed BMI representatives (17.5, 21.25, 25.5, 30) and linearly interpolates. These are pointwise bridges from category summaries, not reported source BMI means. The sex-specific curves retain differences visible in the published cells without adding the adjusted sex coefficient a second time.

Table 3's age contrasts are interpolated at approximate category midpoints and centered with the overall source age counts. Their application to each sex/BMI cell assumes comparable age mixing. The source's age labeling has an overlapping 40-year boundary; this model does not invent its actual coding. The endpoint effect is carried to ages outside the source range. The common age pattern remains provisional: Dallas Heart Study (PMC4669058) Table 2 reports a larger female than male standardized age coefficient, under a substantially different adjustment set. Missing source SDs prevent a defensible conversion to mg/dL per year. Absence of a male significant result is not used as evidence of a zero male effect.

The BMI >=27 source category does not identify a severe-obesity slope. The UK/Malawi comparison (PMC7364601) describes mostly linear inverse BMI-HDL associations and stronger associations in some UK groups. Its numerical supplement is PDF, which the gateway lists as unsupported; it was not accessed. An apparent HDL/LDL labeling inconsistency in a positive coefficient example is excluded from numerical use. Neither this paper nor the category means establish an exact plateau. The selected high-BMI continuation declines exponentially from the BMI-30 anchor toward assumed means of 30 in men and 38 in women, with decay 0.04 per BMI unit. A focused alternative holds means flat above BMI 30. The numerical sensitivity in artificial_checks.json evaluates both at invented BMI values and demonstrates how severe-obesity conclusions can differ while the central anchors agree. These two choices are not a calibrated uncertainty interval.

Conditional SD is interpolated from published cell SDs and held at endpoints. Cell SD includes within-cell variation in continuous BMI, age and unmeasured factors; it is only a proxy for conditional residual SD. No subtraction based on unreported R-squared or imputed predictor covariance is made. Lognormal skew and tail probabilities are assumptions. Population and measurement uncertainty are not added as if they were new independent person-level noise.

Source applicability is the largest general limitation. The primary anchor is Taiwanese volunteers aged 30-70; its exact HDL assay/fasting protocol is not supplied. Dallas is geographically relevant but older, selected, and excludes lipid/hormone therapy and several diseases. Its medians/IQRs are not means/SDs and are not used as a calibration target without the corresponding joint predictor distribution. The UK/Malawi comparison explicitly shows population-dependent associations. The target packet's direct serum assay is bridged to these reports without an estimated assay correction. No target national calibration or survey-weight inference is attempted.

Only target predictor ranges were inspected: 4,885 rows; BMI 14.1-77.5 and age 20-79; 1,255 people fall outside the source age range 30-70 and 378 have BMI above 40. These facts identify extrapolation stakes, not target HDL behavior. No generator draw on target predictors was used for tuning.

The main intended uses are exploratory BMI-HDL associations, sex contrasts, descriptive variation, and explicit stress tests of less constrained age, shape and threshold analyses. Causal effects of BMI change, longitudinal aging, individual recovery, clinical diagnosis, and claims of national population calibration are unsupported. Repeated virtual draws quantify residual draw variation within this model, not independent biomedical evidence or uncertainty across plausible models.

Actual checks are reproducible with run_artificial_checks.py: column/value/order preservation, no input or global RNG mutation, seed reproducibility, seed-dependent residual variability, finite positive outputs over invented extremes, empty input, invalid-sex rejection, exact SE-to-SD arithmetic, source artifact hashes, parameter reference consistency, and Monte Carlo recovery of chosen moments. Source-anchor age-mixture mean checks are explicitly conditional on assumed BMI points and age mixing. Exact source cell/regression replication cannot be computed from the available aggregate information. These are source/numerical checks, not target validation.

Search stopped provisionally after six queries and three maintext downloads. The focused shape search led back to the already read cross-country study, while many other hits concerned different outcomes or populations. This is pragmatic saturation of the present lead, not exhaustive literature coverage. Six queries and nine downloads remain for the single review/revision turn, with age-sex effects and severe-BMI strata ranked as the most consequential attainable follow-ups.

# Final decision after one review

The numerical generator and specification are retained byte-for-byte. The final model remains a positive lognormal draw matched to sex-specific BMI mean/SD curves, a modest common age pattern, and an assumed declining continuation above BMI 30. The specification's original version field is deliberately preserved because this is an exact numerical no-op. New evidence, source extraction, sensitivity diagnostics and gap dispositions are recorded separately. No target outcomes, generated target cohorts, other runs, custodian files or outcome-derived feedback were inspected.

F1 prompted a complete re-extraction of the already approved Taiwan Table 5 age rows and Table 2 age-by-BMI counts. Against age 30-40, the age 61-70 contrasts are 4.24658, 1.03037, 0.65788 and 1.17587 mg/dL across underweight, normal, overweight and obese strata. The pooled contrast is 0.87361. The largest difference occurs in the underweight stratum (total n=408; n=45 at age 61-70). The final checks compare all these contrasts with the selected common-age pattern. Separate p-values do not test interaction; coefficient covariance and a formal age-by-BMI test are not supplied. These richer regressions adjust for lifestyle, WHR and body fat beyond the available predictors. They still do not supply a compatible age-by-sex structure. I retained the common curve as the simpler explicit scenario, rather than interpreting each stratum estimate as a target interaction. This does not establish homogeneity. The selected curve can miss the reported stratum-specific differences, which remains a substantive limitation.

A focused age/sex/BMI search identified PMC7202729, but its maintext was rejected for excluded_study_alias. No rejected content was read or used and no bypass was attempted. The Dallas standardized age effect remains contextual: source-specific age and HDL SDs needed for an unstandardized conversion are not available for those models. No standardized coefficient was treated as mg/dL/year.

F2 did not reveal a wrong SE-to-SD calculation. It highlights a real operator mismatch. Table 2 means and SDs integrate over continuous BMI, age and omitted factors within each sex/BMI cell; the model uses them as pointwise anchors and residual proxies. Marginalizing the added age curve adds between-age variance to the retained cell SD. Final numerical checks explicitly compute this addition under the declared representative-age mixture. That calculation illustrates the assumption and does not repair the unavailable source joint distribution. No covariance was invented, no unexplained variance was deducted, and neither Monte Carlo recovery nor anchor centering is called source or target validation.

F3 triggered a search specifically for obesity classes I, II and III. The pooled U.S. lead PMC7596579 was rejected for excluded_study_alias. A Mexican study (PMC6755842) provides a concrete sex-by-obesity-class table lead and a fasting COBAS c111 measure, but its detailed Table S1 is an unsupported PDF supplement. Only the approved maintext was used for context. The study excluded chronic disease, medications, alcohol, smoking and other conditions, limiting transport even if the table became available.

An approved Turkish primary study (PMC6458951) reports HDL means of 43.60, 42.62 and 38.36 mg/dL in obesity classes I, II and III, with mean BMIs 32.80, 36.92 and 45.77. Its Table 1 explicitly reports SDs, not SEs. The class-III minus class-I mean contrast is -5.24 mg/dL. The final checks compare this descriptive contrast with selected-sex plug-in contrasts at the reported BMI/age means and with the flat-tail alternative. This is not an exact source fit: groups were small (10, 13, 22), unmatched, extensively selected, and their sex mix and joint predictor distributions are missing. The primary class means support continuing decline as a plausible scenario, but cannot determine our asymptotes or decay constant. Secondary numerical claims from this paper's discussion/references are not used. Published p-values compare against normal weight and are not used as within-obesity precision weights.

Accordingly, the high-BMI parameters remain assumed and unchanged. The original flat-versus-declining comparison is retained: the models agree at central anchors while differing by about 4.3 mg/dL at BMI 40 and about 10.5 mg/dL at BMI 70. Neither the finite pair nor the added age comparison is a calibrated uncertainty interval. The new data do not support behavior through the target's largest BMI values.

The primary Taiwan source still determines the means and approximate within-cell SDs, with midpoint interpolation and population/assay bridges stated in the unchanged specification. Target applicability, conditional tails, exact residual variation and age interactions remain unresolved. Population-level differences are not converted into independent person-level noise. No synthetic value is a patient measurement, and repeated draws from this model provide neither new clinical evidence nor independent models. Intended use remains exploratory cross-sectional association and descriptive analyses with these assumptions exposed; causal, longitudinal, diagnostic and national-calibration uses remain unsupported.

Final tests rerun the preserved implementation checks on invented inputs and source arithmetic, verify source hashes and gateway registration, confirm exact initial/final numerical-file equality, compare source age contrasts, account for the assumed marginal age variance, and compute the within-obesity descriptive contrast. Every final gap has a linked revision action and a non-open explicit disposition. No improved target accuracy is claimed.

The same literature session used eight queries and seven download attempts in total, including two revision rejections. The revision added two searches and four download attempts, producing two usable maintexts. Four queries and five downloads remain unused. Search stopped after the specific obtainable leads were assessed; this is bounded, pragmatic stopping, not exhaustive literature saturation or a budget-exhaustion claim.

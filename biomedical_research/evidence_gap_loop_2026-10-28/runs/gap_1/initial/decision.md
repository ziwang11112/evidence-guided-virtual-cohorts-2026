# Initial candidate decision

This candidate generates HDL-C in mg/dL from BMI, age and sex using a lognormal conditional distribution. It preserves all supplied predictors and row order. It is a source-informed virtual-data model, with no target-outcome validation or patient reconstruction claim.

The conditional mean at BMI 27 and age 50 is 52.9263 mg/dL for men and 66.1551 for women. Log-mean BMI slopes are -0.0208808 and -0.0198025 per kg/m², respectively. A small common age profile rises from age 35 to 55.5 and then declines to 65.5; values are held at the endpoints outside that range. Conditional log SD is 0.299122 for men and 0.252124 for women. All full-precision values, equations and provenance are in `model_spec.json`.

## Evidence and transformations

- **Dependence:** Taiwan Biobank Table 2 (E_TW_BMI, PMC6627528) supplies unadjusted sex-by-BMI-category HDL means. Normal-to-obese ratios are 43.25/51.92 for men and 51.06/60.72 for women. I divide their logarithms by an assumed representative BMI difference of 30.0 - 21.25. The normal midpoint is a choice based on the printed interval, and obese BMI 30 is assumed. These are bridge-derived slopes, not reported continuous or age-adjusted coefficients. They preserve the observed source direction without requiring that a heavily adjusted WHR/body-fat coefficient equal the desired BMI association.
- **Age:** Table 3 of the same study (E_TW_AGE) reports additive adjusted contrasts 0, 0.75841, 1.18484 and 0.87361 mg/dL for its age categories. Dividing by an assumed scale of 55 mg/dL gives relative means. I interpolate their logarithms at category centers and center at age 50. The article does not identify sex-specific age trajectories, young-adult behavior or the 71-79-year extension.
- **Location and variation:** MIDUS Results (E_US_MOMENTS, PMC8818198) reports male HDL 50.4 ± 16.5 and female HDL 62.8 ± 18.8 mg/dL, where the spread is SD. Its Table 2 gives age/BMI moments and counts in four race/diabetes cells per sex (E_US_COVARIATES). I compute expected mean multipliers under an explicitly assumed mixture of independent normal age and BMI marginals within these cells, then solve the intercept and residual log SD to reproduce the sex-specific first two HDL moments. BMI exponential moments are analytic; age uses 128-point Gauss-Hermite quadrature. This is a working moment closure. The true source joint-distribution operator is unavailable, and the invented closure must not be interpreted as source microdata or exact empirical validation.
- **Shape context:** The UK/Malawi comparison (E_UK_SHAPE, PMC7364601) supports inverse BMI-HDL association and reports predominantly linear adiposity associations, with population and sex differences. Numerical supplemental tables are in an unsupported PDF. I used neither inaccessible numbers nor a main-text sentence that inconsistently labels a positive LDL-context result as HDL.

The selected log-linear mean is smooth, positive and has a constant relative BMI effect. Its absolute slope gradually attenuates. No discontinuities, tuned p-values or generated-sample correlations are imposed. The source category means do not establish this curvature or its extrapolation to extreme BMI. Source SDs are not copied directly into residual SD, and no reported SE or regression p-value is treated as individual variability.

## Consequential challenge and actions

The consequential initial location gap prompted retrieval of a contemporaneous U.S. primary source instead of direct transfer of Taiwan absolute means. Conditional calibration still requires assumptions about source predictor distributions, omitted determinants and transport. MIDUS includes diabetes and medication use, but its volunteer biomarker selection, older ages, two-race composition, Milwaukee oversampling and fasting protocol differ from the target. No race or diabetes labels are invented for target rows. Source measurement differences are left uncorrected because no calibration offset is available.

The focused countermodel is a gamma distribution with the same conditional mean and variance. Actual calculations show that it changes threshold probabilities despite preserving the available moment constraints. For an invented 50-year-old man at BMI 35, P(HDL > 100) is 0.00229 under lognormal and 0.000891 under gamma. Source quantiles or threshold frequencies conditional on predictors would discriminate the families; marginal log transformation alone cannot do so. The lognormal is retained because of positivity, skew and source distributional context, while tail claims remain weak. This two-model challenge is not a calibrated uncertainty interval.

## Checks, use and stopping

Actual tests confirmed exact predictor/index/order preservation, no input or global NumPy RNG mutation, same-seed reproducibility, changed-seed variation, empty-input behavior, and positive finite outputs on invented inputs. Four invented sex/BMI profiles with 100,000 draws each passed conditional-mean and variance Monte Carlo checks. A BMI 10-100 and age 20-79 grid remained positive. All evidence artifact hashes and parameter reference IDs passed checks. Source-moment agreement holds by construction under the declared closure; 64-versus-128-node multiplier differences were at most 2.84e-5 relative. The unused Taiwan categories do not exactly match representative-point predictions, which limits shape support. Full numerical results are in `artificial_checks.json`.

Only observed predictor distributions were inspected: BMI ranged 14.1-77.5 and age 20-79. No target HDL, target outcome summary, source individual dataset or target generated cohort was accessed or evaluated.

The candidate supports conditional association scenarios for BMI, age and sex. Absolute subgroup levels, threshold prevalence, nonlinear patterns, young/old age comparisons and interactions remain transport- or structure-dependent. It does not support causal effects, treatment decisions, individual diagnosis, survey-weighted national estimates or precision claims obtained by increasing synthetic sample size. Repeated draws sample individual residual variability, not scientific model uncertainty.

Initial retrieval used 6 of 12 queries and 3 of 12 downloads. Broad age searches returned mostly less-applicable endpoints; the concrete UK supplemental lead was unsupported. The key location, variation and dependence constraints are now explicit, so initial searching stops with 6 queries and 9 downloads reserved for review-directed correction. The highest-priority remaining attainable correction is a better age-by-sex constraint or a repair to the source-distribution bridge. `gaps.json` and `actions.json` record their effects and next actions. No `final/` directory has been created.
